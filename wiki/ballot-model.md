# Ballot Model

## Ballot components

A **Ballot** is the Judge's adjudication record for one assigned round. Docket treats its two components separately:

- The **Competitive Result** contains the winner and any scoring values used by standings, pairing, or advancement.
- **Ballot Feedback** contains the Judge's narrative reasoning and comments for the competitors.

## Submission and editing

A Judge may draft both components during an assigned round. **Ballot Submission** validates and locks the Competitive Result so the tournament can tabulate the round and continue operating. Ballot Feedback is not required for Ballot Submission and remains independently editable afterward without staff reopening.

- After Ballot Submission, the Judge receives a receipt and retains read-only access to the submitted Competitive Result.
- The authoring Judge retains read-only access to their own submitted open-ended rubric answers but never receives another Judge's answers.
- Changing a submitted Competitive Result requires an authorized **Ballot Reopening** with a reason and audit record.
- Adding or revising Ballot Feedback does not require Ballot Reopening.
- Feedback revisions retain version history and actor attribution.
- A Judge never receives access to another Judge's Ballot or unpublished standings through a Judge assignment.

Every official tournament gives Judges until the same local clock time seven calendar days after the current published end of the final scheduled competitive round to add, revise, and explicitly publish Ballot Feedback without staff reopening. Docket calculates the **Feedback Deadline** in the tournament's official named timezone and displays that instant in both the tournament and each user's local timezone. The deadline is a hard platform standard: no actor may extend, shorten, or reopen it. Docket freezes its anchor when the final scheduled competitive round ends; a later clerical Schedule correction cannot move it. The invitation shows the resulting deadline. At that instant, every Feedback Draft and Published Feedback version becomes immutable to the Judge and officials.

The hard seven-day duration cannot be overridden by a Tournament Director, Tournament Owner, Tabulation Staff member, Platform Administrator, uploaded configuration, Emergency Amendment, Post-Closure Exception, or Ballot-specific action. Official-tournament Feedback has no reopening path after the deadline.

Tournament Closure does not shorten a Judge's still-open seven-day Feedback window. After the Feedback Deadline, no Judge or official may change official-tournament Feedback, including through a Post-Closure Exception.

## Feedback publication

A **Feedback Draft** is visible only to its Judge. The Judge must explicitly perform **Feedback Publication** to make that revision visible to the adjudicated Competitors, every current Coaching Staff member for each Competitor's School, and that School's sole School Manager. This supersedes the narrower responsible- or assigned-Coach rule. Ballot Feedback is never public in the first slice.

After Feedback Publication:

- Recipients see only the current **Published Feedback** version.
- A later Judge edit creates a new private Feedback Draft; recipients continue seeing the prior Published Feedback.
- Recipients see the revision only after the Judge explicitly publishes it.
- At the Feedback Deadline, an unpublished draft remains private and is flagged incomplete; Docket does not publish it automatically.
- A published version remains available after the deadline even if a later private draft was left incomplete.
- Authorized safety personnel may hide or restrict harmful Published Feedback after competitive and Feedback finality without editing it or reopening the Ballot. Docket preserves the exact restricted version as evidence.
- A recipient-access correction may revoke mistaken access or grant omitted access after the deadline without changing Feedback content. Docket privately notifies affected people and preserves the complete delivery and access history.
- A released-point correction updates the Competitor, every current Coaching Staff member for the Competitor's School, and the School Manager together; Docket notifies them, preserves the prior restricted values, and makes standings consume only the current corrected values.

## Practice Ballots and feedback

A Practice Workspace uses a **Practice Ballot** governed by the event's one platform-wide, versioned **Event Ballot Rubric**, shared across official and practice rounds. Rules-governance Platform Administrators collaboratively edit a document-like Event Ballot Rubric Draft by adding and arranging open-ended question fields. Docket automatically merges nonconflicting simultaneous edits, attributes every edit, and requires an administrator to resolve a same-field conflict explicitly rather than applying silent last-write-wins behavior. Removing or deprecating a previously published field requires a new version and an impact warning; every historical rubric version and Ballot retains its exact original fields. Each new open-ended field defaults to optional but may be marked required, and submission blocks only for an unanswered required field. Answers support autosaved paragraphs, bold, italics, headings, numbered lists, bullet lists, and sanitized `https` hyperlinks within a 5,000-character per-field limit. Docket displays the destination domain, opens links in a safe new browser context, warns that the user is leaving Docket, and rejects shortened or tracking URLs, non-HTTPS protocols, previews, images, tables, attachments, embedded media, custom HTML, scripts, and automatically loaded external content. A draft has no operative effect until validation, examples, diff, reason, audit, and publication review complete. Anyone who changed questions, order, required status, instructions, validation, or answer limits is a substantive editor and cannot be the ordinary second approver; title-only administration is not substantive. Publication normally requires a second distinct rules-governance Platform Administrator. If only one publisher is available, a Tournament Director may edit and publish through the Single-Publisher Rubric Exception after acknowledging a global-effect and self-publication warning, explaining why no other publisher was available, reviewing the complete impact preview, and accepting permanent attribution. The resulting version is Docket-wide and immediately becomes the default for new unpinned uses; the exception cannot create a tournament-specific rubric. Restoring older content always copies it into a new draft, gives it a new version number, and repeats the reason, impact, and publication controls rather than erasing or reactivating history. No School, Organizer, or Practice Session Owner may replace or customize the Event Ballot Rubric per session. New draft practices use the latest version; the Practice Role Lock pins it permanently. An official tournament pins its compatible version with the Event Ruleset before first-round Pairing Publication. Accepted Judges and Competitors preview the version before start. Every Practice Ballot field and submission is visibly nonoperative. A draft may be incomplete, while submission requires only fields the Event Ballot Rubric marks mandatory. Mock winners and scores are optional unless that rubric requires them and remain nonoperative.

Before the Single-Publisher Rubric Exception becomes available, Docket freezes the set of every currently qualified publisher and sends each an authenticated in-app request containing the exact version diff. The ordinary four-hour clock starts only after every required in-app delivery is recorded; email delivery is supplemental, and its failure raises a warning without delaying the clock. Only an explicit approval of that exact diff by a publisher who still holds authority counts as acceptance. Opening, acknowledging, or rejecting the request does not count, and even unanimous rejection does not shorten the four-hour period. A newly qualified publisher receives the live request but does not restart the clock; every authority change is audited. General publication and governing-version notices are informational: missing Director or Practice Session Owner acknowledgment never blocks Practice Role Lock, round start, or session start.

The first authorized Ready-or-Scheduled-to-Started transition for any pairing in a scheduled event-round cohort creates one **Round Rubric Pin** for every room and pairing in that cohort; the transition records actor, time, and exact version. Later-starting rooms use the same version, and a corrected rubric begins no earlier than the next event round. If no pairing has started, a validly published correction may govern the entire cohort and becomes its pin when the first pairing starts. Scheduled time alone and merely opening a Ballot do not pin it. Once pinned, no Platform Administrator, Tournament Director, emergency process, default update, correction, or migration may edit or replace the rubric governing that cohort, including an unsubmitted Ballot. When a defect affects only one pairing, unaffected pairings continue under the cohort pin and only the blocked pairing enters administrative resolution. When a defect prevents valid adjudication more broadly, **Emergency Rubric Publication** requests every qualified publisher, waits fifteen minutes, and then permits a warned, reasoned, permanently attributed Director publication for future event rounds. Failed notices do not block those rounds: Docket retries and escalates delivery and prominently shows the exact governing version on entry.

If the pinned rubric cannot support a valid Judge decision, the Judge reports a **Rubric-Blocked Decision** and Docket pauses decision submission for that pairing without changing the pin. The **Rubric-Blocked Ruling Evidence** contains the pinned version, affected fields, Judge report, validation failure, explanation of why no valid decision is possible, governing Ruleset and Pairing, proposed outcome, and downstream impact preview while excluding unrelated private evidence. The Tournament Director prepares the Administrative Ruling, and a distinct Ballot-correction-authorized Tabulation Staff member verifies it before publication. A Director-only ruling is allowed only when Docket records that no distinct authorized verifier is reasonably available before the result is operationally required; ordinary delay or convenience is insufficient, and the exception requires a prominent warning, availability check, explanation, evidence review, and permanent attribution. The Director must publish the ruling or escalate the unresolved case within thirty minutes of the report and before the next affected event round starts, whichever occurs first. Missing that deadline never creates an automatic outcome. At expiry, Docket sends high-priority in-app and email escalation to every Tournament Director, the Tournament Owner, and Ballot-correction-authorized Tabulation Staff and continues until the case is published or formally assigned. Affected Competitors, their Coaches, and the assigned Judge see only **Administrative Review in Progress**. While unresolved, Docket blocks only dependent standings, Pairings, advancement, and publication; unrelated rounds and events continue. The outcome is labeled `Administrative Ruling — Rubric Defect`, never a Judge Ballot. Affected participants see the general reason and outcome; it appears publicly only wherever the round decision would ordinarily be public and never exposes evidence or staff reasoning. Docket creates no late-Ballot penalty, Assignment Hold, No-Show, reliability record, or qualification effect for the Judge. It tracks a Judge submission only after staff determine that a valid Judge decision remains possible.

An affected Competitor, that Competitor's current represented-School Coach or School Manager, the assigned Judge, or authorized tournament staff may file one **Rubric-Blocked Correction Request** within sixty minutes after ruling notice or before the next affected event round begins, whichever occurs first. The request may seek only an objective correction for incorrect inputs, a misapplied Ruleset, wrong participants, or proven system error; disagreement with the merits creates no appeal, and the public or an unrelated School has no filing authority. After that participant window closes, only a Tournament Director or Ballot-correction-authorized Tabulation Staff member may open an objective correction case. Docket may flag suspected system defects for their review but never corrects a ruling automatically. The original ruling remains preserved. An approved correction publishes a new version, recalculates standings and advancement, issues any required public Correction Notice, and surfaces Downstream Conflicts without rewriting a round that has started. Docket privately notifies affected Competitors, the current Coaching Staff and School Manager for every represented School, the assigned Judge, all Tournament Directors, and involved Tabulation Staff through audience-filtered notices. The authoritative ruling contributes the competitive outcome to standings but creates no Judge-submitted Speaker Points; only clearly labeled Ruleset-defined Administrative Points may apply.

Formal assignment of an overdue Rubric-Blocked case occurs only when one Tournament Director accepts responsibility in Docket. The assignment records actor and time, may transfer to another Director through an audited action, and allows Tabulation Staff to assist without becoming ruling authority. Ballot-correction-authorized Tabulation Staff may open, investigate, and prepare a post-window objective correction, but only a Tournament Director may approve or reject it. If Docket flags a suspected system defect that could change the competitive outcome, only dependent standings, Pairings, advancement, and publication remain blocked until authorized staff dismiss the flag or open a correction; lower-impact flags are nonblocking warnings.

Failure to deliver a corrected-ruling notice never invalidates or reverses the correction. Docket retries in-app and email delivery, preserves the notice in the recipient's Docket inbox, and escalates repeated failures to tournament staff.

Starting without acknowledgment pins the displayed version, records an **Unacknowledged Rubric Notice**, and sends a reminder without creating an operational or competitive penalty. The active warning clears after acknowledgment, while a content-minimized event remains restricted operational telemetry for two years after acknowledgment or tournament or practice closure and never becomes public or Competitive Evidence. Only the responsible Director or Practice Session Owner and rules-governance administrators see the warning. Judges and Competitors see the exact governing rubric, not the internal warning. A restricted Feedback or point download is an **Attributed Restricted Export** whose PDF footer or JSON metadata identifies recipient Account, represented School, generation time, source version, and restricted-use status; it contains no tracking pixel, remote beacon, or remotely loaded content.

A separate **Practice Feedback Rubric** governs written comments and falls back to Docket's versioned event default. When an authorized platform administrator implements a new event-default version, Docket automatically replaces the prior default in every pre-lock Practice Workspace still following that default. It does not replace a selected custom titled version, alter an Event Ballot Rubric, or change a session at or after Practice Role Lock. Only the Practice Session Owner may select or edit a custom version before the lock; Docket accepts no change suggestions from Organizers, Judges, Competitors, or other participants. The owner may title and save versions for School reuse or explicitly share a sanitized titled version across Schools containing only prompts, sections, and instructions. The exact version at Practice Role Lock remains fixed through Practice Feedback Reopening.

That automatic default update sends the owner a version diff and sends accepted Judges and Competitors a governing-version notice. Owner acknowledgment is recorded when supplied but never blocks Practice Role Lock or session start. Docket preserves all draft content, maps stable fields, and places content from removed fields into a private Feedback Migration Review. Removed-field content reaches recipients only if its Judge intentionally maps it into a valid current field and republishes. A new mandatory field does not invalidate a prior Published Feedback version, but an existing draft must complete it before the next publication. Docket never silently deletes, publishes, or relocates draft content.

Submitted open-ended Event Ballot Rubric answers are restricted to their authoring Judge in read-only form, the Competitors actually adjudicated by that Ballot, and authorized officials. In an official tournament, authorized officials are the Tournament Director and only Tabulation Staff holding explicit Ballot-view or correction permission; generic staff, room monitors, and Platform Administrator status alone grant nothing. In a Practice Workspace, the Practice Session Owner and an explicitly designated Organizer holding Ballot-oversight permission are the equivalent officials; other Organizers and Observers are excluded. A Coach authorized for a Competitor receives all Published Feedback delivered to that Competitor plus that Competitor's released scoring points, but not the locked open-ended Event Ballot Rubric answers. For a team or group Entry, the answer-recipient set includes every active Competitor who actually participated on either adjudicated side and excludes alternates, withdrawn members, unrelated School members, and rostered nonparticipants. Answers are never public or available to another panel Judge. Docket blocks the Judge's leave or complete-round action until submission; Feedback may follow later, and browser abandonment does not submit. For a multi-Judge round, Docket withholds every Judge's Ballot from the other Judges permanently while still using every submitted decision to apply the event panel rule, normally strict majority, and produce the official session-scoped **Practice Panel Result**; a failed majority becomes `Tied Practice Panel`. Assigned Competitors may see the submissions adjudicating them. That result is never a Competitive Result and cannot affect official tournament data, qualification, rankings, profiles, or permanent history.

For Feedback and released-point access, “a Coach authorized for a Competitor” means every Account whose active School Membership currently carries a Coaching Staff role for the School represented by that Entry; access ends immediately when that role or Membership ends. Docket rechecks authorization on every request and active session, closes an open protected view, and blocks further export as soon as authority ends, while acknowledging that an earlier downloaded file cannot be erased. The represented School's sole School Manager receives exactly the same Feedback and point scope through the Manager role. A newly authorized Coach or School Manager immediately receives still-retained records for Entries representing that School. None receives locked open-ended answers. They receive the exact Published Feedback version delivered to their Competitor even if it discusses an opponent, but no separately addressed opponent Feedback or opponent-restricted score detail.

After a Competitor transfers, the Competitor retains their personal Feedback history. Each **Represented-School Tournament Record** remains with the School represented by the original Entry and contains its Entries, rosters as operated, Pairings, School-directed notices, authorized Ballots and Feedback, released points, results, and corrections. It excludes opponent-private information, Judge assessment data, safety evidence, staff investigations, and unrelated Competitor history. The represented School's current Coaching Staff and School Manager retain access; the new School's staff receive only records for Entries representing the new School. The Competitor cannot revoke this historical School authorization, although privacy and safety restrictions may limit a particular record.

A newly authorized Coach or School Manager receives one access-granted notice describing historical scope and privacy obligations, plus a **Historical Access Dashboard** listing the available tournaments; Docket does not send one notice per record. The dashboard filters by tournament, season, event, Competitor, record type, and represented School but provides no unrestricted full-text search across private Feedback. Ordinary removal of a role creates no security incident for exports legitimately downloaded while authorized. Docket retains the normal export audit, blocks future access, and opens an incident only if the original access was mistaken or later misuse is reported.

The **Represented-School Data Export** is created directly inside Docket rather than requested as a prepared packet. The School Manager holds **School Data Export Permission** by default and may grant or revoke it for selected current Coaching Staff; general Coaching Staff membership alone grants no export authority. Its **School Export Selection** uses a UI checklist containing only Entries, rosters as operated, Pairings, School-directed notices, authorized Ballots and Feedback, released points, results, and Correction Notices. Filters may narrow by tournament, season, event, Competitor, record type, date range, and CSV or PDF format, but every selection remains constrained to records representing that School. Arbitrary database fields and private-evidence categories never appear.

School Data Export Permission is one indivisible capability and never expands the recipient's underlying record access. Only the School Manager may issue a **School Export Grant**, and fresh Google reauthentication, a consequences preview, attribution, and recipient notice are required. The recipient must accept in-app under current Google authentication within seven days. Decline or expiry closes the offer without activating authority, notifies the Manager, and requires a new grant for later access. A Coach may file one explanatory **School Export Permission Request**. Before decision, the requester may revise its purpose or withdraw it; Docket retains its versions and notifies the Manager of material revisions. Request content, revisions, withdrawals, and decisions are visible only to the requester, current Manager, and governed privacy or audit personnel. An unresolved request receives a three-day reminder and expires after seven days without authority or Platform Administrator escalation. A denial requires a short reason visible to the requester, while an approval note is optional. After denial or voluntary withdrawal, the requester may refile immediately only for a materially different purpose or circumstance and otherwise waits thirty days; there is no separate appeal. The current Manager decides whether a filing is materially different after reviewing both requests side by side and records a brief bypass explanation. Three or more bypasses within 90 days warn the Manager before another bypass and create a governed audit-review flag. Review is limited to request metadata and Manager explanations and cannot automatically block authority or expose export contents; any broader action requires a separately authorized investigation. Losing the applicable Coaching Staff role or School Membership closes a pending request as ineligible, notifies requester and Manager, preserves payload-free history, and never transfers the request. For the same two-year history period, the former requester may see only their own submitted explanation, final status, and Manager decision reason—not current School activity, others' requests, or export data. A Manager transition sends an unresolved request to the successor with notice to both successor and requester while preserving the original expiration. The successor can review the School's retained two-year history of requests, revisions, withdrawals, denials, reasons, and bypasses without gaining export payload access. The grant ends immediately when the Manager revokes it or the recipient's Coaching Staff role or School Membership ends. A Manager transition preserves existing grants but creates an immediate, persistent seven-day review for the new Manager to confirm or revoke each one. The review deadline cannot be extended. At the deadline, every unreviewed inherited grant becomes a **Suspended School Export Grant** without deletion; suspension cancels its queued and running jobs, disables its personal and shared live downloads, and immediately notifies the affected Coach and current Manager that successor review expired without disclosing export contents. Email failure never changes suspension: the notice remains in the Coach's restricted Docket inbox, retries three times over 24 hours, and then alerts the Manager. A successor may bulk-revoke inherited grants, but every continued grant requires individual review and confirmation. If the Manager changes again, the newest Manager receives the unresolved review while the grant remains suspended and no new active window begins. Later confirmation requires the current Manager's fresh Google reauthentication, reuses the Coach's original acceptance only if their Coaching Staff role and School Membership remain valid, and notifies Coach and Manager; otherwise the Manager revokes or issues a new offer. Restoration permits the Coach to access still-available earlier exports only under current record authorization, fresh Google reauthentication, and any applicable outdated warning; it never revives an expired artifact, restarts canceled generation jobs, or reactivates disabled shares. New work requires a new generation action, and the creator or Manager must recheck recipients before creating a new share.

Permission revocation cancels that actor's pending generation, disables their live server-side downloads and **School Export Shares**, preserves payload-free audit history, and warns that external copies cannot be recalled. The School Manager may still download an already completed School export during its remaining seven-day window after its Coaching Staff creator loses authority. A permissioned Coaching Staff member sees only their own two-year School Export History; the School Manager retains the all-School history. The creator or Manager may share a generated export only with another current staff member who already holds School Data Export Permission. Each recipient reauthenticates independently for every download. A School Export Share ends with export expiry, explicit Manager or creator revocation, or recipient permission loss, whichever occurs first; access removal does not erase the sharing audit.

CSV provides structured tabular facts and PDF provides human-readable records; both represent the same authorized facts through format-appropriate layouts. A single output at or below two gigabytes downloads directly. Multiple outputs or one partitioned output above two gigabytes use ZIP, and every human-readable file inside remains CSV or PDF. A single PDF appends its signed integrity page. CSV output is packaged with a signed PDF manifest inside a ZIP. The Docket-signed **School Export Manifest** records the selection, formats, file checksums, record counts, and any ZIP-part sequence; public verification keys and rotation history remain indefinitely so downloaded exports remain verifiable after server deletion.

Generation and every new or resumed file or ZIP-part download require fresh Google reauthentication without ending the ordinary Docket session. Docket never emails the output or creates a separate password. The authenticated pre-download summary shows date range, included and excluded categories, file count, total size, and retention warnings. Each output is limited to the School's already-authorized records and excludes separately addressed opponent Feedback, opponent-restricted points, private eligibility, conflicts, strikes, and staff evidence. Generated filenames contain `Docket`, School display name, covered tournament or date range, record category, and generation date; a Competitor name appears only for an intentionally single-Competitor export.

Only one generation job may run per School; additional selections queue without creating a daily completion cap. Generation pins one consistent source-version snapshot. A source change before completion never mixes versions: Docket finishes from the pin and marks the output outdated. If Docket cannot read the complete pinned snapshot consistently, the whole job fails and retries up to three times. Each retry revalidates authorization and retention, pins the newest complete snapshot available when that attempt starts, and discards data from earlier attempts; the manifest identifies the exact snapshot used by the successful attempt. If required selected records were deleted under their retention rules, generation fails with the affected categories, approximate counts, and governing retention reason while withholding deleted contents, opponent information, and private evidence. It never uses stale data or emits mixed-version or partial output. Exhausting those retries fails the job and notifies the requester. System-caused consistency failures never count toward the abuse throttle. Five user-cancelled or non-system failed generation jobs within sixty minutes pause new generation for one hour, while system-caused failures do not count. The throttle blocks only new generation and preserves the Historical Access Dashboard and already-completed downloads. A failed Google reauthentication denies only that download attempt, leaves the ordinary Docket session and generated export intact, and permits retry without automatic cancellation or regeneration.

When the School, selection, filters, format, and underlying source versions exactly match an export already drafted and downloaded, Docket tells the user that the export was already drafted and downloaded rather than treating it as a duplicate packet request. Any source change marks the earlier output outdated. Every CSV contract has a schema version with stable column identifiers; removing or renaming a column requires a new version. Docket neutralizes formula-triggering leading characters in user-originated CSV cells while preserving displayed text. The generated output otherwise remains available for seven days after readiness. Cancellation deletes every remaining server-side file, preserves payload-free history, warns that downloaded copies cannot be recalled, and never changes source retention.

A generated export is immutable. When an authorized source correction changes one of its inputs, Docket marks it outdated, identifies only the changed record categories, and requires an explicit new generation action. The outdated export remains downloadable through its original seven-day window. Every new or resumed download session displays a prominent warning, requires fresh Google reauthentication and one explicit confirmation covering the specifically listed files or ZIP parts in that session, and records the warning, actor, and time in School Export History. A later session repeats those controls. Each successfully delivered part is recorded; an interrupted session is marked incomplete and resumes only its missing parts under the new session controls. A checksum failure invalidates only the affected part, prevents Docket from representing the export as complete, and permits authenticated redownload while available. After two failed redownloads of the same part, Docket stops serving it, marks the artifact unavailable, notifies requester and Manager, and opens a system-integrity review. A continuously connected transfer authorized before expiration may finish, but a disconnect after expiration cannot resume; expiration blocks every new request and never extends server-side storage. The replacement shortcut copies the prior selection and filters into a new review screen; the actor must confirm before Docket starts a separate generation job. Canceling that replacement affects only the new job and leaves the outdated export unchanged for its remaining original window. Starting, queuing, or failing a replacement never extends the old export's availability. A compromised-key replacement uses the current authorized snapshot, links to the prior artifact, and identifies changed categories instead of promising identical bytes. It receives priority over ordinary queued exports while preserving one active job per School. Its predownload comparison shows only changed categories and counts; individual records remain inside the newly authorized export. The signed export and manifest never change. During that window, Docket sends the privacy-minimized outdated notice only to still-authorized people who actually downloaded the export, including School Export Share recipients; access without download creates no notice.

Permissioned staff may save private named **School Export Presets**. Role loss disables the creator's School-specific private presets without transferring them. Only the School Manager may publish, revise, or retire a School-wide preset. Revision creates a new attributed version, makes it current, and retains prior preset versions for two years in Manager-visible history; configuration-only School presets survive staff and Manager transitions. If an export-schema change invalidates a saved mapping, Docket marks the preset **School Export Preset Needs Review**, blocks generation, and shows the schema difference rather than silently remapping it. The private preset owner may repair a private preset; only the School Manager may repair and republish a School-wide preset. For every removed field, the editor must explicitly remove it or select a documented replacement. Docket may recommend a mapping but cannot apply one automatically. Every repaired version records its predecessor, editor, time, removed fields, chosen replacements, rejected Docket suggestions, and resulting schema version without storing exported data. CSV rows include stable Docket identifiers beside authorized display labels, use ISO 8601 timestamps, and state the tournament timezone for tournament-local dates or deadlines. A transferred Competitor's historical row keeps the historical Entry ID and represented School rather than substituting current affiliation. The first release has no email or contact-information export category and excludes personal email addresses, Google identifiers, authentication data, and unrelated contact details.

Every output is visibly attributed without tracking technology. PDF headers or footers and CSV metadata identify the recipient, represented School, generation time, source version, and restricted-use status. PDFs use searchable text, clear headings, repeating table headers, page numbers, source-version information, and accessible document structure rather than image-only records. A former staff member may still validate a lawfully downloaded file cryptographically with Docket's public keys without regaining School access. Docket's public verification screen requires no Account, checks a signed manifest or checksum without retaining the submitted file, manifest, checksum, or identifying metadata after the check, and applies privacy-preserving rate limits. It reveals only whether the signature is valid and which Docket public key signed it. An invalid result provides general troubleshooting guidance but never exposes expected hashes or internal diagnostics. A cryptographically valid signature from a later-revoked or compromised key states that key status, its revocation date, and that authenticity requires review. A safely retired, uncompromised key remains valid and trusted and shows its retirement date without a compromise warning. Docket's signed, versioned **Docket Verification Key Directory** publishes downloadable snapshots containing active and retired verification keys, rotation history, revocation status, and effective dates without School or recipient information. Offline tools verify against a snapshot and check for newer revocations when connectivity returns. A distinct tightly controlled root key signs the directory; ordinary root rotation requires two Platform Administrators and publishes the transition. If the root is compromised, two unaffected Platform Administrators revoke it, establish a replacement, publish the transition through independent official Docket channels, and maintain an urgent warning until clients trust the replacement root. When an operational key is compromised, Docket notifies current Managers and actual downloaders of affected unexpired exports and offers authorized replacement generation without silently altering or re-signing an existing artifact. A former downloader receives a minimized security notice identifying only the affected artifact and signing key without current School information or restored access. Without current School authority verification reveals no School, recipient, record categories, history, or outdated status; querying live outdated status additionally requires current authorization for that School.

Governed reviewers close a repeated-bypass audit flag as `Expected Use`, `Policy Concern`, or `Investigation Required`, notify the Manager, and retain the classification for two years. The review sees request metadata and Manager explanations only, does not block authority or reveal export content by itself, and notifies a requester only if their own permission changes.

School authority revocation immediately terminates an active transfer even when it began before expiry. Repeated checksum failure never permits staff to repair a signed artifact in place: review preserves its history, confirms invalidation and offers a new authorized generation, or escalates evidence of wider compromise. A compromise replacement waits for the active School generation to finish, then moves ahead of ordinary queued work without violating the one-active-job limit. Its predownload comparison shows changed categories and counts only; if retention prevents an exact count, Docket labels that category unavailable because of retention and does not estimate.

Docket provides a public web verifier and downloadable offline utility with identical validation rules and results. The offline utility displays directory publication time and last online revocation check, warns after 24 hours without an update check while still reporting the local cryptographic result, and later checks for new revocations. Published trust chains keep pre-rotation exports verifiable and report the historical root without treating ordinary rotation as compromise. Root compromise uses a predesignated offline recovery quorum with independently controlled credentials and never a single administrator. The quorum revokes and replaces the root, publishes the transition through independent official Docket channels, and maintains an urgent warning until clients trust the replacement. Failed operational-key compromise notices remain in the Docket inbox when available, retry email three times over 24 hours, record delivery status, and leave the public key directory as the authoritative warning.

When a bypass review closes as `Policy Concern`, Docket shows the Manager a persistent acknowledgment-required warning without suspending authority. `Investigation Required` creates only a restricted referral and needs approval by a second governed audit or privacy actor before a full investigation opens.

Authority revocation during delivery stops all remaining transfer, records which parts were already delivered, and warns the Manager that external copies cannot be recalled. A **School Export Integrity Review** begins after the repeated checksum threshold and must receive an initial classification within 24 hours from a designated-security Platform Administrator who was independent of generation or manual intervention. School and tournament staff may provide evidence but cannot decide. Overdue reviews persistently escalate to Platform security and never restore or replace an artifact automatically. Invalidation notifies requester, current Manager, and actual downloaders; former downloaders receive only artifact identity, invalidation status, and safe next steps.

Official verifier releases share a versioned validation specification and signed test-vector set and must pass the same vectors before publication. Offline utility releases and update manifests are signed through the Docket trust chain, and unsigned or incorrectly signed updates are rejected. If revocation refresh fails, offline output reports the local result, directory age, and `Revocation Status May Be Stale` and withholds a current-trust claim. Multipart output becomes fully valid only after every listed part passes checksum and the signed manifest validates the complete ordered set; missing, duplicated, reordered, or corrupted parts return `Incomplete` or `Invalid`.

The current School Manager may view two years of all-School **School Export History**, including grant, generation, completion, download, sharing, cancellation, failure, expiration, actor, and selection metadata. A permissioned Coaching Staff member sees only their own two-year activity; ordinary Coaching Staff receive none, and privacy and audit personnel retain their governed projections. A Manager transition preserves accepted staff grants subject to the mandatory seven-day review, while canceling the former Manager's personal pending jobs and disabling their personal live downloads. Source records remain unchanged.

When safety personnel hide or restrict harmful Published Feedback, Docket privately notifies the authoring Judge, affected Competitors, every current Coaching Staff member for the represented Schools, the School Managers, and authorized tournament or practice officials. The notice contains only a general safety category and current status, never the report or evidence. The authoring Judge or an affected Competitor may submit one **Feedback Safety Reconsideration** within fourteen days; different Platform Safety personnel decide it while the content remains restricted, and the review cannot change a Competitive Result. Docket sends the outcome to the requester, authoring Judge, affected Competitors, represented-School Coaching Staff and Managers, and authorized officials using only `Restored`, `Remains Restricted`, or `Restricted with Revised Scope`; evidence and reviewer reasoning remain private. `Restored` returns that exact version to ordinary authorized views and future exports and sends the standardized notice without automatically delivering or regenerating a download. Safety-restricted Feedback is otherwise excluded from ordinary Account and School exports and remains available only to authorized safety, legal, and audit actors. If mistaken access included a download, Docket revokes future access, preserves an access-and-export incident, alerts privacy and safety personnel and affected parties, and records that an external copy cannot be recalled.

Neither a School nor Competitor may delete a Represented-School Tournament Record before its retention period ends. Governed correction, access restriction, safety review, and legally required privacy actions remain available, but none silently erases Competitive Evidence or represented-School attribution.

If every publisher in a frozen review set loses authority, Docket invalidates all of their approval capability, preserves the request history, and lets the Director fallback become available when the existing four-hour or fifteen-minute clock expires; the clock never restarts.

Only the Practice Session Owner may authorize a reasoned reopening or correction before closure. Reopening withdraws the current Practice Panel Result, preserves all versions, and waits for every required Ballot to be resubmitted before recalculation. Before closure, the owner may directly make a versioned **Practice Placement Correction** limited to competitor-placement fields on an already-submitted final Practice Ballot. The owner must select a correction category and provide a short explanation. Docket preserves the original Ballot and result as superseded, immediately recalculates the Practice Panel Result, and notifies all assigned Competitors and Judges without exposing private Feedback. The owner cannot change Judge-authored open-ended responses, non-placement scores, Feedback, or attribution or supply a missing Ballot. A correction therefore cannot cure `Incomplete Practice Panel`; only every required Judge's legitimate pre-closure submission can do so. A missing required Ballot at closure produces `Incomplete Practice Panel`, preserves authorized individual submissions, and creates no result after a prominent owner warning. If a Judge becomes unavailable, a preaccepted same-role Practice Standby may independently submit and permanently replace that Judge; without one, the panel remains incomplete.

Closing a browser does not submit a Ballot. Docket preserves its private draft, records it outstanding, notifies the Judge and owner, and creates no reliability or qualification effect. Where supported, an encrypted local draft survives connection loss and retries after reconnection; only a server receipt establishes submission. The owner can wait, activate an eligible standby, or close the incomplete panel but cannot submit for the Judge.

**Practice Feedback** follows the tournament Feedback Draft and Feedback Publication pattern without a Competitive Result dependency. It is optional at Ballot submission, privately drafted, explicitly published, keeps the prior publication visible during later private edits, and never auto-publishes. The deadline defaults to seventy-two hours after the practice round, may be extended but not shortened by the owner, and thereafter requires reasoned owner **Practice Feedback Reopening**, always before the thirty-day deletion ceiling. Default recipients are assigned Competitors and the owner or authorized Organizer. The Judge and owner may inspect complete version history; intended Competitors receive current feedback and change notices but no withdrawn draft. Removal freezes the participant's received Ballot and Feedback set and blocks later publication to them. A reported harmful version is preserved separately and may be hidden, restored, removed, or restricted only through the Practice Safety Report process. Practice Feedback is never public.

The completed Practice Panel Result is visible only to assigned Competitors, all assigned Judges after submission, and the owner or authorized Organizer. Authorized downloads provide a human-readable PDF and structured JSON containing the requester's permitted Practice Ballots, Practice Panel Result, and Practice Feedback plus calculation rule, completion time, current version information, event and session labels, and a prominent nonoperative notice. They exclude private drafts, other participants' material, emails, and safety records. No Practice Panel Result enters an Account, School, qualification, ranking, or public-profile history.

## Competitive Result fields

A normal Lincoln-Douglas preliminary-round Competitive Result requires:

- Exactly one winning Entry
- Speaker Points for both Competitors, within the range and precision defined by the adopted Ruleset

An Elimination Round Competitive Result requires exactly one winning Entry and contains no Speaker Points. Docket rejects Judge-submitted Speaker Points and Administrative Points for every elimination Ballot or outcome, whether the round has one Judge or a panel.

The Ballot receives both Entries and their affirmative and negative side assignments from the published pairing. The Judge confirms the displayed assignment but does not re-enter or redefine it.

Ballot Submission validates that the submitting Judge owns the assignment, the Ballot belongs to the published pairing, the winner is one of its two Entries, both Speaker Points are present and Ruleset-valid, and no conflicting submission already exists.

### Duplicate and conflicting submissions

Docket makes Ballot Submission idempotent. An authenticated retry of the same submission request, or an equivalent submission with the same validated Competitive Result, returns the original Ballot receipt and current locked version without creating another Ballot, result version, or audit event that implies a second decision.

After the Competitive Result locks, a later submission containing different competitive data is a **Conflicting Ballot Attempt**. Docket rejects it without changing the current result, preserves the attempted data, actor, assignment, request identifier, time, and comparison to the locked version in restricted audit history, and notifies the assigned Judge and Tournament Directors or Tabulation Staff with the Ballot and correction permission bundle.

A Judge who intended to correct or disputes the locked result must use the authorized Ballot Reopening workflow. The attempted submission cannot itself reopen, supersede, or overwrite the Competitive Result. Any accepted correction becomes a new Result Correction version under the existing evidence, attribution, impact-preview, and downstream-conflict rules.

For an Elimination Round, the same validation omits the preliminary Speaker Point requirement and instead rejects any point value.

Byes, forfeits, and other non-Judge resolutions are **Administrative Outcomes** entered through a separate, authorized, reasoned, audited staff workflow. Docket must not fabricate a Judge Ballot to represent them.

When an assigned Competitor is classified as a No-Show and no Ruleset-eligible same-School and same-event replacement is available, Tabulation Staff record a Bye Round for the opposing Entry under the locked Ruleset. The bye has no fabricated opponent, Judge Ballot, or Judge-submitted Speaker Points; any standings treatment comes only from the locked Standings Rules Configuration.

A Competitor may file the No-Show Dispute directly in Docket within 60 minutes or before the event's next round starts, whichever occurs first. Docket associates and notifies the responsible Coach, who may add a statement but cannot block, withdraw, or suppress it. Only the filing Competitor may withdraw before decision; explicit confirmation makes withdrawal final for that classification, is audited, and preserves the operative classification. The Director considers check-in, arrival, messaging, staff-contact, delivery-log, and incorrect published time or room evidence without requiring medical records and decides within 30 minutes or before the next round starts. The classification and any approved replacement or bye remain operative during review and an overdue decision escalates without automatic outcome. A rejection is final for the tournament except for an objective system or data-entry correction. Its private in-app and email notice is delivery-tracked but informational, remains in the Competitor Account, and neither requires acknowledgment nor reopens the dispute or delays operations after delivery failure. Before replacement sign-off, Tabulation Staff may restore a late-arriving original assignee; after replacement or bye approval, reversal requires a Director-approved correction, and a Started round never restores the original person. A successful dispute appends a reasoned reversal, removes the current restricted flag, corrects unstarted artifacts, and preserves the original classification only in restricted audit history; Started and Completed artifacts remain as operated under the locked Ruleset. Any public correction uses the generic reason “No-Show classification corrected” and withholds absence details, evidence, private reasoning, and restricted actor metadata.

An elimination bracket bye is generated from the locked Advancement Field and advances the seeded Entry without a Judge Ballot, Speaker Points, or fabricated Competitive Result. Its recorded provenance identifies the Advancement Plan and Field versions, seed, bracket line, and bye reason.

After any elimination round Starts, a withdrawal or disqualification cannot refill or reseed the bracket. Docket records the affected opponent's advancement as the applicable versioned bye, forfeit, or Disqualification Ruling without creating Speaker Points or a fabricated Judge decision. The original Advancement Field and bracket position remain auditable.

### Panel decisions

A multi-Judge panel must contain an odd number of assigned Judges. Every panel Judge submits an independent Competitive Result and separate Ballot Feedback. The **Panel Decision** awards the round to the Entry receiving a strict majority of those individual Judge decisions; Docket preserves the complete vote split and each Judge's attribution.

Docket locks the Panel Decision as soon as one Entry reaches an **Irreversible Majority** of the assigned panel: more than half of all assigned Judges, making the winner mathematically unchangeable by outstanding Ballots. Pairing, standings, and advancement may consume the locked winner immediately so the tournament can continue.

Remaining panel Competitive Results are still required and remain visibly outstanding with reminders and escalation. Their later submission cannot change an already irreversible winner but completes the panel record. Each Judge's feedback follows the independent feedback workflow and deadline.

In a preliminary panel, Speaker Point aggregation follows the adopted Ruleset and retains every individual Judge-submitted value and the aggregation inputs. In an Elimination Round, neither individual panel Ballots nor the Panel Decision contains Speaker Points. Reopening or correcting an individual panel decision uses the normal versioning rules and creates a Downstream Conflict if the corrected vote would change a previously consumed Panel Decision.

### Ballot due time and escalation

Every round has a **Ballot Due Time** equal to its scheduled end time plus the tournament's configured Ballot grace period. The grace period defaults to 15 minutes. Docket shows the due time on the Judge's assignment and retains the schedule version and grace-period value used to calculate it.

Docket applies the following escalation sequence to every outstanding Competitive Result:

1. At the scheduled round end, remind the assigned Judge that the Competitive Result remains due.
2. At the Ballot Due Time, mark the Ballot **Late** and notify the Judge again.
3. Ten minutes after the Ballot Due Time, notify Judge-and-room operations staff and all Tournament Directors so they can contact, locate, or replace the unavailable decision source.

At the third stage, Docket also places a tournament-local **Judge Assignment Hold** on the Judge. While the hold is active, the Judge is unavailable for new round assignments at that tournament. Submission of the missing Competitive Result releases the hold automatically; a Tournament Director may instead clear it explicitly with a recorded reason, actor, and time. A Director may remove the Judge's future assignments through the applicable pairing or schedule-revision workflow and notices.

An Irreversible Majority allows a Panel Decision to advance while each missing individual Ballot remains Late and outstanding. A single-Judge round, or a panel without an Irreversible Majority, blocks result-dependent standings, advancement, and pairing until the Competitive Result arrives or a Tournament Director issues an evidence-backed Administrative Ruling. The ruling records its evidence, reason, Director, time, and resulting competitive-data resolution; it never fabricates or impersonates a Judge Ballot.

For Final Results Publication, an outstanding Ballot after an Irreversible Majority does not block the settled panel winner. If that Ballot would also supply a scoring value required by the locked standings or award rules, the missing scoring input remains a separate readiness blocker until supplied or resolved without fabrication under those rules. Ballot Feedback never blocks final results.

Late or missing status is operational evidence, not a competitive result. Docket never infers a winner, creates an automatic forfeit, or penalizes an Entry merely because a Judge has not submitted.

A Judge Assignment Hold and any removal from future assignments are operational actions for the current tournament only. They do not change the Judge's assessment score, Event Qualification, Judge Qualification Tier, Judging Profile, or eligibility at another tournament.

### Speaker Points for administrative decisions

When an adjudicated completed preliminary round later receives a Disqualification Ruling, its Judge-submitted Speaker Points remain the current point values by default even when the competitive winner changes. The adopted Ruleset may explicitly require those points to be excluded or replaced; Docket applies that versioned rule prospectively to standings while retaining the original Judge-submitted values and their provenance. Elimination Rounds never acquire point values through a Disqualification Ruling.

When no preliminary-round adjudication occurred, including a pre-round forfeit or bye, Docket does not create Judge-submitted Speaker Points. If the adopted Ruleset defines an averaging formula or another point value for that preliminary Administrative Outcome, Docket creates **Administrative Points** labeled with the outcome, Ruleset version, formula version, inputs, and calculation time. Administrative Points are never displayed or audited as if a Judge submitted them. An Elimination Round contains neither Judge-submitted nor Administrative Points.

Standings calculations distinguish Judge-submitted Speaker Points, Administrative Points, excluded points, and absent points. A manual administrative value without explicit Ruleset authority is invalid.

A published standings snapshot may expose aggregate point metrics used by its locked tie-break sequence, but its standings projection never links an individual score to a Judge or exposes a detailed Ballot or Ballot Feedback. School-scoped calculation breakdowns preserve result and point provenance without broadening access to restricted Ballot content.

## Competitive Result correction

Only a Tournament Director or Tabulation Staff member with the Ballot and correction permission bundle may initiate Ballot Reopening. The action requires a reason, an impact preview, and explicit confirmation.

A corrected submission creates a **Result Correction** rather than overwriting the original Competitive Result. Docket then recomputes derived standings and advancement while retaining prior revisions for audit.

Whenever possible, Ballot Reopening returns the Competitive Result to the original Judge for resubmission. If that Judge is unavailable, a Tournament Director or Tabulation Staff member with the Ballot and correction permission bundle may enter a **Staff Result Correction** only when supported by documented Judge confirmation or a formal **Administrative Ruling**.

A Staff Result Correction records its evidence, correcting actor, and reason under the staff member's identity. It is never attributed to the Judge. Correcting the Competitive Result does not alter Ballot Feedback or its publication state.

### Completed-round decision correction

After a round reaches Completed, only a Tournament Director may create a **Completed Round Decision Correction**, and only for a verified input error or a disqualification. An input error is incorrect competitive data entered or imported into Docket rather than a disagreement with the Judge's adjudication. A disqualification correction must identify the disqualified Entry or Competitor, applicable Ruleset authority, evidence, and resulting competitive decision.

The Director reviews an impact preview and explicitly confirms the correction. Docket preserves the original Judge-submitted Competitive Result or Administrative Outcome and creates a new Director-attributed decision version; it does not reopen, edit, or impersonate the Judge's Ballot. Ballot Feedback and its publication state remain unchanged.

The correction may change the winner or decision type and, in a preliminary round only, Speaker Points when required by the verified input error or disqualification. It may not add points to an Elimination Round or change the completed round's Entries, affirmative and negative sides, Judge, room, scheduled block, Pairing Publication, or Pairing Plan. Strategic dissatisfaction, a preferred advancement outcome, or reconsideration of the Judge's substantive decision does not qualify.

Docket recomputes standings and advancement from the corrected decision and creates Downstream Conflicts wherever later artifacts disagree. The correction, evidence, Ruleset basis, impact preview, approving Director, previous and current versions, time, and notices remain auditable. If public standings, advancement, pairings, awards, or final results change, Docket also issues a public human-readable Correction Notice that explains the changed public values and competitive effect without exposing internal metadata or evidence.

### Disqualification scope

Every disqualification is a Director-created **Disqualification Ruling** with one scope supported by the adopted Ruleset:

- **Round-only loss** — changes only the identified round's competitive decision.
- **Prospective tournament removal** — preserves prior completed decisions and makes the Entry ineligible for remaining rounds.
- **Full-tournament disqualification** — removes future eligibility and applies retroactive forfeits only when the adopted Ruleset explicitly requires them.

Prior completed decisions remain current by default. When a full-tournament disqualification requires retroactive forfeits, the Director must create a separate Completed Round Decision Correction for every affected completed round; Docket does not apply one bulk overwrite. The correction preserves each original decision and all historical pairings.

Before confirmation, Docket displays the ruling's Ruleset authority, evidence, scope, affected rounds, new decisions, Entry eligibility, standings, advancement, awards, published results, and Downstream Conflicts. The Director confirms the exact previewed version. A change in scope or effects requires a new preview and confirmation, and affected participants receive versioned notices.

Already published or completed later rounds never change silently. If their Entries, sides, pairings, or advancement no longer agree with corrected inputs, Docket creates a **Downstream Conflict** and requires an explicit, attributed, audited resolution.

### Downstream Conflict resolution

Tabulation Staff may investigate a Downstream Conflict and prepare its impact analysis and resolution plan. Only a Tournament Director may approve the resolution.

Docket resolves the conflict according to how far the affected artifact has progressed:

- Unpublished artifacts are regenerated from the corrected competitive data.
- A published round that has not started is withdrawn and republished with corrected assignments and notices to affected participants.
- A started or completed downstream pairing remains as played. A Tournament Director records an Administrative Ruling that states how the triggering correction affects future standings and advancement without rewriting that pairing. The round's own competitive decision changes only through a separately justified Completed Round Decision Correction for its input error or disqualification.
- Any affected public artifact receives a human-readable **Correction Notice** describing its previous and corrected public values, competitive effect, and required participant action without internal metadata or evidence.

Every resolution records its approving Director, reason, impact, affected artifacts, and notices. Docket retains the original artifact and all prior result, standing, advancement, publication, and notice versions; resolution never deletes history.

See [ADR 0010](../docs/adr/0010-version-results-and-surface-downstream-conflicts.md).

See [ADR 0009](../docs/adr/0009-separate-results-from-feedback.md).

Under [[retention-model]], complete Ballots, every Ballot Feedback version, Judge-linked scores, panel provenance, corrections, and related competitive evidence remain restricted under their existing audiences for seven years after Tournament Closure. Expiry deletes those restricted records unless a Legal Hold applies; published round decisions, pairings, results, and public Correction Notices remain permanently available through their public projections. Judge Assessment data follows [[judge-qualification-model]] instead.
