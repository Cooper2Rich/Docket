# Pairing Model

## Tournament-defined Pairing Plan

Docket does not impose one universal Lincoln-Douglas pairing algorithm. Each tournament has a versioned **Pairing Plan** containing an ordered **Round Specification** for every configured preliminary and elimination round. The Tournament Director selects the adopted Ruleset, and the Pairing Plan expresses how that tournament will pair each round within the Ruleset and Docket's integrity constraints.

A Round Specification identifies the round label and stage, its pairing method and method-specific options, side-assignment policy, bracket and pull-up behavior when applicable, bye handling, treatment of rematches and same-School pairings, manual-adjustment permissions, and the corresponding Tournament Schedule block. A tournament may combine different supported methods and options in any order across its configured rounds rather than selecting one method for the entire tournament.

Tournament Directors and Tabulation Staff with the Pairing operations permission bundle may determine and edit a draft Pairing Plan. The plan version included with the selected Schedule Candidate is approved when the Tournament Director performs Schedule Selection. Every manual pairing adjustment records the changed pair, prior generated value, actor, reason, and time.

Schedule Selection also creates the **Pairing Configuration Lock**. The pairing method and all method-defining options assigned to every configured round become locked for the tournament. The locked options include same-School and rematch rules, side balancing, brackets and pull-ups, bye policy, standings inputs, seeding order, and whether manual pairing is permitted. Updated Entries, results, Judge availability, rooms, and other live inputs may flow through the locked policy, while schedule times and resources may change only through approved schedule revisions.

The only policy-changing exception is a **Pre-Tournament Emergency Pairing Correction** performed and approved by a Tournament Director before any tournament round enters the Started state. It may replace a locked method or option only with a recorded error or emergency reason, validation report, impact preview, explicit confirmation, and new immutable plan version. Pairing-authorized staff may provide information but cannot perform or approve this exception. Any schedule impact must complete the Schedule Revision workflow, and the corrected Pairing Plan and schedule must be republished on the Tournament Invitation Page before the first round starts. Once any round starts, no actor or workflow may change a locked method or option.

## Ruleset-aware recommendation

Docket generates a recommended Pairing Plan for the Tournament Director's adopted Ruleset, Tournament Overrides, configured round sequence, expected Entry count, advancement structure, and selected scheduling constraints. The recommendation identifies each proposed method and option, explains why it fits the round, and reports likely tradeoffs such as rematch exposure, side imbalance, bracket pull-ups, byes, and schedule pressure.

The recommendation is advisory. A Tournament Director or Pairing-authorized Tabulation Staff member may configure another supported combination. Docket validates the plan and distinguishes mandatory Ruleset or integrity violations from warnings and preferences; it does not silently replace the administrators' plan with its recommendation.

### Default recommendation when the Ruleset is silent

For preliminary rounds, Docket recommends avoiding rematches and same-School pairings whenever a feasible alternative exists. It recommends keeping each Entry's cumulative affirmative-minus-negative difference within one, minimizing bracket pull-ups, and never assigning an Entry a second bye while another eligible Entry has received none. When a bye is necessary, the recommended recipient is the lowest-ranked eligible Entry without a prior bye.

If those preferences conflict, Docket presents the infeasibility, identifies the affected Entries and constraints, and recommends an explicit relaxation rather than silently choosing one. The administrators select and lock the applicable policy before Schedule Selection; a runtime pairing conflict must be resolved through the locked policy or an authorized correction workflow.

For elimination rounds, Docket recommends preserving the seeded bracket even when it produces a rematch or same-School matchup. In an elimination **Flip Round**, cumulative affirmative/negative difference is not considered. Entries without a prior matchup use the tournament's locked side-flip procedure. When the Entries have previously met, no flip occurs: Docket finds their most recent prior Pairing Publication that reached the Started state and reverses its sides, making the prior affirmative Entry negative and the prior negative Entry affirmative.

The resulting **Prior-Match Side Lock** records the source tournament, round, Pairing Publication version, and prior side assignments. It depends on the published pairing that governed the matchup, not on its Competitive Result or winner. Ballot Reopening, Result Correction, Administrative Ruling, or another later result change therefore cannot change the locked elimination sides.

## Initial pairing-method catalog

### Bounded calculation

Backend interview Q20–Q21 sets a 30-second initial round-pairing target per event/division, with visible progress, and allows the best valid candidate found within that budget without proof of global optimality. Validation must enforce the adopted Ruleset, locked Pairing Plan, and mandatory integrity constraints. Candidate ranking can optimize only the preferences that those rules permit. This does not authorize changing a locked method, weakening a hard constraint, or bypassing approval and publication.

If the budget ends with a valid candidate, return it as valid without claiming proven optimality. If no valid candidate was found, report timeout/resource-budget exhaustion; claim infeasibility only when established. No invalid pairing becomes operative merely to meet the response target. Retain source versions, seed, algorithm version, validation outcome, and preference metrics for reproduction. See [[backend-implementation-proposal]] for resource and verification details.

Q22 requires a clear timeout message with a Retry action. Only an authorized staff member can request another attempt after timeout; the application does not automatically loop the search. Each intentional retry creates a separately identified attempt under the same time budget and records its relationship to the prior outcome. Revalidate current grants, source versions, and the locked policy, and prevent duplicate delivery or repeated clicks for the same retry request from creating duplicate jobs. Retrying does not relax a constraint, imply success, or publish a pairing.

The first Lincoln-Douglas backend release supports these versioned methods:

- **Random Draw** — forms valid matchups from the eligible Entry pool using a recorded random seed after applying the selected hard constraints.
- **Preset Pairings** — uses a complete pairing set entered or imported before the round and validates it against the Ruleset and locked options.
- **Seeded High-Low** — orders the eligible field by the selected seed and pairs the highest remaining Entry against the lowest remaining Entry.
- **Power High-Low** — groups Entries into the configured current-record brackets, ranks each bracket with the locked standings inputs, and pairs its upper portion against its lower portion.
- **Power High-High** — groups and ranks Entries under the locked standings policy, then pairs adjacent highest-ranked available Entries.
- **Round-Robin** — generates the configured rotation so each eligible Entry meets every other Entry in its pool once when the round structure permits.
- **Seeded Elimination Bracket** — places advancing Entries into a fixed high-versus-low elimination bracket and advances winners through the locked bracket structure.
- **Fully Manual Pairing** — allows Pairing-authorized operators to construct each valid matchup directly under the Ruleset, locked options, audit, review, and publication controls.

Each method has a stable identifier, version, declared input contract, validation rules, deterministic tie-break behavior, and reproducibility record. Random Draw additionally stores its seed. Every generated or manual output retains the exact method version, locked configuration, inputs, and actor or system action that produced it. The first release does not execute tournament-supplied scripts, formulas, plugins, or arbitrary custom pairing code.

Regardless of configuration, a pairing may not place an Entry against itself, assign an active Entry more than once in one round, use an Entry that is ineligible for that round, violate a mandatory adopted Ruleset constraint, or publish a round outside its approved Tournament Schedule block. Rules for rematches, same-School pairings, side balance, brackets, pulls, and byes are controlled by the selected Round Specification except where the adopted Ruleset makes one mandatory.

## Ordinary pairing approval and publication

Docket validates each generated, preset, or manual pairing against the adopted Ruleset, locked Pairing Plan, current eligible Entries, standings inputs, Judge eligibility, room assignments, and matching Tournament Schedule block. An invalid pairing cannot receive **Pairing Approval**.

For an elimination round, Docket auto-populates the schedule block with the current bracket Competitors and eligible recommended Judges as a Pending Out-Round Schedule Review. Pairing-authorized Tabulation Staff may confirm, deny, or edit the Competitor and Judge proposals. Each edit creates a new version and repeats validation; the pending review is nonoperative and cannot become a Pairing Publication without the Out-Round Verification Gate.

A power-pairing method may consume a current versioned standings snapshot before that snapshot's participant-publication checkpoint. The snapshot and every source result version remain recorded on the pairing. The Standings Publication Policy controls disclosure only and cannot force Docket to pair from stale data.

A Tournament Director or Tabulation Staff member with the Pairing operations permission bundle may approve one exact validated pairing version. Any later edit or regenerated output creates a new version and invalidates the prior approval. The approving actor, validation result, inputs, method version, configuration version, and approval time remain recorded.

After approval, a Tournament Director or Tabulation Staff member with the Publication operations permission bundle may publish only that exact approved version. One person may perform both actions when they hold both authorities. Pairing Approval and Pairing Publication remain separate attributed events even when performed by the same person.

Pairing Publication creates a Competitor-scoped view for each authenticated Competitor named in that pairing and sends that Competitor an in-app and email notice. A Competitor may see only their own Pairing Publications through this private view. Direct Competitor tournament notices are otherwise limited to Disqualifications and Corrections; Entry acceptance and rejection notices go to the responsible Coach.

Ordinary approval does not grant correction authority. A Pre-Tournament Emergency Pairing Correction, Pairing Withdrawal, or Round Pairing Correction continues to require the specific Tournament Director action defined below.

## Pairing lifecycle

Every round pairing follows this ordered lifecycle:

1. **Draft** — authorized operators may change inputs or manually construct the pairing.
2. **Generated or Entered** — Docket or an authorized operator creates one immutable output version.
3. **Validated** — Docket confirms the exact version satisfies its Ruleset, Pairing Plan, eligibility, assignment, and schedule checks.
4. **Approved** — an authorized actor approves that exact validated version.
5. **Published** — an authorized actor releases that exact approved version as the Pairing Publication.
6. **Started** — the published round begins and its pairing becomes immutable.
7. **Completed** — the round concludes and permanently retains the pairing that governed it.

A failed validation returns the work to Draft with structured errors. Any change to an input or pairing before publication creates a new Draft version and invalidates prior validation and approval. A Published pairing may transition to **Withdrawn** only before Started and only through Director-approved Pairing Withdrawal; its replacement must repeat generation or entry, validation, approval, and publication.

Docket does not permit a skipped, reversed, or in-place transition. It preserves every version, state change, validation result, actor, and timestamp. A Started or Completed pairing can never return to an earlier state or be replaced. Completion freezes the pairing, not every competitive decision: the narrow Director authority to change a completed round's decision for an input error or disqualification is defined in [[ballot-model]] and never changes participants, sides, Judge, room, or schedule history.

## Schedule and invitation-page alignment

Every Round Specification maps to one round block in the selected Tournament Schedule. Before the tournament starts, **Tournament Schedule Publication** places the approved round sequence, labels, stages, start times, expected durations, Scheduled Breaks, and human-readable Pairing Plan rules on the public **Tournament Invitation Page**. This publishes the tournament plan, not future Entry-versus-Entry assignments or internal version metadata.

Each later Pairing Publication must use the Round Specification for that round and the corresponding published schedule block. Docket blocks Pairing Publication when the pairing belongs to a missing or different round block or would contradict the current published Tournament Schedule.

The first-round Pairing Publication also references and atomically locks the Director-accepted Standings Rules Configuration, Advancement Plan, and Award Plan for that event. Docket blocks publication when any required accepted fingerprint is missing, when a Plan references different governing sources, or when the artifacts are incompatible. Later standings, advancement, seeding, and award calculations must identify and use those locked versions internally; ordinary uploads cannot replace them.

The Seeded Elimination Bracket method consumes the versioned Advancement Field from [[advancement-model]], not the preliminary standings table directly. Each elimination pairing records the Advancement Plan version, Advancement Field version, standings snapshot, and locked standings configuration fingerprint that supplied its seed and bracket position.

Docket cannot generate an operative elimination pairing from a draft or merely approved Advancement Field. The exact Field fingerprint must have completed Advancement Field Publication. Any Field recalculation invalidates dependent unstarted pairing drafts and approvals; later elimination pairing validation must reference the current published Field.

An advancement Play-In uses configured elimination Round Specifications and follows the same immutable pairing lifecycle and point-free Elimination Round Ballot rules. A Seeded Draw at the cut is an advancement selection procedure, not the Random Draw pairing method; it records its separately published seed, algorithm version, ordered tied Entry inputs, and output in the Advancement Field before bracket pairing begins.

The Seeded Elimination Bracket method accepts partial fields. It uses the Advancement Field's next-power-of-two bracket capacity, places highest seeds on the bye lines, and pairs the remaining field through Docket's versioned canonical high-versus-low seed map. Rematches, same-School matchups, and other preferences cannot alter bracket positions. A bye is an advancement artifact rather than a fabricated pairing or Judge Ballot.

Before any elimination round Starts, an Advancement Field Recalculation invalidates unstarted elimination pairings derived from the prior field. A published pairing requires Pairing Withdrawal, then regeneration, validation, approval, and republication against the new Field version. Once any elimination round Starts, no later eligibility change may regenerate, refill, or reseed the bracket; only the vacated line's versioned advancement outcome changes.

The Out-Round Verification Gate prevents a successful No-Show Dispute from ordinarily changing advancement or award inputs after elimination publication. Before Tournament Schedule Publication, the Director selects one supported tournament-scoped Out-Round Confirmation Permission rather than a person. The selection locks at first-round start; only a reasoned staffing-access correction may replace it prospectively, never rewrite completed verifications. The standard gate uses one Pairing-authorized Tabulation Staff verifier and a second distinct active holder of that permission. If an intended confirmer loses permission before acting, Docket discards the unfinished confirmation but preserves the first verification while its exact sources remain unchanged and allows another active holder to confirm. If no eligible second confirmer is reasonably available, a Director may activate a Single-Person Out-Round Verification Exception for one exact round and schedule version. Its verifier must hold Pairing authority, need not hold the unavailable confirmation permission, and may be a Director who also holds Pairing authority. Docket records the activation reason, availability check, exact sources, warning acknowledgment, actor, and time as restricted audit data and does not expose the exception to participants absent an ordinary later correction.

The gate verifies the prior result or Administrative Outcome, Ballot or panel majority, unresolved disputes and corrections, disqualifications, advancing Entry identity and School, seed, bracket position, sides, opponent, Judge qualification and conflicts, room, scheduled time, current Advancement Field, and bracket lineage. A relevant source change makes the approval stale and requires a new exception if separation remains unavailable. Before the round Starts, the gate briefly confirms that no source changed. A non-overridden gate may use a fresh warned single-person exception again when no second verifier is still available. An earlier override remains visibly marked as overridden and requires two distinct authorized actors to confirm the exact version again; the single-person exception cannot satisfy that step, and any new hard-constraint failure blocks the round. A failed pre-publication gate may proceed only when a Tournament Director invokes an Out-Round Gate Override and a second distinct holder of the Out-Round Confirmation Permission confirms it after Docket displays every failed check and the competitive and downstream impact warning. Docket records the reason, impact preview, affected round and Entries, source versions, actors, confirmation, and internal time. The override may address only operational uncertainty or incomplete verification and cannot bypass a verified conflict, eligibility, Judge qualification, disqualification, Ruleset requirement, bracket identity, or another hard integrity constraint. Override metadata stays restricted; participants see only the ordinary Pairing Publication and later applicable Correction Notices. A restricted active-risk warning remains until round completion and downstream review, after which the full record remains Seven-Year Competitive Evidence. A harmless review closes the warning as `Reviewed—No Competitive Effect` without a public Correction Notice unless public information changed. If the pre-start confirmation finds an error, Docket returns to the full first verification, requires correction at the authoritative source, regenerates the pending review, and repeats approval and Pairing Publication. If a round starts without completing the gate, Docket preserves it as operated, creates a high-priority Downstream Conflict for Director resolution, and corrects only unstarted downstream artifacts with applicable Correction Notices.

A pre-elimination-start Emergency Advancement Plan Correction follows that same withdrawal and republication path. After any elimination round Starts, a corrected Plan cannot invalidate or regenerate the bracket: every existing pairing retains the Plan and Field versions that governed it, and Docket records a Downstream Conflict against the later corrected policy instead of rewriting the matchup.

If a Pairing Plan change alters round order, count, timing, duration, or resource assumptions, the affected Tournament Schedule must be revised and published before the changed pairing can be published. The invitation page always presents the current human-readable schedule and Pairing Plan rules without raw versions, publishing attribution, or exact publication time; authorized staff and audit history retain that metadata and prior versions.

## Lock and change lifecycle

Tournament Directors and Pairing-authorized Tabulation Staff may edit and replace draft Pairing Plan versions until Schedule Selection. Docket validates every draft, but no draft is participant-facing or locked merely because it was generated or recommended. Schedule Selection locks every round's method and method-defining options immediately; Tournament Schedule Publication later locks the full displayed Pairing Plan version on the invitation page.

Tournament Schedule Publication locks the approved Pairing Plan version shown on the Tournament Invitation Page. After that publication, Pairing-authorized Tabulation Staff may prepare a **Pairing Plan Revision**, including its validation report and impact preview, but an ordinary revision must retain every locked method and method-defining option. A Tournament Director must approve and republish the exact new plan version with a reason. If the revision changes timing, round order, duration, rooms, Judge assumptions, or another schedule input, the existing Schedule Revision Approval and Publication workflow must finish before the Director republishes the aligned Pairing Plan. Only the Director-only pre-tournament emergency process may alter the locked policy.

Once the affected round has had a Pairing Publication, a plan or pairing change is allowed only while the round remains unstarted. A Tournament Director must approve **Pairing Withdrawal**, after which Docket preserves the withdrawn version and sends a delivery-tracked impact notice to affected Directors, staff, Judges, and active School Members. The corrected round must receive a new Pairing Publication using the same locked method; Docket never edits the published pairing in place.

The corrected public pairing also receives a human-readable Correction Notice stating the previous and corrected public assignment and participant action without exposing internal versions, fingerprints, actors, exact timestamps, evidence, or audit details.

Tabulation Staff with the Pairing operations permission bundle may prepare a **Round Pairing Correction** only to resolve an error in one unstarted round. The correction must apply the current locked method and options, show the original pairing, corrected pairing, error, affected assignments, and validation result, and receive explicit Tournament Director sign-off before it takes effect. If the round was already published, Pairing Withdrawal and a new Pairing Publication are required. Operational correction authority cannot be used to choose a preferred matchup or change the tournament's pairing policy.

A **Qualifying Pairing Error** is limited to objectively verifiable evidence that:

- an Entry was inactive, omitted, duplicated, or assigned more than once;
- the calculation used incorrect standings or another corrected tournament input;
- Docket failed to apply the locked pairing method, method-defining options, or deterministic tie-break policy;
- the pairing violates a locked rematch, same-School, side, bracket, pull-up, seed, or bye rule;
- the Pairing Publication contains an ineligible Judge or a room or schedule conflict; or
- a software or calculation failure produced an invalid result.

Strategic dissatisfaction, matchup preference, perceived competitive advantage, disagreement with a valid deterministic outcome, or a desire to improve an otherwise valid pairing is not an error. The correction request must identify the category and evidence before the Tournament Director may sign off. A corrected standings input arising from a Ballot correction also remains subject to [[ballot-model]].

An approved Emergency Standings Rules Correction is another corrected standings input. Docket regenerates unpublished pairings, but a published unstarted pairing requires Pairing Withdrawal and a newly validated, approved, and published version. Started and completed pairings remain as played and receive a Downstream Conflict when they no longer agree with the corrected standings. The emergency correction cannot be used to rewrite them.

A started or completed round is immutable. A Pairing Plan Revision cannot rewrite its participants, sides, Judge, room, schedule block, or governing Round Specification. Later corrections must operate prospectively and use the Ballot and Downstream Conflict rules where applicable.

An Entry Restoration after first-round Pairing Publication is allowed only before any round starts, only for a mistakenly withdrawn Entry that was Accepted immediately before withdrawal, and only with Tournament Director approval. Every affected Published pairing follows Pairing Withdrawal and complete regeneration or correction, validation, approval, republication, and notice requirements under the existing locked method. Once any round starts, Entry Restoration cannot alter the active field or any pairing.

Late Registration Exceptions end at first-round Pairing Publication and cannot add or edit an Entry afterward. A Late Withdrawal remains possible at any time. Unpublished pairings regenerate without the Entry; an affected Published but unstarted pairing requires Pairing Withdrawal and republication; Started and Completed pairings remain as operated and use the applicable future-eligibility, correction, and Downstream Conflict rules.

An Approved or Partially Approved Accommodation Request supplies only its minimized active operational constraints to pairing. It does not expose the complete request to pairing staff or become a Qualifying Pairing Error. An affected unpublished pairing regenerates under the locked method. An affected published but unstarted pairing requires Director-approved Pairing Withdrawal, validation, approval, and republication; the accommodation grants no emergency policy-change or direct-edit authority. Started and completed pairings remain as operated, although a feasible implementation instruction may apply during the active round without changing participants, sides, Judge, room identity, or the competitive record.

Pairing impact notices and any public Correction Notice state the changed assignment and required participant action with only a generic restricted-operational-adjustment reason. They never name, link to, or confirm an Accommodation Request. The restricted accommodation audit history retains the actual cause.

Only the Competitor's Coach may submit a verified Judge Conflict for the Competitor or Entry. An optional per-event Judge Striking Policy is published before registration opens and locked with the Pairing Plan. One portal window governs a multi-day tournament unless that policy predefines separate event sessions. It opens 36 hours before Tournament Start Time and closes at 3:00 p.m. in the official named tournament timezone on the preceding calendar day, controls across daylight-saving changes, and is also displayed in each user's local time. Each Entry designates a Primary Responsible Coach and may predesignate a Backup Responsible Coach before opening; both require active School Membership tied to the affected Competitors, and the backup acts only when the primary is unavailable. The Entry initially chooses its hard strikes and ranked preferences, while the acting responsible Coach resolves teammate disagreement and approves the submission in Docket. The transactional portal receipt makes it operative without emailing the Tournament Director. Hard strikes use the same configured limit for every Entry and prevent assignment; ranked preferences influence optimization without guaranteeing exclusion; verified conflicts remain separate and uncapped. Before closing, every change requires fresh Coach approval. Unapproved selections expire after six-hour and one-hour reminders. Ordinary workflow permits no post-close Judge addition or supplemental strike. A late submission requires platform-health evidence affecting that Entry, a recovery window equal to the outage capped at 30 minutes, Director approval, and no known opponent or pairing. If no qualified Judge remains, publication blocks until staff add one or the acting responsible Coach releases a strike after reviewing the issue with the affected Competitors; that release is final, and Docket does not let staff choose or override a constraint. Strike selections, approvals, corrections, releases, and assignment provenance use the same restricted seven-year Competitive Evidence retention as the other Competitor tournament records. They are restricted to the submitting Competitors and responsible Coach and authorized tournament or audit staff, with only anonymized aggregates available operationally. Judges and opposing Schools cannot see them. See [[scheduling-model]].

An Emergency Replacement Judge may enter after portal close only for an unexpected withdrawal or No-Show and only through reasoned Director or Judge-and-room-authorized Tabulation Staff approval. Docket revalidates every verified conflict, does not reopen strikes, prefers eligible Judges previously available to the strike process, and requires assignment-specific Tabulation Staff sign-off. Docket immediately sends a generic privacy-minimized in-app and email notice to the Judge, affected Competitors and Coaches, Directors, and relevant Tabulation Staff. The Judge must acknowledge within ten minutes and before start, using the remaining pre-start time when shorter. Until then the assignment is unconfirmed. One delivery cycle dispatches both electronic channels and fails only when neither confirms delivery; successful delivery without acknowledgment is not failure. Staff may record a restricted operational contact attempt but cannot acknowledge for the Judge. After three failed cycles the assignment becomes Delivery Failed, creates no No-Show or Judge Reliability Record, and staff must select another eligible Judge. A late acknowledgment cannot revive it, and later selection requires a new assignment. Tentative identity and Published Judging Profile are limited to affected parties and staff until acknowledgment and sign-off. Every uncapped use requires a fresh reason and sign-off; the second use in one event or third tournament-wide and every later use produces a Director staffing warning. An acknowledged emergency Judge who does not appear follows the ordinary Judge No-Show workflow and any replacement requires a new emergency decision. The emergency path cannot bypass the ordinary pool or strikes. An affected Coach may report a verified conflict, qualification error, or assignment-data error before start; a verified conflict invalidates assignment and a potentially disqualifying allegation must be resolved before start, but the Coach has no new strike or veto.

After Judge-and-room-authorized Tabulation Staff classify a Judge or Competitor as a No-Show following the default ten-minute or configured published grace period and required contact attempts, Docket may generate a Replacement Candidate Pool for the affected round. A Competitor may file directly in Docket within 60 minutes or before the event's next round starts, whichever is first; the responsible Coach is associated and notified and may add a statement but cannot block, withdraw, or suppress the dispute. Only the filing Competitor may withdraw before decision; explicit confirmation makes withdrawal final for that classification, preserves the operative classification, and creates an audit event. Evidence may include check-in, arrival, messaging, staff-contact, and delivery records or proof of an incorrect published time or room, without required medical records. The Director decides within 30 minutes or before the next event round, whichever is first; overdue review escalates while the classification and replacement remain operative without automatic approval or denial. A rejection is final for the tournament except for an objective system or input correction through the governed correction path. Docket sends its privacy-minimized notice in-app and by email, tracks delivery without requiring acknowledgment, keeps it in the Competitor Account, and does not reopen the dispute or delay operations after delivery failure. The pool shows every eligible candidate and separately ranked recommendations but never inserts one automatically. A Competitor candidate must be authenticated, fully eligible and authorized for the same School and event, have no conflicting assignment, and satisfy the Ruleset's timing and substitution rules. If none exists, the opposing Entry receives a Bye Round under the Ruleset. Before sign-off, Tabulation Staff may restore a late-arriving original assignee; after replacement or bye approval, reversal requires a Director correction, and no restoration is possible after Started. A successful dispute appends a reasoned reversal, removes the current restricted No-Show flag, and corrects every unstarted artifact while preserving the original classification in restricted audit history. Tabulation Staff sign-off creates a new validated pairing or schedule version, preserves the original, sends affected-party notices, and publishes a Correction Notice when public information changed; the public notice states the prior and corrected artifact and effect with the generic reason “No-Show classification corrected” and no private evidence, notes, or actor metadata. No replacement may rewrite a Started or Completed pairing.
