# Backend Roadmap

## Current milestone

Build and verify one complete tournament lifecycle for a single debate format before expanding backend breadth or beginning the main UI build.

The lifecycle must cover:

1. Tournament creation and configuration
2. Entry registration
3. Preliminary-round pairing and scheduling
4. Judge and room assignment
5. Ballot submission and validation
6. Standings and advancement
7. Elimination rounds when configured
8. Final results publication

This is an end-to-end product slice, not a commitment to implement every planned Docket capability before UI work begins. The first slice should expose and stabilize the core domain model, state transitions, invariants, and administrative recovery needs. Later debate formats and broader capabilities should extend this foundation incrementally.

Authentication enters the TypeScript backend only through a Google OpenID Connect adapter. A runtime-validated `GoogleIdentitySubject` contains the accepted issuer and stable subject identifier and has a database uniqueness constraint across all Docket Accounts; email, name, and hosted-domain claims remain replaceable attributes. The server verifies Google's signature and key, issuer, Docket client audience, time claims, verified email, one-time state, nonce, and cross-site request protections before issuing a Docket Session. No client-only Google success result can create a session.

The authentication adapter does not contain Docket authorization. A verified Google account may create a role-free Basic Docket Account for Competitor, Coach, or Judge use. Platform-permission assignment additionally validates an approved organization-managed Workspace domain, but domain membership never generates a grant. Command handlers resolve explicit platform permissions, Competitor-School relationships, School Memberships, Tournament Staff Assignments, Judge assignments, and other scoped grants independently, allowing one Account to hold multiple roles without combining their contexts.

The session module uses explicit runtime-validated policy classes: ordinary sessions have a 12-hour inactivity and seven-day absolute limit; entering an Active Role Context for Platform Administrator or Legal and Privacy Operations activates the 30-minute inactivity and 12-hour absolute privileged limits through Docket's native role switcher without a separate Account or login merely to switch. Each browser tab stores its own current context while an Account-level last-used-context value synchronizes across devices and may restore an authorized privileged context at sign-in. No user command clears this memory; authority invalidation clears an unusable value. Privileged restoration emits a security alert and a visible banner with device, approximate location, time, and session termination. Every request carries an explicit context identifier validated against current grants; stale contexts fail without resource disclosure. The client persistently displays its role and scope, uses distinct privileged styling, and destroys context-scoped caches on switching. A switch command is rejected while the client reports unresolved local edits and never serializes an unfinished form into the destination context.

Deep links contain a context reference but no private payload and require confirmation before a cross-context switch, with privileged auto-switch prohibited. One Account-level notification projection labels every item by exact context and withholds private detail until that context is active. Per-context routine delivery preferences cannot suppress typed required security, authority, emergency, Legal Hold, or governance notices. Retention services keep ordinary switch events for two years and privileged entry and exit provenance for seven years.

A typed recent-reauthentication guard still requires a Google result no older than 10 minutes for ownership transfer, role or permission change, identity-link replacement, Legal Hold action, sensitive-data export, Account suspension, and Final Results publication or correction. Database and session-store constraints allow at most five ordinary sessions and one privileged session per Account; a new privileged session revokes the old one, and Account-facing commands list and revoke sessions.

Docket persists no password or additional-factor secret and delegates factor enrollment, challenges, and Google-account recovery to Google. Email and profile changes update restricted identity attributes without changing the stable Account link or grants. After failed Google recovery, `ReplaceGoogleIdentityLink` and `ResolveDuplicateDocketAccounts` operate only through a Platform Identity Review that requires replacement-account control plus two independent identity links, preserves original event attribution, and never automatically merges records. The affected holder, a responsible School Manager or Tournament Owner, or a documenting Platform Administrator may request review; only Platform Administrators decide it. Approval cardinality is one administrator for ordinary Accounts and two for Accounts with current or historical sensitive authority. One reconsideration command is available to the affected holder for 14 days when it carries new evidence or a specific process error; it excludes prior approvers, appends rather than edits its final decision, and changes current links and grants prospectively. An idempotent retention job deletes encrypted evidence 30 days after final resolution while retaining only the two- or seven-year content-free decision record and respecting Legal Hold. During a Google outage, valid Docket sessions continue only until normal expiry; sign-in, renewal, link changes, and reauthentication-guarded commands fail closed. Provider failure has no local-password, support-bypass, or impersonation fallback.

A runtime-validated Access Offer aggregate contains the intended verified email, exact authority scope, offering actor and authority proof, issue and expiry instants, and Pending, Accepted, Redundant, Revoked, Expired, or Invalidated state. School and tournament offers expire after seven days and platform offers after 24 hours. Email delivery is required and a separate Access Inbox projection is added when a matching Account exists; delivery state never mutates authority state or expiry. Acceptance requires the bound Google email and exact-offer confirmation; platform, Owner, and Director offers also require recent Google Reauthentication. Exact existing authority produces Redundant without a new grant, while any exclusivity conflict rejects atomically. Retargeting is prohibited, reissue creates a new aggregate and revokes prior exact-email/exact-scope Pending versions, notice resend preserves the original token and expiry, and loss of offering authority invalidates every dependent Pending offer.

The backend has no batch Access Offer command. Every School Membership, tournament staff, Director, Owner, platform, and Legal and Privacy Operations offer is validated and issued individually. Wrong-recipient correction revokes and reissues instead of editing. Acceptance uses one transaction to revalidate offer state, Google identity, offering authority, and exclusivity, so only the first terminal action wins. Delivery retries exactly three times over 24 hours; a permanent bounce stops retries, marks only that delivery Undeliverable, retains an available matching Access Inbox item, and alerts the offering actor without extending expiry.

Typed `SuspendDocketAccount`, `ReviewAccountSuspension`, `ChallengeAccountSuspension`, `ReinstateDocketAccount`, `ExtendAccountSuspension`, `ScheduleVoluntaryAccountDeactivation`, `CancelVoluntaryAccountDeactivation`, and `ExecuteVoluntaryAccountDeactivation` commands keep security response separate from deletion. Suspension immediately revokes sessions and blocks authentication, requires independent review within 24 hours, and expires after seven days absent approval; reinstatement requires resolved-risk evidence and independent approval, and each extension is capped at 30 days with fresh review. One final challenge within seven days requires new evidence or a process error and two administrators uninvolved in imposition or prior approval while the suspension remains effective. Evidence deletion runs 90 days after final disposition, retaining a two- or seven-year content-free decision record. Suspension emits a private Account notice and minimized affected-School and Tournament Owner notices, never transfers authority, and routes continuity to existing recovery, reassignment, and schedule-revision commands. Failed Account notice delivery retries three times over 24 hours, persists the restricted inbox notice, and escalates without changing suspension state.

Deactivation scheduling rejects while an exclusive duty remains and permits cancellation for seven days only after Google Reauthentication; a Platform Identity Review opened before execution pauses the job when Google access is lost. A Competitor may lodge the request during an active tournament, but the seven-day execution window begins only when the Competitor has no future round assignment and every Disqualification or Correction requiring their response is resolved or past its deadline. It does not wait for Tournament Closure or unrelated Judge feedback. Execution irreversibly removes the Google link and future authority. A 30-day job removes unnecessary profile data while retention services preserve required records, names already part of an independently retained public or historical tournament record, and only minimal restricted attribution elsewhere. See [[access-model]], [[retention-model]], and [ADR 0029](../docs/adr/0029-use-google-as-the-sole-authentication-provider.md).

Session termination is Docket-scoped: ordinary logout revokes one Docket Session without ending the Google session, while Log Out Everywhere revokes all Docket Sessions. Security notifications are emitted for new or replaced privileged sessions, global logout, administrative session termination, and suspension, but not routine single-session logout.

The Google profile adapter accepts only issuer and subject, verified email, and display name. It rejects and does not store the Google profile image and requests no unrelated Google product data or demographic fields. Docket stores a separately governed user-selected Display Name while retaining the Google name only in restricted identity projections. Self-service Display Name changes are limited to one per 30 days; a documented Platform Administrator review may authorize an earlier safety or identity correction, and historical publications retain their original name. The first slice collects no birth date for Coach or Judge authentication and leaves Competitor minor privacy and consent rules unresolved.

A self-service Account Data Export projector includes the holder's profile, role and authority history, Access Offer history, sessions, notification settings, Judge qualification summaries, and own submissions while excluding third-party private data and restricted security, identity, Legal Hold, and investigation evidence. A fresh-Google-reauthenticated command creates it asynchronously, rate-limits the Account to one completed export per 24 hours, provides only an authenticated download, sends a readiness notice with no attachment, and deletes the generated archive after seven days. The Account Security History projection contains only sign-ins across devices, Google Reauthentication, suspension-status events, and completed exports and expires after two years.

An authenticated Active Tournament Directory read model powers the application's Invitations section. First Tournament Invitation Page publication inserts a tournament, cancellation removes it, and Tournament Closure moves it into the public Tournament Archive; Drafts never project. Cancellation retains a public direct page with status, date, explanation, prior publications, and notices but disables participation actions, removes internal discovery links and sitemap inclusion, emits search-engine noindex instructions, and creates no searchable cancellation projection. It does not create an Account, Entry, role, assignment, or authority grant. The active projection supplies the accepted listing fields, underway-first then nearest-date default order, alternative sorts, and accessible search and filters in [[tournament-directory-model]].

The unauthenticated Tournament Archive projection supports the accepted tournament, host, School, event, year, date, location, modality, and event-family search and filters and defaults newest-first. It shows current results by default, keeps chronological public Correction Notices visible, and exposes superseded public versions through expandable Tournament Publication History without restricted metadata. Both directory projections use opaque cursor pages of 50 and preserve query and return-position state. Geographic inputs support country, state, city, and user-entered ZIP or postal-code radius without Google-profile or required device location. Account-scoped Saved Tournament Filters persist up to 20 named query, filter, sort, and optional explicitly entered location preferences across devices, allow one explicit default, never infer a location, and support rename, edit, and deletion.

Account-scoped Saved Tournaments support active and Closed tournaments and separate opt-in schedule, registration, pairing, results, and Correction Notice subscriptions. Cancellation emits one notice and removes the item from saved-tournament discovery.

Register or Manage Entries routes an authenticated Coach with the Entry Manager role in the required School Membership into the existing Registration Policy commands. The exclusive School Manager may also hold Entry Manager and every other compatible Coach role. Without a qualifying Membership, `RequestExistingSchoolMembership` routes to the current School Manager for sign-off and expires after seven days or at the applicable registration deadline. A reminder precedes expiration; denial records a reason and permits one materially corrected re-request. Platform intervention is valid only when no functioning Manager exists and never overrides an active Manager. Absence of a verified School or Manager routes to Provisional School and first-membership platform verification.

`IssueCompetitorSchoolInvitation` is available only to an authorized Roster Manager and binds the invitation to the identified Competitor's exact verified Google email without domain inference. Docket delivers it by email and private Account inbox, expires it after seven days, permits pre-acceptance revocation, treats an identical pending issue as a no-op, and resends without extending expiry. A wrong recipient requires revocation and reissue because retargeting is invalid. Only the identified Competitor may accept. Acceptance replaces the Account's single current Competitor School Affiliation if it names a different School; it never creates a second simultaneous affiliation or a new Account. The replacement applies immediately to future activity while existing Entries retain their represented School through the tournament; a same-tournament invariant prevents the Account from representing both Schools, and acceptance emits notices to the Competitor and authorized Managers of both Schools. Either the Competitor or Roster Manager may terminate an affiliation: new Entries for that School stop immediately, while final removal waits until no active Entry represents it. Authentication without accepted affiliation cannot supply a Tournament Roster candidate. Only a Coach whose active School Membership carries Entry Manager may place an affiliated Competitor on the Tournament Roster, provide final School sign-off, or prepare an Entry; that role grants no Tournament Schedule, Pairing, or publication command. Coach commands may manage only affiliation and School-scoped Entry data, never Competitor identity, Account security, Display Name, settings, exports, or another School's data.

The cross-School projection follows explicit ownership boundaries. Competitor profile settings and personal tournament history follow the stable Account; historical Entries and public results preserve represented-School attribution. A destination School receives only current affiliation and future-Entry information, never former-School private notes, attestations, accommodations, communications, evidence, or internal records.

Competitor notification projection emits in-app and email messages only for that Competitor's Pairing Publications, Disqualifications, and Corrections. Entry admission commands and acceptance or rejection notices remain Coach and authorized tournament responsibilities.

Judge This Tournament creates pending Judge Pool Participation with availability and event preferences. A Director or Judge-and-room-authorized staff member accepts it only after qualification, preference, availability, and conflict validation, then Docket emits in-app and email acceptance or categorized decline without qualification effect. The first release has no automatic capacity limit or waitlist. Authenticated Coaches, Competitors, accepted Judges, and the Director may read the accepted pool; authorized staff retain operational access. The participant projection always shows the current pool with a subject-to-change notice, adding or removing a Judge immediately when acceptance changes. It contains only accepted Judge Docket Display Name, Independent or School-Supplied affiliation, and accepted event categories, excluding qualification score or tier, assessment information, availability, conflicts, contact data, request state, private Judging Profile, withdrawal records, and pool-change actors, reasons, or timestamps. Pending and declined Judges see only their own participation status; authorized staff retain restricted pool-change history.

A runtime-validated optional per-event Judge Striking Policy is published before registration opens and locks with the Pairing Plan. When disabled, only verified Coach-submitted Judge Conflicts constrain assignments. When enabled, the native Judge Strike Portal opens at `tournamentStartAt - 36h` and closes at 3:00 p.m. in the tournament timezone on the preceding calendar day. Each individual Competitor or the Competitors in a team Entry initially select an equal configured allowance of hard strikes plus ranked preferences; only the responsible Coach assigned to the affected Competitors may resolve disagreement, approve the submission, or voluntarily release a hard strike. Approval is an atomic Docket command that returns an in-app receipt and creates the host-authorized projection without email delivery. Every pre-close edit invalidates prior approval. Reminders issue six and one hours before close; unapproved selections become nonoperative. Ordinary commands reject post-close Judge additions and provide no supplemental strike path. Post-close input correction or late submission requires a verified Docket outage, Director approval, and proof that no opponent or pairing was known. Hard strikes prohibit assignment; ranked preferences influence optimization without guaranteeing exclusion; verified conflicts remain separate uncapped hard constraints. When no qualified Judge remains, validation blocks publication and exposes staff remedies to add an eligible Judge or request the responsible Coach's final voluntary release after Competitor review; staff cannot choose the released strike or override a constraint. Strike selectors remain restricted to the submitting Competitors and Coach and authorized pairing or Judge-room staff, while Judges and opposing Schools cannot read them and staff analytics expose only anonymized aggregates. Policy change is limited to the existing Director-controlled pre-tournament emergency correction.

One window governs a multi-day tournament unless the policy predefines separate event sessions. The close derives from the official named tournament timezone and local calendar across daylight-saving changes; the read model also displays the user's local equivalent without changing the controlling instant. Each Entry identifies one Primary Responsible Coach and may predesignate one Backup Responsible Coach before opening. Both require an active School Membership tied to the affected Competitors, the backup may act only when the primary is unavailable, and every action records which Coach acted. The outage exception requires platform-health evidence, applies only to affected Entries for the verified duration capped at 30 minutes, and retains all other late-submission guards. Strike selections, approvals, corrections, releases, and assignment provenance use the same restricted seven-year Competitive Evidence policy as other Competitor tournament records.

`AdmitEmergencyReplacementJudge` accepts an unexpected post-close withdrawal or No-Show, a reason, and a Director or Judge-and-room-authorized staff approval. It revalidates all verified conflicts, never reopens discretionary strikes, ranks previously available eligible Judges first, and cannot create an assignment without separate Tabulation Staff sign-off. Admission emits privacy-minimized in-app and email notifications to the assigned Judge, affected Competitors and Coaches, Directors, and relevant Tabulation Staff. The assignment state remains `Unconfirmed` until acknowledgment by `min(notifiedAt + 10m, roundStartsAt)`. One `EmergencyJudgeDeliveryCycle` atomically requests both electronic deliveries and is failed only if neither confirms delivery; a delivered message without acknowledgment is not failed. Staff may record a restricted manual contact attempt but cannot submit acknowledgment for the Judge. Three failed cycles transition only the assignment to `DeliveryFailed`, require staff to select another candidate, and create neither a No-Show nor a Judge Reliability Record. A later acknowledgment is rejected against the canceled assignment, and reusing the Judge requires a new assignment command. The tentative projection exposes public Judge identity, Published Judging Profile, and Unconfirmed status only to affected participants and authorized staff; the public pairing serializer excludes it. Every uncapped use requires a fresh reason and sign-off. A warning appears at the second use for one event or third tournament-wide and every later use. A verified Coach-reported conflict invalidates the assignment; a potentially disqualifying qualification or data allegation blocks start pending urgent validation without granting a new strike or veto. If an acknowledged emergency Judge does not appear, `ClassifyNoShow` applies the ordinary Judge guards and a further replacement requires a new emergency command.

Accepted participation records Independent or Judge-confirmed School-Supplied source and counts the latter toward a School obligation only after confirmation and acceptance. Pre-lock source correction is requestable by the Judge or School Judge Manager; post-lock correction requires a reasoned Director or Judge-and-room-authorized staff decision. Removing a Judge from the School count creates an immediate obligation deficit and notices; replacement is allowed until the published deadline, after which only a locked Registration Policy remedy applies. `WithdrawJudgePoolParticipation` rejects ordinary withdrawal at the published Tournament Start Time minus 96 hours. The start is an explicit tournament-timezone instant configured before Judge participation opens and may move only later afterward. `RequestSpecialCircumstanceWithdrawal` captures a category and short explanation without medical records, immediately removes operational availability, shows impact, records an authorized Excused or Unexcused decision, and emits privacy-minimized notices. An Unexcused decision creates a two-year restricted Judge Reliability Record visible to the Judge and future authorized pool reviewers without the private explanation and without automatic qualification, tier, or acceptance effect. A withdrawal touching a published assignment emits the existing schedule-revision requirements.

Judge testing is initially an external dependency: a third-party Assessment Provider administers Judge Qualification Assessments and sends results to Docket. The backend slice must ingest those results and enforce Docket's qualification, retake, tier, privacy, and assignment rules without requiring Docket to host the testing experience. See [[judge-qualification-model]] and [ADR 0013](../docs/adr/0013-import-results-from-an-external-assessment-provider.md).

Assessment persistence separates a valid `QualificationSummary`, purgeable encrypted `FullAssessmentEvidence`, a minimized `QualificationHistorySummary`, Judge-controlled current and superseded `JudgingProfileVersion` records, and tournament-scoped disclosed profile snapshots. Idempotent jobs delete full evidence at attempt completion plus two years, delete minimal history seven years after qualification expiry or supersession, and delete unused superseded profiles after two years. A profile snapshot actually disclosed for a tournament uses that tournament's seven-year Competitive Evidence clock. Quick detail keeps its earlier full-completion or follow-up-deadline deletion trigger. Every job checks an exact Legal Hold before deletion and leaves assignment provenance without raw assessment content.

Question design, score calculation, ranking, tier cut scores, and calibration are deferred to that separate testing product and are not part of the current Docket backend milestone. Docket's immediate responsibility is the versioned integration boundary and consumption of verified qualification data for tournament operations.

Registration uses a typed Entry Registration State union of Draft, Submitted, Waitlisted, Accepted, Withdrawn, and Rejected. A separate Competitive Eligibility model prevents Disqualification Rulings from being encoded as registration rejection or withdrawal. The one-active-Entry invariant treats Draft through Accepted as active and preserves Withdrawn and Rejected records and transition history.

Typed registration commands enforce a transition matrix. School Entry Managers place affiliated Competitors on Tournament Rosters, create and edit Drafts, provide the final School sign-off, submit complete Entries, update them, and perform withdrawals. Docket requires no Competitor Entry Confirmation. A Competitor may submit a private Entry Error Report but cannot independently submit, withdraw, accept, or reject the Entry. The responsible Coach must respond within 24 hours or before the applicable registration or pairing deadline, whichever comes first. Unresolved reports escalate to the School Manager and then tournament registration staff without directly mutating the Entry. Registration-authorized staff and Directors return Submitted Entries for correction and decide Submitted or Waitlisted Entries as Waitlisted, Accepted, or Rejected. Every tournament-side command requires a reason. The current invitation-page Schedule drives overlap validation and revision revalidation. Cross-Entry Compatible status locks before registration with the earlier Entry-edit-or-first-affected-pairing request deadline and a fifteen-minute-default hold limit. `CrossEntryRequest` binds one Competitor, School, complete event set, and Schedule version. Staff may prepare it; a Director verifies it and acknowledges the delay warning. Adding an event requires a new request, approval cannot exceed the hold limit, and pre-start infeasibility permits Director revocation with notices and Pairing block. Only each affected round's assigned Judge receives the round indicator among participants. Suspending the Competitor Account blocks login but never withdraws an Entry or creates a competitive outcome.

Entry Restoration is a separate typed exception. Before first-round Pairing Publication, Registration-authorized staff or a Director may return the same Withdrawn or Rejected Entry to Submitted after evidence and impact review. After publication but before any round starts, only a Director may restore a mistakenly withdrawn formerly Accepted Entry and must complete affected pairing withdrawal and republication. The aggregate rejects every post-start active restoration and every attempt to restore Accepted directly or create a duplicate replacement identity.

A runtime-validated Tournament Registration Policy supplies timezone-aware registration opening, submission, School edit, admission-decision, and waitlist-closing deadlines to the registration command guards. A Director-only Late Registration Exception authorizes one declared late command before first-round Pairing Publication without changing the policy for other Entries. Withdrawal commands remain available at all times; post-edit-deadline withdrawals produce typed Late Withdrawal impact, notice, and downstream-work requirements instead of rejection.

The Policy aggregate permits Director replacement before opening. After opening, its revision validator accepts only monotonic extensions or requirement relaxations, rejects earlier deadline instants and stricter requirements, calculates the impact on every Entry state, and produces a new immutable version plus public change and affected-School notices. First-round Pairing Publication closes the revision command permanently.

The TypeScript Admission Policy is a runtime-validated component of the Registration Policy containing capacity, optional per-School caps, and ordered objective priority groups. Its deterministic calculator sorts complete Entries by group and complete submission time, uses a retained seed for exact ties, and produces an immutable Admission Recommendation. Confirmation commands accept only the exact recommendation; arbitrary Entry selection and waitlist reordering are invalid. Withdrawal and capacity changes recalculate promotions, and waitlist closing generates a confirmable standardized capacity-rejection batch.

The TypeScript registration boundary must define a runtime-validated `CoreEntryRegistrationData` contract containing an authenticated Competitor Account reference, public display name, canonical represented School ID, event ID, grade or eligibility category, versioned School eligibility attestation, Minor Participation Authorization when applicable, and responsible Coach Membership reference. The authorization records only authorized School actor, time, School, Competitor, every declared applicable School, tournament, home-jurisdiction, and host-jurisdiction policy or legal basis, and expiration; the School attests that all are satisfied under the strictest declared requirement. An unresolved conflict blocks affiliation or participation without Docket interpreting law. It lasts one school year and requires renewal after School transfer or material policy change. Revocation blocks future Entries immediately, preserves history, and sends an active Entry to a noticed Director-reviewed eligibility or withdrawal workflow without silent withdrawal or disqualification. Google authentication is never consent. Date of birth, home address, a minor's personal email or phone, guardian information, medical information, guardian documents, and accommodation details are not members of this core contract. Separate typed restricted and public projections prevent serialization drift: the public Entry projection may expose only an explicitly published Accepted Entry's display name, School, and event, while restricted registration fields remain behind Competitor, School-scoped, and operational authorization.

A typed `ClassifyNoShow` command defaults the grace period to ten minutes after the current published round start. A Director may configure a different per-event duration before Schedule Selection and publish it, but post-publication comparison rejects shortening. The command requires an in-app notice, email, and documented operational attempt through the responsible Coach for a Competitor or the Judge's restricted tournament contact method for a Judge without requiring or exposing a minor's phone. Only Judge-and-room-authorized Tabulation Staff may classify. A Competitor may submit a private `NoShowDispute` directly until `min(classifiedAt + 60m, nextEventRoundStartedAt)`. The command associates and notifies the responsible Coach and permits a statement but rejects Coach withdrawal, suppression, or blocking. `WithdrawNoShowDispute` authorizes only the filing Competitor before decision, requires explicit confirmation, appends an audit event, leaves the No-Show operative, and makes withdrawal final for that classification. Runtime validation accepts check-in, arrival, message, staff-contact, delivery-log, or incorrect published time or room evidence and never requires medical records; an absence explanation affects consequence rather than factual classification. The Director decision is due at `min(disputeFiledAt + 30m, nextEventRoundStartedAt)`. Current classification and replacement state remain operative during review, and an overdue job escalates without automatic approval or denial. A rejection is final for the tournament; only the existing objective system- or data-error correction command may change it, never a second merits decision. The private result projection includes the general reason category, Ruleset effect, deciding authority, and time but excludes internal notes and third-party restricted data. Its in-app and email delivery is tracked but requires no acknowledgment, remains readable in the Competitor Account, and cannot reopen the dispute or delay operations after failure. The command creates no automatic penalty and defers consequences to the locked Ruleset.

A deterministic selector may then produce every eligible candidate in a Replacement Candidate Pool plus a separate recommendation ranking. Judge ranking uses qualification, conflict and strike constraints, availability, competitive-round fit, workload, and disruption. Competitor filtering requires an authenticated Account, complete authorization and eligibility, the same School and event, no conflicting assignment, and Ruleset permission before the Entry's first round or an express mid-tournament substitution rule. The projection is nonoperative: Docket cannot insert a Judge or Competitor automatically. Before sign-off, Tabulation Staff may withdraw the classification and restore a late-arriving original assignee. After replacement or bye approval, reversal requires a Director correction; Started and Completed rounds reject restoration. Tabulation Staff sign-off invokes the applicable command, which creates and validates a new version, preserves the original, sends notices to every affected Judge, Competitor, Coach, and authorized staff actor, and publishes a Correction Notice if public information changed. A successful dispute appends a reasoned reversal, removes the current restricted No-Show flag, regenerates every not-yet-started dependent artifact, and keeps the original classification only in restricted audit history. Started and Completed pairings remain as operated under the locked Ruleset's correction treatment. If published standings changed, Docket recalculates from authoritative sources, invalidates affected approvals, republishes an immutable snapshot, and surfaces downstream pairing, advancement, award, and Final Results conflicts. Public serializers use only “No-Show classification corrected,” previous and corrected public values, and competitive effect; they exclude absence detail, evidence, internal reasoning, and restricted actor metadata. A timely unresolved dispute blocks the affected event's Final Results readiness and is a hard Tournament Closure blocker. When no eligible Competitor exists, the opponent receives a Ruleset-governed Bye Round. A Competitor No-Show uses the tournament's restricted competitive-evidence retention and audience only and cannot populate a cross-tournament score or public history. An Unexcused Judge No-Show creates the same restricted two-year Judge Reliability Record as an Unexcused Special Circumstance Withdrawal without automatically changing qualification.

Additional fields use a runtime-validated, declarative `RegistrationDataSchema` referenced by exact version from the Tournament Registration Policy. Each field definition carries a stable ID, purpose, required flag, supported value type, validation contract, audience, retention or disposition rule, and public-visibility classification. The first slice accepts only restricted visibility for additional fields. The validator rejects medical or accommodation content, missing governance declarations, executable validation, and post-opening revisions other than field removal, collection cessation, or required-to-optional changes. Stored responses retain their governing Schema version; the future TypeScript UI must render and submit the same contract rather than inventing UI-only questions.

Accommodation support uses a separate restricted TypeScript aggregate rather than an optional property on `CoreEntryRegistrationData`. Its authorization policy distinguishes the complete `AccommodationRequestRestricted` projection from the minimized `AccommodationImplementationInstruction` delivered to a specific operational assignment. The request boundary omits diagnosis by default, encrypts and audits sensitive access at the persistence and service layers, and prevents general tournament permissions from satisfying the Accommodation Operations guard. Instruction generation must be attributed and must exclude supporting evidence and unrelated narrative before delivery to Tabulation Staff, room staff, or Judges.

The aggregate uses a discriminated `AccommodationRequestState` union of Draft, Submitted, ClarificationNeeded, Approved, PartiallyApproved, Denied, Withdrawn, and Closed with command-specific transition guards. Submitted content and all decisions are immutable versions. Staff approval validates exact fulfillment; partial approval, denial, or any reduction of a prior approval validates Director authority, a specific reason, and any available alternative. A committed decision change atomically revokes superseded instructions, creates the new minimized instruction set, and enqueues private recipient notifications so an old and new instruction cannot both appear current.

The runtime-validated Registration Policy carries an `accommodationRequestBy` instant defaulted to `entrySubmissionDeadline`; its post-opening comparison permits only extension. Request submission never guards on that instant. Instead, Docket derives a `LateAccommodationRequest` provenance flag and continues through the same state machine. Scheduling and pairing modules consume only an opaque active accommodation-constraint projection, not the restricted request. A late decision invalidates affected unpublished artifacts or opens the existing revision and withdrawal workflow for published unstarted artifacts; started and completed round identities and competitive records remain immutable.

Accommodation storage separates purgeable encrypted content from the durable content-free `AccommodationAuditStub`. Withdrawal completion and Tournament Closure revoke active instructions and drive requests to Closed. Closure schedules purgeable content for deletion at `tournamentClosedAt + 30 days`; a valid own-School early-deletion command advances that time after Tournament Closure. Only a separately authorized Legal Hold record can suspend the purge worker. The idempotent deletion transaction removes request, communication, evidence, decision-reason, alternative, and instruction content, then records only the deletion event in the audit stub so APIs and exports cannot reconstruct the removed material.

A separate idempotent retention job deletes `AccommodationAuditStub` at `tournamentClosedAt + 1 year` unless an active Legal Hold suspends it. The stub has no public, ordinary-tournament, or School-export serializer. Its deletion transaction is isolated from public results, pairing, standings, award, and Correction Notice stores so accommodation retention cannot remove or rewrite independent tournament history.

`LegalHold` is a platform aggregate outside tournament administration. Its activation, scope expansion, extension, and release commands require two different actor IDs, both carrying Legal and Privacy Operations permission, and an immutable prepared proposal followed by approval. Runtime validation requires legal basis, external case reference, exact record selectors, start instant, and `reviewBy` no more than 90 days from activation or prior review. Overdue review emits persistent escalation but never changes Active status. Release atomically clears the retention guard and enqueues every due purge idempotently. Public and tournament serializers omit the aggregate; the affected-School projection exposes only `deletionPausedByLegalHold: boolean`.

A typed `TournamentRetentionClass` separates `PermanentPublicRecord`, `SevenYearCompetitiveEvidence`, and `TwoYearOperationalTelemetry`. Tournament Closure emits immutable retention anchors and idempotent jobs compute deletion from `tournamentClosedAt`, checking exact Legal Hold record selectors before purge. The classification validator makes competitive-evidence precedence explicit, while accommodation and Judge Assessment aggregates opt into their accepted specific policies. Storage and deletion boundaries isolate permanent public projections from restricted source records so expiry cannot cascade into public result loss. Tests must cover classification, boundary instants, repeated purge execution, active and released holds, and proof that deleted payloads are absent from APIs and exports.

Production Competitor identity is an authenticated Docket Account under ADR 0030, so the earlier destination-School record creation and record-reassignment commands are removed from the current design. Entries reference the stable Competitor Account and separately preserve the represented School. A non-production test adapter accepts only a versioned runtime-validated JSON Competitor Test Fixture with a synthetic fixture ID, display name, School reference, and test-required eligibility fields. It rejects real emails, Google subjects, contact details, and authority fields; production deployments reject the fixture command entirely. Competitor School Transfer keeps the same Account and replaces its one current affiliation. Profile settings and personal tournament history follow the Account; historical represented-School attribution remains; destination access is limited to current affiliation and future-Entry needs; and former-School private data never transfers. A historical `CorrectEntrySchoolAttribution` case still joins Platform-validated identity evidence to Director-controlled correction and republication without copying cross-School private data.

Platform support uses a closed `SupportActionCommand` union: `InspectRedactedDiagnostics`, `ResendExistingMessage`, `ReplayIdempotentJob`, `ReplayVerifiedProviderDelivery`, `RebuildDerivedCache`, `RebuildSearchIndex`, `ClearExpiredProcessingLock`, and `RegenerateDerivedProjection`. No free-form command, patch payload, arbitrary record selector, or administrator-authored source value is accepted. State-changing commands runtime-validate a ticket, reason, scope, preview fingerprint, Affected Owner, original guard result, and notification plan. Execution is idempotent where applicable and records success or failure. Tests must prove that each command cannot mutate authoritative source versions, bypass validation, cross audience boundaries, publish, or act under another identity.

## Implementation language

Docket will ultimately be implemented in **TypeScript** across the backend and participant-facing application. Current backend models, interfaces, validation contracts, calculation modules, tests, and administrative tooling should therefore be designed for direct TypeScript implementation rather than as language-neutral pseudocode that must later be translated.

TypeScript's static types do not replace runtime validation. Every external or stored boundary—including `.docket-standings.json`, Assessment Provider messages, uploaded result files, API requests, and persisted version payloads—must be validated at runtime before becoming domain data. Deterministic tournament calculations should use typed, side-effect-controlled modules with explicit inputs, version identifiers, and reproducible outputs. Framework, runtime, database, deployment, and monorepo choices remain unresolved.

See [ADR 0016](../docs/adr/0016-use-typescript-across-docket.md).

## Acceptance boundary

The milestone is complete when the chosen debate format can run through the lifecycle using tested backend interfaces, with enough administrative controls to recover from realistic operator mistakes. A polished participant-facing UI is outside this milestone; minimal developer or administrative tooling may be used to exercise the backend.

## First supported format

The first end-to-end slice will support **Lincoln-Douglas Debate**. Its one-person entries reduce initial team-management complexity while still exercising affirmative and negative sides, pairings, judges, ballots, standings, and elimination rounds. Preliminary standings use a Tournament Director-uploaded configuration described in [[standings-model]].

Every Tournament Schedule is created and versioned natively in Docket. As elimination inputs become available, a deterministic service fills the corresponding native round block with bracket Competitors and eligible Judge recommendations and emits a nonoperative `PendingOutRoundScheduleReview`. Pairing-authorized Tabulation Staff may confirm, deny, or edit the proposal; edits create new versions and rerun validation. For a live native schedule edit, Pairing-authorized Tabulation Staff prepare the exact version and impact preview, the Tournament Director approves it, and the Director or Publication-authorized staff publishes it. Typed critical reasons include Judge No-Show, Competitor No-Show, conflict, disqualification, room change, and `OtherTournamentCriticalOperation`; the last requires a specific explanation and impact statement. A noncritical live edit remains technically possible but displays a stronger warning and requires explicit confirmation that the edit is necessary. Every edit preserves version, approval, publication, and notice controls.

Critical schedule notices target affected Judges, Competitors, responsible Coaches, Directors, and relevant Tabulation Staff. A failed delivery does not undo publication: the delivery service runs three in-app-and-email cycles, escalates continued failure, and permits recorded restricted operational contact without surrogate acknowledgment. A changed Judge must acknowledge within ten minutes after successful delivery and before the revised start, using the earlier deadline; failure activates the Judge Continuity Plan and replacement workflow. Reversal is a new `ScheduleReversalRevision` that preserves the intervening version and may be approved by any current Director before the affected round starts.

Each configured event exposes an Event Workspace for its complete round sequence, locations, assignments, and role-scoped activity. Staff receive complete operations, Competitors and Coaches receive their own or School-scoped views, assigned Judges receive assigned-round views, and Public Viewers receive only public schedules, Pairings, and results. Status projections include check-in, assignment, room, Judge acknowledgment, and scheduled, started, completed, or result-pending state without exposing private messages, detailed Ballots, strikes, conflicts, assessment data, or staff notes. Practice is a separate tournament-independent UI action. A private, undiscoverable Practice Workspace accepts any exact-email Google-authenticated Docket Account recipient through an expiring single-session invitation without School approval, affiliation, or tournament authority. Resend preserves recipient, token, and expiry; mistargeting requires revocation and replacement. The creating Entry Manager owns the session with same-School Entry Manager transfer only. Organizer, Competitor, Judge, and Observer roles enforce least privilege; pre-start authority increases require participant acceptance, and all roles become immutable at session start. Reasoned removal is immediate with a bounded own-material export; irreversible closure or cancellation prevents new activity; copying carries configuration only. Ordinary Judge identity and profile remain public while specific session participation and feedback remain nonpublic. Format rules supply Competitor structure; owner-set Judge and Observer limits remain inside a hard one-hundred-Account platform maximum. Practice timing never interacts with official schedules or conflict warnings. Defined notices are informational except invitation acceptance, and absences create no No-Show or reliability history; the same-role live replacement path remains open. Mock activity is nonoperative, unranked, excluded from permanent performance history, and deleted no later than thirty days after creation. Platform safety staff receive complete evidence and decide restricted reports, may impose practice-specific protections while broader suspension follows existing governance, protect authenticated reporter identity, allow one fourteen-day reconsideration by a different reviewer, append objective corrections, honor exact Legal Holds, and retain minimized safety evidence for two years after resolution. For online debate or speech practice, Docket creates a Google Meet link and delivers it to assigned Competitors and Judges without recording or importing audio or video. A Personal Tournament Agenda composes already-authorized assignments as a read-only view, highlights conflicts, and sends informational in-app and email change alerts without broadening permission or acknowledgment requirements.

Cross-entry execution activates a typed hold automatically at the affected round's current published start. Only a pre-start published Schedule revision may move the start; Judge or Coach extension commands are invalid. Timely arrival clears the hold and records actual start. Expiry transitions directly to the ordinary Competitor No-Show workflow without stacking another grace period.

The backend contract for that upload is a versioned, declarative `.docket-standings.json` file. The first slice must validate it against Docket's supported schema and generate explainable sample calculations before acceptance; uploaded code, formulas, macros, PDFs, and spreadsheets are not standings execution inputs. The future UI will generate the same contract.

The configuration may include optional Director-provided Standings Acceptance Cases using synthetic inputs and expected ordering and metrics. Every supplied case must pass before acceptance and rerun after schema migration or emergency correction. The TypeScript implementation uses shared types for developer ergonomics while preserving runtime schema validation at the upload boundary.

A Tournament Director accepts one exact validated configuration after reviewing its preview. Docket then publishes its human-readable summary without internal metadata on the Tournament Invitation Page and locks the internal version at first-round Pairing Publication. The backend must reject standings calculations and later power-pairing inputs that do not identify the locked fingerprint internally.

After locking, the backend permits an Emergency Standings Rules Correction only to repair verified divergence from the already published policy. It requires Director approval of a validated replacement plus calculation and full impact previews, recalculates standings from preserved results, regenerates only mutable downstream artifacts, and surfaces correction notices and conflicts without rewriting started pairings.

Advancement is a separate typed domain module. A versioned Advancement Plan references the locked standings fingerprint and defines the elimination break size, eligibility, tie-at-the-cut rule, seeding, and bracket byes. Applying it to a standings snapshot produces the reproducible Advancement Field consumed by elimination pairing; it never changes how standings are calculated.

The Plan uses a versioned declarative `.docket-advancement.json` contract. Docket validates it at runtime and produces an explainable sample preview before Director acceptance. The future TypeScript UI generates the same file. Docket publishes the accepted Plan's human-readable summary without internal metadata before the first round, then first-round Pairing Publication atomically locks compatible standings and advancement fingerprints internally.

The Advancement Plan chooses one explicit tie-at-the-cut variant: expand the full tied field, use pre-scheduled elimination play-ins, or use a deterministic draw whose seed and algorithm version were published before the first round. Validation proves that the chosen policy is feasible and the TypeScript domain model represents the variants as a closed discriminated union; operators cannot select a preferred tied Entry after standings are known.

The Director may choose any integer break size from 2 through the eligible field. The advancement module maps the actual selected field to the next power-of-two bracket, gives byes to the highest seeds, and uses a versioned canonical high-versus-low seed map without matchup-driven rearrangement. Bracket math uses deterministic integer operations, and byes remain attributed advancement artifacts rather than fabricated Ballots.

Before any elimination round starts, an eligibility change deterministically recalculates and versions the full Advancement Field, promotes the next-highest eligible Entries, reseeds, or shrinks the field and recalculates byes. After any elimination round starts, the backend prohibits all replacement and reseeding and resolves only the vacated bracket line through an attributed bye, forfeit, or disqualification outcome.

An Emergency Advancement Plan Correction repairs only verified divergence from the publicly disclosed Plan. It requires Director confirmation of a validated replacement, Plan preview, full impact preview, conflicts, and notices. Before elimination starts it can recalculate and republish the Field and pairings; afterward it becomes a versioned correction record while the original bracket and its source references remain operative and immutable.

Pairing-authorized staff may prepare the deterministic Advancement Field, but a Tournament Director approves its exact validated fingerprint. The Director or Publication-authorized staff then publishes only that approved version. Source changes invalidate approval, recalculation repeats the workflow, and no elimination pairing can become operative before exact Field publication. Before Tournament Schedule Publication, the Director selects the tournament-scoped permission bundle that may provide the second out-round confirmation; the selection is not a named-person assignment, locks when the first tournament round starts, and may later be corrected only by the Director for prospective staffing access without rewriting prior confirmations. The standard `CompleteOutRoundVerification` command requires distinct actor IDs for a Pairing-authorized Tabulation Staff verifier and an active holder of the configured permission. If the intended confirmer loses permission before acting, the command discards the unfinished confirmation but preserves first-verifier work while the exact source versions remain unchanged. When no eligible second confirmer is reasonably available, a Director may activate a typed Single-Person Out-Round Verification Exception for one exact round and schedule version. The verifier must hold Pairing authority, need not hold the unavailable confirmation permission, and may be a Director who also has Pairing authority. The command requires the activation reason, availability check, source versions, warning acknowledgment, actor, and time and projects them only to restricted staff and audit views. The gate checks the prior result or Administrative Outcome, Ballot or panel majority, disputes, corrections, disqualifications, Entry identity and School, seed, bracket position, sides, opponent, Judge qualification and conflicts, room, time, eligibility, operative Advancement Field, and bracket lineage. Relevant source changes mark it stale and require a new exception if separation remains unavailable; a non-overridden pre-start check may use another freshly warned exception, but an overridden gate always requires two distinct actors.

`OverrideOutRoundGate` is available only to the Tournament Director together with a second distinct active holder of the configured confirmation permission. It must show a prominent competitive-risk warning and persist the failed checks, impact preview, specific reason, affected round and Entries, governing source versions, both actors, and timestamp. The override can address operational uncertainty or incomplete verification only; validation rejects attempts to override a verified conflict, eligibility failure, Judge qualification failure, disqualification, governing Ruleset, bracket identity, or another hard integrity constraint. Override details are restricted to authorized staff and audit projections; participant-facing views expose only the ordinary pairing and any later Correction Notice. The pre-start command retains the overridden state and requires two distinct authorized actors to confirm the exact unchanged version; any new hard failure blocks start. A restricted risk flag remains until round completion and downstream review, and the complete record remains Seven-Year Competitive Evidence. A no-effect finding closes the warning without public notice unless public data changed. A pre-start error invalidates the pairing, returns it to the full first verification, and requires authoritative correction, regeneration, approval, and republication. Starting without a completed gate preserves the round, creates a high-priority Downstream Conflict, and limits correction to unstarted downstream artifacts.

Field publication exposes a public Advancement Field Summary with qualifiers, Schools, seeds, byes, and tie outcome but no raw versions, fingerprints, publishing attribution, or exact timestamp. Each School receives a separate authenticated projection for its own excluded Entries' calculations and scoped eligibility explanations. The TypeScript backend constructs and validates public, School, and staff data-transfer projections explicitly; privacy cannot depend on hiding fields in the UI.

The standings configuration includes a closed TypeScript Standings Publication Policy variant: after every preliminary round, after specified round checkpoints, or after all preliminaries. The invitation page discloses and first-round publication locks the choice. Every release waits for all standings-required results and blocking conflicts at that checkpoint and creates an immutable version, while authorized power pairing may use a newer internal standings snapshot before participant disclosure.

At an eligible checkpoint, Docket automatically calculates and validates a draft but never publishes automatically. A Director or Pairing-authorized staff member approves the exact fingerprint; a Director or Publication-authorized staff member publishes that version. The same actor may do both with both permissions, while the TypeScript backend records separate validation, approval, and publication events and rejects direct metric or rank mutation.

Published standings expose public Entry identity, School, rank, record, and every aggregate metric that affected ordering so no public rank depends on a hidden metric, but no raw versions, fingerprints, publishing attribution, or exact timestamp. A School receives a separate per-round calculation breakdown for its own Entries. Judge-linked scores, Ballots, feedback, eligibility and correction evidence, and assessment data remain restricted. TypeScript response schemas explicitly separate public, School, operational-provenance, and sensitive-evidence projections.

Final Results Publication requires a validated draft with every placement- and award-affecting result, correction, disqualification, conflict, and calculation resolved. A Director approves the exact fingerprint, then the Director or Publication-authorized staff publishes it. Feedback and outcome-irrelevant panel Ballots do not block readiness, source-dependent scoring inputs cannot be fabricated, and later corrections create new versions and notices.

Final Results Publication transitions the tournament to Competitive Completion rather than closing it. Only the Tournament Owner may later perform Tournament Closure after the applicable Feedback Deadline and required operational work are complete or resolved. The configurable deadline is published and locked before the first round, defaults to 72 hours after the final scheduled round, and may later be extended but never shortened by a Director. Closure waits for expiration but not universal feedback submission. It ends routine operation while preserving public pages, immutable history, and authorized versioned correction workflows.

The TypeScript closure command consumes a runtime-validated Closure Readiness Review. It rejects closure for unpublished Final Results, an open round, an unexpired Feedback Deadline, a timely filed unresolved No-Show Dispute, or a result-affecting unresolved correction, conflict, or unpublished newer result version. Outcome-irrelevant missing panel Ballots, missing or unpublished feedback, unacknowledged notices, and incomplete optional exports or notes are warnings the Owner must complete or explicitly acknowledge with a reason.

Closed-tournament command guards preserve public pages, permission-scoped historical queries, audit access, and exports while rejecting ordinary registration, staffing, configuration, schedule, pairing, round, Ballot, feedback, standings, advancement, award, and result writes. The command layer admits only typed, runtime-validated Post-Closure Exceptions for authorized corrections and republication, Feedback Reopening, Ownership Recovery, and legally required privacy actions under their existing permissions.

The TypeScript aggregate keeps `TournamentState` as Closed while a separate scoped Post-Closure Correction Case progresses. Its command allowlist is the intersection of existing actor authority and case scope. The Tournament Director initiates and approves an objective No-Show correction after Closure; a Platform Administrator may validate system evidence but cannot decide the case or change the result. Qualifying evidence is limited to objective delivery logs, check-in records, immutable timestamps, a proven published time or room error, or a proven data-entry defect. New merits explanations do not qualify. The command guard calculates one immutable deadline exactly 168 hours from the Closure instant, displays it in the tournament timezone, and requires approval and republication before it. A rejected case records a reason, sends private Competitor and responsible-Coach notices, remains restricted Seven-Year Competitive Evidence, and cannot be appealed or resubmitted on the same evidence; materially different objective evidence may support another case only within the window. At the deadline, an unresolved case becomes `ExpiredCorrectionWindowEnded`, rejects further edit or republication, sends private notices, remains retained for seven years, and leaves current public results unchanged. The case cannot reopen merits review or ordinary tournament operations. Case resolution, rejection, withdrawal, or expiry closes only the case and never performs a second Tournament Closure transition.

Awards use a separate versioned `.docket-awards.json` Award Plan. Docket validates and previews it, the future TypeScript UI generates it, and a Director accepts it before first-round Pairing Publication atomically locks it with standings and advancement. A deterministic award module automatically consumes exact authoritative source versions and regenerates the Award Results Draft used by Final Results without duplicating source calculations. The exact Award Results Draft is approved and published only as part of the Final Results Draft, with no separate award gate. The first Lincoln-Douglas release supports configurable elimination placement awards and preliminary Speaker Awards, allows either family to be disabled, and defers sweepstakes and subjective awards until multi-event support.

After locking, only a Director may approve an Emergency Award Plan Correction, and only to make the Plan conform to award rules published before the tournament. Docket rejects outcome-driven policy changes, validates the replacement, produces Plan and full award impact previews, preserves both versions, regenerates affected awards, invalidates prior Final Results Approval, and requires any applicable public Correction Notice.

The public results projection contains champions, finalists, configured placements, the complete elimination bracket and decisions, final standings, every configured award and recipient, represented Schools, and human-readable Correction Notices. It excludes detailed Ballots, feedback, Judge-linked scores, private eligibility and disqualification evidence, assessment data, governing configuration fingerprints and versions, publishing attribution, exact timestamps, correction evidence, internal notes, and audit details. Authorized staff and audit projections retain the full records.

## Governing rules

Each tournament will adopt an immutable, versioned **Ruleset** modeled from the applicable NSDA High School Unified Manual. Allowed **Tournament Overrides** will be explicit, validated, and auditable; publishing a later Ruleset must not silently change an existing tournament.

Overrides use a strict allowlist. Initial override categories are preliminary-round count, elimination break size, a versioned Pairing Plan containing supported per-round methods and options, judge-panel size, and preliminary-round speaker-point scale. The Pairing Plan may combine methods across the configured round sequence, but the defining Lincoln-Douglas structure, the prohibition on Elimination Round Speaker Points, and Docket's ballot-integrity, authorization, security, and audit rules cannot be overridden by a tournament. See [[pairing-model]].

The first-release Pairing Method Catalog contains Random Draw, Preset, Seeded High-Low, Power High-Low, Power High-High, Round-Robin, Seeded Elimination Bracket, and Fully Manual Pairing. Docket versions and validates every method and does not run tournament-supplied custom pairing code in the initial slice.

The adopted Ruleset and Tournament Overrides generally lock at the first round's **Pairing Publication**. Pairing configuration is the stricter exception: Schedule Selection locks every configured round's method and method-defining options. Only a Tournament Director may replace that policy through a fully audited Pre-Tournament Emergency Pairing Correction before any round starts; no policy change is allowed afterward. Other later changes require an **Emergency Amendment** with a reason, an impact preview, explicit confirmation, and a permanent audit record. An Emergency Amendment must not silently rewrite a completed round.

New tournaments default to the latest verified Ruleset. Existing tournaments remain pinned to their adopted version. Before first-round Pairing Publication, an authorized administrator may perform an explicit **Ruleset Migration** after reviewing a version diff and validation report. A locked tournament cannot migrate Rulesets and must use Emergency Amendments instead.

See [ADR 0001](../docs/adr/0001-versioned-rulesets-with-audited-overrides.md) for the architectural consequences. The exact initial NSDA manual version remains to be confirmed from an authoritative current source.
