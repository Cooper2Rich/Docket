# Scheduling Model

## Pre-tournament schedule generation

Before the tournament begins, Docket produces three separate feasible **Schedule Candidates**. Each candidate is optimized for a different objective:

1. **Judge-Use Objective** — place the greatest possible number of correctly tiered Judges into the appropriate and most competitive rounds according to [[judge-qualification-model]].
2. **Buffer-Time Objective** — maximize the smallest recovery interval between consecutive round blocks, then distribute remaining recovery time as evenly as possible within the tournament's configured start and end times.
3. **Time-Efficiency Objective** — minimize the tournament's total elapsed duration and produce the earliest feasible finish.

Selecting one Schedule Candidate establishes it as the **Tournament Schedule**. Docket does not combine the three candidates or silently change the selected schedule.

Docket creates every Tournament Schedule natively and remains the authoritative source for its round blocks, times, rooms, breaks, and resource assumptions. Tournament staff may edit only through versioned Docket workflows; the operating model does not require an externally authored schedule import.

### Schedule Selection

Tabulation Staff with the Pairing operations permission bundle may compare the candidates, inspect their objective metrics and constraint tradeoffs, and prepare a recommendation. Only a Tournament Director may approve **Schedule Selection** and establish the Tournament Schedule.

Schedule Selection records the selected candidate and version, all displayed objective metrics, the approving Director, the staff recommendation when one exists, the Director's reason, and the selection time. Docket retains all three candidates and their inputs so the decision can be audited and reproduced; unselected candidates are not deleted or blended into the selected schedule.

Schedule Selection also locks the Pairing Plan's method and method-defining options for every configured round. Every later Schedule Revision Proposal must preserve that policy while adjusting only permitted schedule or live pairing inputs. Before any round starts, a Director-approved Pre-Tournament Emergency Pairing Correction may establish a new locked policy version; once a round starts, a proposal that changes a method or locked option is invalid and cannot be approved or published.

All candidates must satisfy the same mandatory eligibility, conflict, availability, room, Round Specification, Pairing Plan, round-order, and Ruleset constraints. Each candidate maps every configured pairing round to one schedule block. Optimization ranks feasible schedules; it does not make an ineligible Judge or impossible assignment valid. See [[pairing-model]].

The current Tournament Schedule published through the Tournament Invitation Page is authoritative for Cross-Entry Schedule Conflict validation, and every revision revalidates approvals. Docket ordinarily rejects positive overlap between event intervals; adjacent intervals are allowed and Scheduled Breaks are not participation. A Director may publish and lock Cross-Entry Compatible status only before registration opens, together with a request deadline at the earlier Entry-edit or first-affected-pairing boundary and a Cross-Entry Hold Limit defaulting to fifteen minutes. The responsible Coach submits one exact Competitor, School, event-set, and Schedule-version request. Tournament staff may prepare it, but the Director need only verify it and acknowledge the schedule-delay warning. Adding an event requires a new request. Approval cannot exceed the hold limit and may be revoked before either affected round starts when infeasible, with immediate notice and affected-Pairing block until resolved. Only the assigned Judge for each affected round sees the cross-entry indicator and hold time among round participants; it is not public or opponent-facing, while requesting parties and authorized staff retain their scoped views.

For an affected round, Docket automatically activates the Cross-Entry Hold at that round's current published start and displays the countdown to the assigned Judge and authorized staff. A published Schedule revision made before the round begins moves that start; neither the Judge nor the Coach can extend the hold. Arrival before expiry clears the hold successfully, records the actual start, and begins the round while Docket monitors resulting downstream delay without silently revising later times. If the Competitor remains absent at expiry, the hold ends and Docket immediately enters the ordinary Competitor No-Show workflow with no additional grace period because the hold replaces, rather than stacks with, No-Show grace.

As elimination inputs become known, Docket fills each out-round block from the current native Tournament Schedule, Advancement Field, bracket, and eligible Judge pool as a nonoperative Pending Out-Round Schedule Review. Pairing-authorized Tabulation Staff may confirm, deny, or edit its proposed Competitors and Judges before the Out-Round Verification Gate and Pairing Publication; the gate normally uses two people but supports its separately governed warned single-person exception. Auto-population never assigns or publishes a round by itself.

Only the Competitor's Coach may submit a verified Judge Conflict for that Competitor or Entry. Docket treats it as a hard assignment constraint after ordinary validation. A Tournament Director may optionally enable a Judge Striking Policy per event and must publish it before registration opens. If disabled, only verified Judge Conflicts apply. One portal window governs the full tournament unless a pre-registration policy deliberately defines separate event sessions. The native Judge Strike Portal opens 36 hours before Tournament Start Time and closes at 3:00 p.m. in the tournament's official named timezone on the calendar day before the tournament. Tournament time controls across daylight-saving transitions, while Docket also displays each user's local equivalent. Each Entry has one Primary Responsible Coach and may predesignate a Backup Responsible Coach before opening; both require an active School Membership tied to the affected Competitors, and the backup acts only when the primary is unavailable. Each individual Competitor or the Competitors in a team Entry initially choose the Entry's strikes. If teammates disagree, the acting responsible Coach resolves the selection. Docket records approval transactionally and returns an in-app receipt, with no email delivery to the Tournament Director or external host. The policy supports an equal configured number of hard strikes per Entry plus ranked preferences: hard strikes prevent assignment, ranked preferences influence optimization without guaranteeing exclusion, and verified conflicts remain separate uncapped hard constraints. Before closing, changes require fresh Coach approval. Missing approval leaves the choices nonoperative after reminders six and one hours before closing. Ordinary workflow does not add Judges after closing and has no supplemental strike path. A late exception requires platform-health evidence that the affected Entry could not submit or obtain approval, a recovery window equal to the verified outage duration capped at 30 minutes, Director approval, and no known opponent or pairing. If conflicts and hard strikes leave no qualified Judge, Docket blocks publication and allows staff to add an eligible Judge or ask the acting responsible Coach to release a strike after reviewing the warning with the Competitors; the release is final for the tournament, and staff cannot select it. Docket never silently overrides either constraint. Selections, approvals, corrections, releases, and assignment provenance use the same restricted seven-year Competitive Evidence period as other Competitor tournament records, with no special extension. They remain visible only to the submitting Competitors and responsible Coach and authorized tournament or audit staff; Judges and opposing Schools cannot see them, though anonymized aggregates may support operations. The policy locks with the Pairing Plan and may change only through the existing Director-controlled pre-tournament emergency correction.

An unexpected post-close withdrawal or No-Show may produce an Emergency Replacement Judge only after a reasoned Tournament Director or Judge-and-room-authorized Tabulation Staff approval. Docket revalidates verified conflicts, does not reopen discretionary strikes, prefers eligible Judges who were available during the strike process, and requires assignment-specific Tabulation Staff sign-off before using the emergency Judge. Docket sends an immediate generic operational-replacement notice in-app and by email to the Judge, affected Competitors and Coaches, Directors, and relevant Tabulation Staff without private withdrawal or No-Show evidence. The Judge must acknowledge within ten minutes and always before start, using the remaining time when less than ten minutes remains. The assignment stays unconfirmed until then. Each timestamped delivery cycle sends both electronic notices and fails only when neither confirms delivery; delivery without acknowledgment is not failure. Staff may record use of the restricted operational contact method but cannot acknowledge on the Judge's behalf. After three failed cycles Docket marks only the assignment Delivery Failed, creates no No-Show or Judge Reliability Record, and requires another Judge. A late acknowledgment cannot revive it, though the Judge may be selected again through a new governed assignment. Tentative identity and Published Judging Profile remain limited to affected parties and staff until acknowledgment and sign-off. Every use needs a fresh reason and sign-off, with no numeric cap; the second use in one event or third tournament-wide and every later use raises a Director staffing warning. An acknowledged emergency Judge who fails to appear follows the ordinary Judge No-Show process, and another replacement requires a new emergency decision. Before start, an affected Coach may report a verified conflict, qualification error, or assignment-data error for validation but cannot exercise a new strike or veto; a potentially invalid assignment cannot start until validation resolves it.

Only accepted Judge Pool Participation contributes an available Judge to schedule generation. Interest alone is excluded, and the pool has no automatic capacity limit or waitlist. Independent versus Judge-confirmed School-Supplied source affects applicable obligation and staffing calculations but never raises qualification. The School-Supplied source counts toward an obligation only after Judge confirmation and tournament acceptance; a post-lock correction requires a Director or Judge-and-room-authorized staff decision with a reason. Removal from a School's count immediately creates an obligation deficit and private notices; replacement is accepted until the published deadline, after which only a locked Registration Policy remedy may apply. An ordinary withdrawal before the Judge Withdrawal Lock Time removes the Judge. A Special Circumstance request after it marks the Judge immediately unavailable, presents an impact preview, and removes the Judge prospectively while forcing the applicable schedule impact and revision workflow for any published future assignment.

When the Advancement Plan selects the Play-In Tie-at-the-Cut Policy, every Schedule Candidate must reserve the configured contingency round blocks, rooms, and qualified Judge capacity needed to run the maximum supported play-in field. Those blocks and their activation conditions appear on the Tournament Invitation Page. If no play-in is needed, Docket treats the unused block according to the published contingency schedule; it cannot silently remove time that later rounds depend on.

An active Judge Assignment Hold is a hard tournament-local availability constraint: Docket excludes that Judge from every new or revised assignment until the missing Competitive Result releases the hold or a Tournament Director clears it. Removing an already published future assignment follows the normal Schedule Revision Approval, publication, and notice controls. The hold has no effect on qualification or on scheduling for another tournament.

The No-Show grace period defaults to ten minutes after the current published round start for Judges and Competitors. A Tournament Director may configure a different per-event period before Schedule Selection and publish it on the invitation page, but cannot shorten it after publication. Before classification, Docket requires an in-app notice, email, and a documented operational attempt through the responsible Coach for a Competitor or the Judge's restricted tournament contact method for a Judge; it never requires or exposes a minor's phone number. Judge-and-room-authorized Tabulation Staff classify the No-Show after verifying those guards. A Competitor may file a private No-Show Dispute directly in Docket within 60 minutes or before that event's next round starts, whichever occurs first. Docket associates and notifies the responsible Coach, who may add a statement but cannot block, withdraw, or suppress the dispute. Only the filing Competitor may withdraw before decision; Docket requires explicit confirmation, audits the withdrawal, makes it final for that classification, and leaves the classification operative. Supported evidence includes check-in or arrival records, message timestamps, staff-contact records, delivery logs, and proof that the published time or room was wrong; medical records are never required, and an explanation for absence affects consequence rather than proving misclassification. The classification and replacement remain operative during review. The Director decides within 30 minutes of filing or before the next event round starts, whichever is first; an overdue review escalates while the current resolution stays operative and is neither automatically approved nor denied. Rejection is final for the tournament except for an objective system or data-entry correction under the existing correction workflow. The Competitor and Coach receive a privacy-minimized in-app and email notice of decision, general reason category, Ruleset effect, authority, and time. Delivery is tracked but informational: acknowledgment is not required, failure does not reopen the dispute or delay operations, and the notice remains in the Competitor Account.

Docket then produces a nonoperative Replacement Candidate Pool but never writes a Judge or Competitor into the round automatically. Judge candidates must satisfy qualification, conflict and strike, availability, competitive-round fit, and workload constraints. Competitor candidates must be authenticated, fully authorized and eligible for the same School and event, free of conflicting assignments, and permitted by the Ruleset before the Entry's first round or under an express mid-tournament substitution rule afterward. The pool shows every eligible candidate plus separately ranked recommendations based on minimum disruption and the applicable constraints, never an automatic selection. Before replacement sign-off, Tabulation Staff may withdraw the No-Show and restore a late-arriving original assignee. After a replacement or bye is approved, reversal requires a Director-approved correction; after Started, the original person cannot return to that round. Tabulation Staff must sign off on the selected replacement. If no eligible Competitor is available, the opposing Entry receives a Bye Round under the locked Ruleset. Sign-off creates a new pairing or schedule version, preserves the original, repeats validation, notifies every affected Judge, Competitor, Coach, and authorized staff actor, and publishes a Correction Notice when prior public information changed. A successful dispute appends a reasoned reversal, removes the No-Show from the Competitor's current restricted record, corrects every not-yet-started affected artifact, and preserves the original classification only in restricted audit history. Started and Completed pairings remain as operated; Docket applies the locked Ruleset's correction treatment and publishes a Correction Notice when public information changed. That notice identifies the prior and corrected pairing, Bye Round, result, or standings effect using “No-Show classification corrected” without absence details, submitted evidence, private staff reasoning, or restricted actor metadata. A Competitor No-Show otherwise remains restricted tournament competitive evidence visible only to that Competitor, their authorized Coach, and authorized tournament staff and creates no cross-tournament score or public history. An Unexcused Judge No-Show creates the same restricted two-year Judge Reliability Record as an Unexcused Special Circumstance Withdrawal without changing qualification automatically.

### Tournament Schedule Publication

After Schedule Selection, the approving Director or Tabulation Staff with the Publication operations permission bundle performs **Tournament Schedule Publication** before the tournament begins. Publication places the approved round sequence, labels, stages, start times, expected durations, Scheduled Breaks, and human-readable Pairing Plan rules on the public Tournament Invitation Page. It does not publish future Entry-versus-Entry assignments or internal version metadata.

Every Pairing Publication must reference the current internal Pairing Plan version and occupy the matching published schedule block. A Pairing Plan change that affects round order, count, timing, duration, or resource assumptions requires Schedule Revision Approval and Schedule Revision Publication before the changed pairing may be published. The invitation page displays current human-readable content without raw versions, publishing attribution, or exact publication times, while authorized staff and audit history retain that metadata and earlier versions.

Tournament Schedule Publication also locks the displayed Pairing Plan version. A post-publication Pairing Plan Revision with no schedule impact still requires a Tournament Director to approve and republish the exact plan version. A revision with schedule impact must complete the schedule-revision workflow first. See [[pairing-model]] for changes after a round's Pairing Publication.

### Event Workspaces

Every configured event has its own Event Workspace. It is the event-specific dashboard for that event rather than a new Account, separate tournament, or competing schedule source. Authorized tournament staff receive the complete operational view. Competitors and Coaches receive the event schedule plus their own or their School's assignments and statuses. Assigned Judges receive only their assigned rounds. Public Viewers receive only normally public schedules, Pairings, and results.

Role-appropriate status includes check-in, assignment, room, Judge acknowledgment, and Scheduled, Started, Completed, or Result Pending state. The workspace never broadens access to private messages, detailed Ballots, Judge strikes, conflicts, assessment data, internal staff notes, or another School's restricted status.

Practice is a separate main-interface action rather than part of a tournament Event Workspace. A tournament-independent Practice Workspace remains event-specific, and all mock assignments, Ballots, results, and activity are nonoperative. The creating Entry Manager is the Practice Session Owner and may transfer ownership only to another Entry Manager at the same School; this grants practice controls only. Entry Managers may create sessions for affiliated Competitors, Competitors may join, Judges participate voluntarily, and other observers require explicit authorization.

Any person with a Docket Account may accept a Practice Invitation sent to that Account's exact Google-authenticated email address, including a minor, without School approval. Acceptance grants access only to that session. Resending preserves the same recipient, token, and expiration; a mistargeted invitation must be revoked and replaced rather than edited or retargeted. The invitation expires after seven days or when the session ends, whichever occurs first. The inviting Coach may revoke it before acceptance. Before acceptance, the recipient sees the Organizer and School, event, scheduled time and timezone, expected role, session expiration, participating Schools, and applicable privacy rules, but not participant email addresses, private profiles, Ballots, or feedback.

The Practice Session Owner may remove an accepted participant prospectively with a recorded reason and notice. Removal immediately ends participation and session access, but the former participant retains a restricted export window for only their own Ballots, feedback, and authored material for seven days or until the session's earlier deletion. Practice Workspaces are never public, searchable, or browsable; only the owner, accepted participants, and explicitly authorized observers can discover the session.

Every participant receives one or more compatible Practice Participant Roles: Organizer, Competitor, Judge, or Observer. An Organizer manages the session, a Competitor supplies only Competitor material, a Judge supplies Ballots and feedback without editing Competitor material, and an Observer reads only the material explicitly exposed to that role and cannot submit Ballots or feedback. Before start, the Organizer may reduce a participant's permission with notice; granting new access or submission authority requires the participant to accept the revised role. The Practice Role Lock fixes every accepted participant's role when the session starts, and no live role change is allowed. The Practice Session Owner may close the session, which locks new rounds, Ballots, feedback, and edits while retaining permitted reading and downloads. Closure is irreversible, requires a new session for further practice, and cannot extend deletion beyond thirty days after original session creation.

Participants may delete their own drafts and other unshared material. After a warning, the Practice Session Owner may delete the complete session; neither action affects official records. Practice creates no rankings or permanent performance history. Before deletion, each participant may download only their own practice Ballots and feedback, excluding other participants' private material. Docket deletes all remaining practice content no later than thirty days after session creation.

An Organizer may copy only reusable session configuration such as event, format, timing, role slots, and instructions. Copying never carries participants, invitations or acceptance, Ballots, feedback, private notes, or completed practice results. A Judge's ordinary Docket identity and public profile remain public, but the public cannot see that Judge's participation in a particular private Practice Workspace. Practice feedback remains restricted to its intended recipients and authorized Organizer.

Any participant may file a restricted Practice Safety Report with the Practice Session Owner and platform safety staff. When the subject is a minor, Docket also notifies an authorized Coach from that minor's School when one exists. The owner may immediately remove a participant but cannot dismiss, suppress, or decide the formal report. Platform safety staff decide it. They may immediately restrict the reported Account from the affected session and from creating or accepting new Practice Invitations; broader Account suspension must use the existing suspension workflow and cannot be imposed solely by the owner. One reconsideration may be requested within fourteen days and must be reviewed by different platform safety staff. Protective restrictions remain during review, and the second decision is final except for an objective system or identity error. An objective identity, timestamp, delivery, or system error may be corrected at any point during retention through an appended version that preserves the original without reopening the merits.

Platform safety staff can inspect the complete report evidence. The reporter and reported Account see only their own submissions and a privacy-minimized decision summary; the owner receives operational instructions only, and a minor's authorized Coach receives only necessary safeguarding information. Reports must come from authenticated Accounts, and platform safety staff know the reporter, but the reported participant does not receive reporter identity unless safety, procedural fairness, or law requires disclosure. An unsuccessful good-faith report creates no penalty. Evidence of deliberate abuse receives a separate platform safety investigation and may use the existing Account restriction or suspension workflow without affecting tournament results, qualifications, or competitive history. Docket retains only the minimized report, decision, and necessary evidence for two years after resolution, separate from ordinary practice deletion and Account exports unless disclosure is legally required; the existing two-person Legal Hold process may preserve an exact report beyond that period and resumes deletion after release.

The event format supplies the required Competitor structure. The Practice Session Owner sets additional Judge and Observer limits within a hard platform maximum of one hundred accepted Accounts, and Docket rejects invitations that exceed a role or total limit; ordinary practice rounds are expected to use substantially fewer Accounts. A session may carry its own start, expected end, and timezone for practice operations and notices, but Docket never compares that time with official tournament obligations, blocks either activity, or raises an overlap warning. Invitations, acceptance, material time or role changes before the Practice Role Lock, participant removal, cancellation, and closure produce in-app and email notices; only invitation acceptance requires action. Practice absence creates no No-Show classification, reliability record, qualification effect, or tournament history, and the Organizer may replace the absent participant, continue, reschedule, or cancel. The permissible post-start replacement path remains unresolved because no live role changes are allowed.

Cancellation is an irreversible pre-completion state requiring an Organizer-supplied reason and immediate participant notice. It blocks new activity, preserves permitted reading and exports only until the original thirty-day deletion deadline, and cannot be restarted. For online debate or speech practice, Docket creates a Google Practice Meeting and sends its Google Meet link to the assigned Competitors and Judges. Docket does not natively record audio or video and does not import meeting recordings; its retained practice materials remain the governed written Ballots, feedback, and session records.

A read-only Personal Tournament Agenda combines the assignments an authenticated Competitor, Coach, or Judge is already authorized to see across Event Workspaces, highlights conflicts, and sends in-app and email alerts for new or canceled assignments, changed times or rooms, and detected conflicts. Alerts require acknowledgment only when an existing workflow independently requires it.

Tournament-configured Round Tier Requirements are also hard constraints. Docket validates them against the Judge pool and any explicit Tournament Tier Grants before producing Schedule Candidates. If the pool cannot satisfy them, Docket reports which rounds and tiers are short and requires configuration, staffing, or an authorized local-grant decision to be addressed; it never silently assigns a lower-tier Judge.

For debate, a Temporary Debate Qualification from another tournament is never eligible input. A Judge without a current Event Qualification must complete a newly authorized Quick Judge Assessment for the current tournament before Docket may include that Judge in a feasible debate schedule. A provider result in Pending Verification supplies no qualification or eligibility, and a Tournament Tier Grant cannot bypass that rule; tournament staff must use another eligible Judge until verification succeeds.

Within those constraints, the Judge-Use Objective uses the Judge's Effective Tournament Tier as its assignment signal and records whether that tier came from the Event Qualification or a Tournament Tier Grant. It may use an available Judging Profile from a full or Quick Judge Assessment as a secondary assignment-fit signal. A missing optional speech assessment is recorded as unavailable data rather than treated as a failing score, and a profile preference never lowers a Judge's assessment or effective tier.

## Competitive-round priority

Docket calculates **Round Competitiveness Priority** only from the current tournament's structure, pairings, results, and advancement consequences:

1. Elimination rounds have higher priority than preliminary rounds. Priority increases by elimination stage toward the final.
2. Within preliminary rounds, top-record power pairings and **Bubble Rounds** whose outcomes directly affect advancement receive higher priority.
3. Docket never uses School reputation, competitor fame, external rankings, or results from another tournament to raise or lower priority.

Before specific pairings exist, the Judge-Use Schedule Candidate reserves higher-tier Judge capacity for higher-priority round categories. Once current-tournament pairings and records are available, Docket calculates specific priorities and assigns eligible Judges. The calculation uses only data available at assignment time and retains the inputs and score used to explain each assignment.

## Scheduled breaks

Docket supports first-class **Scheduled Breaks** for lunch, dinner, awards preparation, or another tournament-wide pause. Each break has a label, required duration, and either a fixed start time or an allowed placement window.

Scheduled Breaks are mandatory schedule constraints and appear explicitly in every Schedule Candidate and the selected Tournament Schedule. Their time does not count as optimization buffer. The Buffer-Time Objective distributes additional recovery time around rounds and Scheduled Breaks without shortening, overlapping, or removing a configured break.

## Correction-driven schedule revision

If a Result Correction or another round-data error creates a **Schedule Disruption**, Docket produces two separate **Schedule Revision Proposals** for Tabulation Staff to review:

1. **Time-Efficiency Objective** — minimize the remaining tournament duration and added delay after the disruption.
2. **Judge-Use Objective** — place the greatest possible number of correctly tiered Judges into the appropriate and most competitive rounds according to [[judge-qualification-model]].

These are recommendations rather than automatic changes to the Tournament Schedule. Tabulation Staff with the Pairing operations permission bundle may compare the proposals and prepare a recommendation, but only a Tournament Director may perform **Schedule Revision Approval** for one exact proposal version.

After approval, the approving Director or Tabulation Staff with the Publication operations permission bundle may perform **Schedule Revision Publication**. Publication makes that approved version the current Tournament Schedule and sends the required notices. A changed proposal requires new Director approval before publication. Docket never selects, approves, applies, or publishes a revision automatically.

During the tournament, Pairing-authorized Tabulation Staff may prepare a Critical Tournament Schedule Edit and its impact preview, but only a Tournament Director may approve the exact version and only the Director or Publication-authorized staff may publish it. Standard critical categories are Judge No-Show, Competitor No-Show, verified conflict, disqualification, and room change. `Other Tournament-Critical Operation` requires a specific explanation and impact preview. A noncritical edit remains technically available through the same authority but Docket displays a stronger advisory warning and requires explicit confirmation that the change is necessary during active competition. Every edit still requires a reason, validation, impact review, Schedule Revision Approval, publication, and applicable notices. Staff preference or convenience does not change the locked Pairing Plan or bypass those controls.

Approval and publication record the proposal and version, objective metrics, impact analysis, staff recommendation when present, approving Director, approval reason and time, publishing actor, and publication time. Docket preserves the original Tournament Schedule and every later version. When the disruption is a Downstream Conflict, the additional resolution rules in [[ballot-model]] also apply.

### Revision notices and acknowledgment

Schedule Revision Publication immediately creates a **Schedule Revision Notice** for every Tournament Director, relevant Tabulation Staff member, affected Judge, and active School Member for each affected Entry. Each notice identifies the changed schedule version, affected rounds, old and new times or assignments, publication reason, and action required. Docket records delivery attempts and delivery status.

Publication remains effective when a notice cannot be delivered. Docket retries the in-app and email notice through three delivery cycles and escalates continued failure to tournament operations. Staff may use and record the Judge's restricted operational contact method but cannot acknowledge for the Judge. A failed Competitor or Coach notice remains informational and does not block or roll back the revision.

When the revision corrects information already shown on a public Docket page, Docket also posts a human-readable Correction Notice beside the current public schedule. It explains the prior and corrected public schedule information, operational effect, and participant action without exposing internal versions, fingerprints, actors, exact timestamps, private evidence, or audit details.

Only a Judge whose assignment, room, or time changed must provide **Assignment Acknowledgment** within ten minutes after successful delivery and no later than the revised round start, whichever occurs first. Until acknowledgment, the changed Judge assignment remains visibly unconfirmed and is escalated on the tournament operations dashboard. Missing the deadline activates the Judge Continuity Plan and replacement workflow. Acknowledgment confirms receipt and availability; it does not approve the revision. School and Competitor notices are informational, do not require acknowledgment, and cannot block tournament operation.

A published critical edit may be reversed before the affected round Starts only through a **Schedule Reversal Revision**. It creates a new version restoring the earlier values, repeats the impact preview, Director approval, publication, and affected-party notices, and preserves the intervening version. Any currently authorized Tournament Director may approve the exact reversal; it need not be the Director who approved the preceding edit, and Docket retains both actors and reasons in restricted history. Docket never silently rolls back or edits published history in place. A Started round cannot be reversed.

### Urgent revisions

Each tournament has an **Urgent Change Threshold**, configured by a Tournament Director before Schedule Selection and defaulting to 30 minutes. Docket measures it from Schedule Revision Publication to the earliest affected round's scheduled start. It is a warning and confirmation boundary, not a hard minimum or publication ban.

When a proposal falls inside the threshold, Docket marks it as an **Urgent Schedule Revision**. Schedule Revision Approval then requires the Director to explicitly confirm the short lead time and attach a **Judge Continuity Plan** describing outreach, replacement-Judge options, and the conditions for holding or delaying the affected round if a Judge remains unacknowledged or becomes unavailable.

Urgency never authorizes a change to a round that has started. Started or completed rounds remain governed by the Downstream Conflict rules in [[ballot-model]].

### Accommodation-driven revisions

An active Accommodation Implementation Instruction may introduce a schedule, room, or assignment constraint after Schedule Selection or Publication. Scheduling consumes only the minimized operational constraint, never the complete Accommodation Request. Unpublished artifacts regenerate under the current locked policy. A published unstarted artifact follows the ordinary Schedule Revision Proposal, Director Approval, Publication, notice, and urgent-change controls; the accommodation does not create staff approval authority or bypass the Judge Continuity Plan.

The public Correction Notice and ordinary affected-recipient notices identify only the changed schedule information, generic restricted-operational-adjustment reason, and required action. They do not name, link to, or confirm an Accommodation Request. A feasible instruction may apply during an active round without rewriting the schedule or competitive record; otherwise the change is prospective. Started and completed schedule artifacts remain immutable.

## Deterministic tie-breaking

When multiple feasible schedules have the same primary objective score, Docket applies the **Schedule Tie-Break Policy** in this fixed order:

1. Preserve more of the existing published Tournament Schedule.
2. Change fewer participant, Judge, room, and Scheduled Break assignments.
3. Balance Judge workload and rest more evenly.
4. Prefer the earlier tournament finish.
5. Compare stable internal identifiers in canonical order as the final reproducible tie-breaker.

Each step applies only while the preceding steps remain tied. The first two steps apply to revisions; pre-tournament candidate generation begins with Judge workload and rest because no Tournament Schedule has yet been published. Docket never uses randomness. It records the policy version, scheduling inputs, objective score, and tie-break values so the same inputs can reproduce the same result.
