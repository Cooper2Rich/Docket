# Active Tournament Directory

## Purpose

Docket's authenticated application contains an **Invitations** section that functions as the **Active Tournament Directory**. A tournament enters the directory when its Tournament Invitation Page is first published and remains active through tournament operations. Draft tournaments never appear. Tournament Closure removes it to the Tournament Archive, and cancellation removes it from the directory.

A tournament listing is not an invitation to create a Docket Account and does not itself grant a School Membership, tournament role, Judge assignment, Entry, or other authority. Private authority grants are separately named **Access Offers** and appear in the Access Inbox. See [[access-model]].

Competitors, Judges, and Coaches create their own Basic Docket Accounts through Google authentication and can use the directory to discover tournaments. A record-only Competitor may exist solely as a non-production test fixture and never as a production participant. See [[access-model]] and [[registration-model]].

Each directory item links to the tournament's current public **Tournament Invitation Page**, which contains its published tournament information and governing human-readable policies. When applicable, it prominently labels the tournament **Cross-Entry Compatible**; absence of that label means overlapping event participation cannot be requested or approved. The directory does not replace the Tournament Invitation Page or the tournament's registration and Judge-participation workflows.

Directory browsing requires a Google-authenticated Docket Account. The linked Tournament Invitation Page remains publicly accessible by direct link for Public Viewers.

Every active published tournament is listed; the first release has no hidden or unlisted active tournament. Each listing shows the tournament name, host, physical location or online status, dates, events, registration status and deadline, current lifecycle status, and link to the Tournament Invitation Page. It excludes internal IDs, configuration fingerprints, publishing actors, and exact metadata timestamps.

The default order places tournaments currently underway first and then upcoming tournaments by nearest start date. Users may switch to recently published or alphabetical order. Search supports tournament and host name. Filters support date range, location, online versus in-person, debate or speech event, registration status, and tournament status, with keyboard and screen-reader operability.

Cancellation removes a tournament from this active directory. Its direct Tournament Invitation Page remains publicly accessible with a prominent Canceled status, cancellation date, and Director-supplied public explanation. Registration and Judge This Tournament actions disappear, while previously published information and applicable Correction Notices remain readable. Docket does not place a canceled tournament in the Tournament Archive, a separate cancellation index, or any other searchable tournament-discovery view; a person must already possess its direct link. Docket removes the page from public sitemaps, publishes search-engine noindex instructions, and creates no internal discovery link to it.

Tournament Closure moves the tournament into the public, unauthenticated, searchable **Tournament Archive**, where anyone can return to its permanently retained public results and history. Archive search supports tournament, host, represented School, event, and year. Filters support date range, location, online versus in-person, and event family. The default sort is newest first, with alphabetical and oldest-first options.

An archived tournament prominently shows the current corrected results by default and keeps all public Correction Notices visible in chronological order. An expandable **Tournament Publication History** contains every prior public results version, each clearly labeled Superseded. Correction evidence, internal metadata, and publishing actors remain restricted. See [[retention-model]] and [[final-results-model]].

Both active and archive result sets use cursor-based pages of 50 tournaments with accessible Previous, Next, and Load More controls. Search, filters, sort, and list position persist when a user opens a tournament and returns. Geographic discovery supports country, state, city, and an optional user-entered ZIP or postal-code radius. Docket neither imports a Google location nor requires device geolocation. An authenticated user may explicitly save a location as part of a named **Saved Tournament Filter**.

Authenticated users may create, rename, edit, and delete up to 20 Saved Tournament Filters containing directory search, filter, and sort preferences. One may be explicitly selected as the default directory view. The filters synchronize across the holder's devices but contain no privileged tournament data, and Docket never selects or stores a default location automatically.

An authenticated user may save an active or Closed tournament and separately opt into notifications for its schedule, registration, pairing, results, and Correction Notice changes. Saved tournaments synchronize across devices. If an active saved tournament is canceled, Docket sends one cancellation notice and automatically removes it from saved-tournament discovery; the cancellation notice does not place it in a searchable view.

An authenticated Coach uses **Register or Manage Entries** on the Tournament Invitation Page and must hold the required School Membership before entering the tournament's Registration Policy workflow. The tournament does not issue an Account invitation for this action.

An authenticated Judge uses **Judge This Tournament** to submit availability and event preferences to the tournament's Judge pool. The Tournament Director or Judge-and-room-authorized Tabulation Staff accepts participation after qualification, preference, availability, and conflict validation; submission alone does not grant assignment eligibility. Docket sends the Judge an in-app and email notice when the request is accepted or declined. A decline includes an operational reason category and never changes the Judge's qualification score or tier.

The accepted Judge Pool is viewable by authenticated Coaches, Competitors, accepted Judges, and the Tournament Director. Judge-and-room-authorized Tabulation Staff retain the operational access needed to manage it. The participant view always shows the current accepted pool with a clear **subject to change** notice. A departing Judge disappears immediately and a newly accepted Judge appears upon acceptance. The participant view shows only the accepted Judge's Docket Display Name, Independent or School-Supplied affiliation, and accepted event categories. It excludes qualification scores and tiers, assessment status and data, availability, conflicts, contact information, pending or declined requests, private Judging Profiles, withdrawal records, and change actors, reasons, or timestamps. A pending or declined Judge sees only their own participation status. Authorized staff retain the restricted pool change history.

Docket imposes no automatic Judge Pool capacity limit or waitlist in the first release. Authorized staff may accept every operationally useful Judge, while schedule generation determines which accepted Judges receive assignments.

An accepted Judge may withdraw ordinarily only before the Judge Withdrawal Lock Time 96 hours before the published Tournament Start Time. The Tournament Start Time is one explicit published instant in the tournament's official timezone and must be configured before Judge participation opens. Once participation opens, the Tournament Director may move the start and corresponding lock later but never earlier. After the lock, only a Special Circumstance Withdrawal is available. An allowed withdrawal affecting a published assignment uses the schedule-revision and notice lifecycle in [[scheduling-model]].

A Judge requesting Special Circumstance Withdrawal supplies a category and short explanation, but Docket never requires medical records. Docket immediately marks the Judge operationally unavailable and shows authorized staff the staffing and schedule impact. The Tournament Director or Judge-and-room-authorized Tabulation Staff then records an Excused or Unexcused outcome with a reason. Docket sends privacy-minimized operational notices, including to the School Judge Manager when the participant is School-Supplied.

An Unexcused outcome creates a restricted Judge Reliability Record for two years. The Judge and future Tournament Directors or Judge-and-room-authorized staff reviewing Judge Pool Participation may see its tournament, date, and outcome but not the private explanation. It never automatically changes qualification, tier, or acceptance and creates no platform blacklist. An Excused outcome creates no negative cross-tournament disclosure.

Judge Pool Participation is recorded as Independent or School-Supplied. A School Judge Manager may nominate a School-Supplied Judge, but the Judge must confirm the relationship. A School-Supplied Judge counts toward the School's judging obligation only after that confirmation and tournament acceptance. Before the Judge Withdrawal Lock Time, the Judge or School Judge Manager may request a relationship correction. After the lock, the Tournament Director or Judge-and-room-authorized Tabulation Staff must approve the correction with a recorded operational reason. The source and its correction affect obligation and scheduling but not qualification.

If withdrawal or source correction removes a School-Supplied Judge from its School's count, Docket immediately marks the obligation deficient and notifies the School Manager, Judge Manager, Entry Manager, and authorized tournament staff. The School may provide a replacement until the published replacement deadline. After it, only a remedy disclosed in the locked Registration Policy applies; Docket permits no improvised penalty.

When Register or Manage Entries finds no qualifying School Membership, Docket sends a membership request to the existing School's current School Manager for sign-off. It expires after seven days or at the applicable registration deadline, whichever is earlier, and Docket sends a reminder before expiration. The Manager may approve or deny it with a reason, and the Coach may submit one new request after materially correcting a denial. Platform Administrators intervene only when no functioning Manager exists and never override an active Manager. If there is no verified School or current Manager, the Coach enters the Provisional School and first-membership Platform Administrator verification workflow. Tournament registration never creates School authority by itself.

An authenticated Competitor receives direct in-app and email notices only for their own Pairing Publications, Disqualifications, and Corrections. Entry acceptance and rejection notices go to the responsible Coach, and a Competitor never receives admission authority merely because their Account is referenced by an Entry.

Only the Competitor's Coach may submit a verified Judge Conflict for that Competitor or Entry. A Tournament Director may enable a separate per-event Judge Striking Policy and publish it before registration opens. One native portal window governs a multi-day tournament unless the policy predefines separate event sessions. It opens exactly 36 hours before Tournament Start Time and closes at 3:00 p.m. in the official named tournament timezone on the preceding calendar day, with the user's local equivalent also displayed. Each Entry designates a Primary Responsible Coach and may predesignate a Backup Responsible Coach before opening; both require active School Membership tied to the affected Competitors, and the backup acts only when the primary is unavailable. The Entry chooses its strikes, and the acting Coach resolves disagreement, approves, corrects, or voluntarily releases them. Docket's transactional in-app receipt makes approval operative without emailing the Tournament Director. Every pre-close change requires fresh approval. Unapproved choices are nonoperative after reminders six and one hours before closing. Ordinary workflow permits no post-close Judge addition or supplemental strike. A late exception requires platform-health evidence affecting that Entry, a recovery window equal to the outage capped at 30 minutes, Director approval, and no known pairing or opponent. The policy provides equal configured hard-strike allowances and ranked preferences and restricts selections from Judges and opposing Schools; authorized staff may use anonymized aggregates. If conflicts and hard strikes leave no qualified Judge, Pairing Publication blocks until staff add one or the acting Coach releases a strike after reviewing the warning with the affected Competitors; the release is final and staff cannot choose it. Strike records follow the same restricted seven-year Competitive Evidence retention as other Competitor tournament records. The policy locks with the Pairing Plan subject only to its existing pre-tournament emergency correction rules.

An unexpected post-close withdrawal or No-Show permits a reasoned Emergency Replacement Judge approved by the Tournament Director or Judge-and-room-authorized Tabulation Staff. Docket revalidates verified conflicts, does not reopen strikes, prefers previously available eligible Judges, and requires assignment-specific Tabulation Staff sign-off. The assigned Judge, affected Competitors and Coaches, Directors, and relevant Tabulation Staff receive immediate in-app and email notice with a generic operational reason and no private withdrawal or No-Show evidence. The Judge must acknowledge within ten minutes and before start, using the shorter remaining interval. Until then the assignment is unconfirmed. One delivery cycle sends both electronic messages and fails only when neither confirms delivery; no acknowledgment after confirmed delivery is a separate timeout. Staff may record restricted manual contact but cannot acknowledge for the Judge. After three failed cycles the assignment becomes Delivery Failed, creates no No-Show or Judge Reliability Record, and staff must find another Judge. A late acknowledgment cannot revive it. Tentative identity and Published Judging Profile are available only to affected parties and staff, not the public Pairing Publication. Every uncapped use requires a fresh reason and sign-off; the second in one event or third tournament-wide and every later use warns the Director. An acknowledged emergency Judge who fails to appear enters the ordinary Judge No-Show process. An affected Coach may report only a verified conflict, qualification error, or assignment-data error—not exercise a new strike or veto—and Docket blocks start while a potentially disqualifying allegation remains unresolved.

The No-Show grace period defaults to ten minutes after the current published round start. A Tournament Director may configure and publish another per-event period before Schedule Selection but cannot shorten it afterward. Docket requires in-app and email notices plus a documented operational attempt through the responsible Coach for a Competitor or restricted tournament contact method for a Judge, never a minor's required or exposed phone number. Judge-and-room-authorized Tabulation Staff classify the No-Show. A Competitor may file directly through Docket within 60 minutes or before that event's next round starts, whichever is first. Docket associates and notifies the responsible Coach, who may add a statement but cannot block, withdraw, or suppress it. Only the filing Competitor may withdraw before decision; explicit confirmation makes withdrawal final for that classification, the withdrawal is audited, and the classification stays operative. The dispute may rely on check-in, arrival, messaging, staff-contact, and delivery records or proof of an incorrect published time or room, without required medical records. The Director decides within 30 minutes or before the next event round, whichever is first; an overdue review escalates while the classification and replacement stay operative without automatic approval or denial. A rejection is final for the tournament except for objective correction of a system or data-entry error. Its privacy-minimized in-app and email notice is delivery-tracked but requires no acknowledgment, remains available in the Competitor Account, and neither reopens the dispute nor delays operations if delivery fails.

Docket may then present a complete eligible Replacement Candidate Pool and separately ranked recommendations, but it cannot write a replacement Judge or Competitor into the round. Judge ranking uses qualification, conflicts and strikes, availability, competitive-round fit, workload, and minimum disruption. Competitor candidates must be authenticated, fully authorized and eligible for the same School and event, free of assignment conflict, and allowed by the Ruleset before the Entry's first round or under an express mid-tournament substitution rule. If no Competitor is available, the opposing Entry receives a Bye Round. Before sign-off, Tabulation Staff may restore a late-arriving original assignee; after replacement or bye approval, reversal requires Director correction, and Started rounds cannot restore the original. A successful dispute appends a reasoned reversal, removes the current restricted No-Show flag, corrects unstarted artifacts, and preserves the original classification only in restricted audit history. Tabulation Staff sign-off creates a new validated pairing or schedule version, preserves the original, sends all affected-party notices, and produces a Correction Notice when public information changed. The public notice identifies the previous and corrected pairing, Bye Round, result, or standings effect using the generic reason “No-Show classification corrected” and excludes absence details, evidence, internal reasoning, and restricted actor metadata. Started and Completed rounds remain as operated. A Competitor No-Show otherwise remains restricted evidence for that tournament, visible only to the Competitor, their authorized Coach, and authorized tournament staff, and never creates a cross-tournament score or public history. An Unexcused Judge No-Show produces the same restricted two-year Judge Reliability Record as an Unexcused Special Circumstance Withdrawal.

The Historical Access Dashboard exposes authorized Represented-School Tournament Records through tournament, season, event, Competitor, record-type, and represented-School filters without full-text search across private Feedback. Only the School Manager may request a freshly reauthenticated bulk export, limited to one completed atomic delivery set per School every twenty-four hours and a seven-day authenticated download window that begins when every numbered part succeeds. A master checksum manifest is copied into each part, and date-range preflight discloses included and excluded retention categories plus imminent scheduled deletion without extending retention. Later corrections notify the Competitor and original represented School, and notify a destination School only when its represented Entry changes. Retention-protected history cannot be deleted early by either the Competitor or School.
