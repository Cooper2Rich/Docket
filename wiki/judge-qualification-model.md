# Judge Qualification Model

## Tournament participation interest

An authenticated Judge may discover an active tournament through [[tournament-directory-model]] and use Judge This Tournament on its Tournament Invitation Page to submit availability and event preferences to that tournament's Judge pool. This self-service expression of interest is neither an Access Offer nor guaranteed acceptance, assignment, or qualification.

The Tournament Director or Judge-and-room-authorized Tabulation Staff accepts Judge Pool Participation only after Docket validates qualification status, event preferences, availability, and conflicts. Accepted participation records a Judge-confirmed Independent or School-Supplied source without changing qualification. A School Judge Manager may nominate the latter relationship, but the Judge confirms it.

Docket delivers acceptance or decline by in-app and email notice; a decline includes an operational category and does not alter qualification. Authenticated Coaches, Competitors, accepted Judges, and the Tournament Director may view the accepted Judge Pool, while authorized Tabulation Staff retain operational access. A pending or declined Judge sees only their own participation status. Docket imposes no automatic pool capacity limit or waitlist. The participant view always shows the current pool with a subject-to-change notice, adding or removing a Judge immediately when acceptance changes. It contains only accepted Judge Docket Display Name, Independent or School-Supplied affiliation, and accepted event categories. It excludes qualification scores and tiers, assessment status and data, availability, conflicts, contact information, pending or declined requests, private Judging Profiles, withdrawal records, and change provenance. Authorized staff retain restricted pool-change history.

A School-Supplied Judge counts toward the School's judging obligation only after Judge confirmation and tournament acceptance. Before the Judge Withdrawal Lock Time, the Judge or School Judge Manager may request a source correction. After the lock, the Tournament Director or Judge-and-room-authorized Tabulation Staff must approve the correction with a recorded operational reason. No source or correction changes qualification.

If a withdrawal or source correction removes that Judge from the School's obligation count, Docket immediately marks the obligation deficient and notifies the School Manager, Judge Manager, Entry Manager, and authorized tournament staff. Replacement remains available until the published deadline; afterward only a remedy already disclosed in the locked Registration Policy may apply.

Ordinary withdrawal is available until 96 hours before the explicit Tournament Start Time published in the tournament's official timezone. The Tournament Start Time must be configured before Judge participation opens and may move later but never earlier afterward. After the Judge Withdrawal Lock Time, Docket accepts only a Special Circumstance Withdrawal request containing a category and short explanation, without medical records. Docket marks the Judge operationally unavailable immediately; the Tournament Director or Judge-and-room-authorized staff records an Excused or Unexcused outcome and Docket sends privacy-minimized notices. An Unexcused outcome creates a two-year restricted Judge Reliability Record visible to the Judge and future Director or Judge-and-room-authorized reviewers, containing the tournament, date, and outcome but not the explanation. It never automatically changes qualification, tier, or acceptance. An Excused outcome creates no negative cross-tournament disclosure. Every allowed withdrawal touching a published assignment uses the existing schedule-revision workflow. Every later assignment still passes the qualification, conflict, availability, staffing, and schedule controls below.

An Unexcused Judge No-Show classified by Judge-and-room-authorized Tabulation Staff after the default ten-minute or configured published grace period, in-app and email notices, and a documented restricted contact attempt creates the same restricted two-year Judge Reliability Record. Docket may include every eligible Judge in a Replacement Candidate Pool and rank recommendations by qualification, conflicts and strikes, availability, competitive-round fit, workload, and disruption, but cannot assign one automatically; Tabulation Staff must sign off and complete the applicable governed workflow. No-Show classification and replacement never automatically change Judge qualification. Director review applies only to a dispute or Ruleset competitive penalty.

An Emergency Replacement Judge admitted after the strike portal closes must still satisfy the current event qualification, availability, and every verified conflict before assignment. Reasoned Director or Judge-and-room-authorized staff admission does not reopen discretionary strikes or itself assign the Judge; Docket prefers eligible Judges who were already available and requires assignment-specific Tabulation Staff sign-off. Three failed electronic delivery cycles end only the unconfirmed assignment as Delivery Failed and create no No-Show or Judge Reliability Record. An acknowledged emergency Judge who later fails to appear follows the ordinary Judge No-Show workflow.

## Assessment applicability

Every Judge is encouraged to complete an event-specific **Full Judge Qualification Assessment**, but the assignment requirement differs by event family:

- A debate Judge must complete the Full Judge Qualification Assessment for that debate event before assignment, unless an authorized Quick Judge Assessment supplies temporary eligibility during the current tournament.
- A speech Judge is not required to complete an assessment before assignment. The Full Judge Qualification Assessment remains recommended so Docket can capture qualification and judging-philosophy information.

This supersedes the earlier rule that every debate and speech Judge had to complete the full assessment. It also resolves the prior local-grant conflict for debate: staff may bypass an assessment result or tier, but a debate Judge must have completed either the full or quick form for the event.

The Full Judge Qualification Assessment supplies the evidence used to place a Judge into one **Judge Qualification Tier**:

- **Qualified Judge** — eligible for the tournament's ordinary rounds.
- **Advanced Judge** — suited for more competitive or technically demanding rounds.
- **Elimination-Qualified Judge** — suited for elimination rounds and the tournament's highest-priority competitive rounds.

## Assessment and profile retention

Judge Qualification Assessment data does not inherit the general tournament periods in [[retention-model]], even when a qualification was used at a tournament. Tournament-local assignment and decision provenance may qualify as Seven-Year Competitive Evidence without copying assessment answers or raw scores into the tournament record.

Docket retains the current **Qualification Summary** while its Event Qualification remains valid. Full-assessment submitted answers, detailed scores, Critical Competency Item detail, provider payload, and Assessment Integrity Review evidence are immutable while retained and are automatically deleted two years after the attempt's completion unless an active Legal Hold applies.

When an Event Qualification expires or is superseded, Docket retains a restricted **Qualification History Summary** for seven years from that expiration or supersession. It contains only the Judge, event, assessment version, completion date, resulting tier, validity period, qualification source, and aggregate Critical Competency pass or fail. It contains no submitted answers, question-level outcomes, detailed score, Judging Profile text, provider payload, or integrity evidence.

The current Judging Profile remains until the Judge replaces or deletes it. A superseded profile is deleted two years after supersession unless held. When a specific Published Judging Profile version was disclosed for a tournament, Docket stores that exact tournament-scoped version as restricted Competitive Evidence for seven years after that tournament's Closure; retaining it does not make it public or extend disclosure to unrelated Schools.

Quick Assessment details continue to use their earlier deletion trigger: completion of the Full Judge Qualification Assessment or the missed Quick Assessment Follow-up Deadline, whichever applies first. The remaining minimal quick-assignment provenance follows the seven-year Competitive Evidence period for the tournament that relied on it. Every assessment attempt and profile version remains immutable until its scheduled deletion; deletion does not rewrite historical tournament assignments or their minimal provenance.

## Assessment sections

The Full Judge Qualification Assessment contains two independently used sections:

1. The scored **Judge Competency Section** tests applicable rules, ethics, and argument evaluation. Its result determines whether the Judge is assignment-eligible and which Judge Qualification Tier the assessment supports.
2. The unscored **Judging Profile** records how the Judge evaluates and interacts with arguments. Docket uses it to improve assignment fit and to explain the Judge's approach where disclosure is authorized.

A legitimate judging preference recorded in the Judging Profile never reduces the Judge's competency score or Judge Qualification Tier. Docket keeps competency evidence separate from expressed argument preferences and never treats a preference as a wrong answer merely because it reflects a different accepted judging philosophy.

## Quick Judge Assessment

When a staffing need requires a Judge to move into a debate event for which the Judge lacks a current Event Qualification, a Tournament Director or Tabulation Staff member with the Judge and room operations permission bundle may authorize a **Quick Judge Assessment**. It is a shortened event-specific assessment that captures a temporary score and Judging Profile for use during the current tournament.

Completing it creates a **Temporary Debate Qualification** valid only for the authorized tournament days. It does not create or change the season-bounded Event Qualification. A Tournament Tier Grant may still set the Judge's Effective Tournament Tier at staff discretion, but the quick assessment must be completed before the moved Judge receives the debate assignment.

After the tournament, Docket sends the Judge a **Quick Assessment Expiration Notice** explaining that the temporary qualification has ended. The notice states that the Judge must complete the event's Full Judge Qualification Assessment by the **Quick Assessment Follow-up Deadline** to avoid taking another quick assessment before a later debate assignment, and that detailed quick-assessment data will be deleted either when the full assessment is completed or when the deadline is missed.

The deadline depends on the Judge's next debate assignment:

- If the Judge has no future tournament debate assignment in Docket, the deadline is 30 days after the prior tournament ends. Docket sends reminders on days 14 and 27.
- If the Judge is scheduled to judge debate at another tournament, the deadline is two days before that next tournament begins, replacing the 30-day deadline even when it falls later. Docket updates and sends the deadline when the future assignment is established.

If the Judge misses the applicable deadline, Docket deletes the prior quick score, detailed responses, and Judging Profile. The Judge must complete a new Quick Judge Assessment before judging debate at the next tournament unless the Full Judge Qualification Assessment has since been completed. A prior Temporary Debate Qualification never carries into the next tournament.

Deletion removes the quick score, detailed responses, and Judging Profile. Docket retains only the minimal historical record needed to explain prior assignments: Judge, tournament, event, quick-assessment version, completion time, authorizing actor, temporary eligibility dates, and assignments that relied on it.

The Full Judge Qualification Assessment always stands on its own. It captures new scored responses and a new Judging Profile; Docket does not import, merge, or use a quick score, response, or profile to produce the full result. When the Judge completes the full assessment, Docket immediately deletes the detailed quick data, uses only the resulting Event Qualification and full Judging Profile for future decisions, and retains the same minimal quick-assessment audit record for historical assignments.

## Tier assignment

Docket assigns the Judge Qualification Tier automatically from the Judge Competency Section using the assessment version's published **Tier Scoring Bands**. The assessment also identifies **Critical Competency Items** covering essential rules and ethics; all required critical items must be answered correctly before any passing tier may be issued, regardless of the overall score.

Tier Scoring Bands and Critical Competency Items are platform-controlled parts of a versioned, event-specific assessment. Docket calibrates them through pilot testing before publishing the assessment version. A Tournament Owner, Tournament Director, or Tabulation Staff member cannot alter questions, scores, bands, critical-item requirements, or the meanings of the three tiers.

Routine staff discretion cannot raise, lower, bypass, or correct the assessment-computed Event Qualification tier. Docket provides no appeal of an assessment result, raw score, Critical Competency Item outcome, or tier. A Judge seeking a different result must complete another assessment under the applicable retake or Tier Test-Out Attempt rules; Docket never substitutes a staff-authored corrected score or qualification.

A Judge may permanently improve the tier within an Event Qualification only through a new assessment attempt whose score tests into a higher Tier Scoring Band.

## Retakes and test-out attempts

After a nonpassing Full Judge Qualification Assessment, Docket assigns event-specific remediation and enforces a 24-hour waiting period before another attempt. The retake must use a randomized, equivalent form of the same current assessment version. A Judge may take no more than two such retakes within any rolling 30-day period.

A Judge who already holds a current Event Qualification may make one **Tier Test-Out Attempt** every 30 days. A higher passing result upgrades the current Event Qualification tier. A lower or nonpassing result does not reduce or invalidate the Judge's existing current tier; Docket continues using the highest current valid result.

Every attempt and result remains immutable and linked to its assessment version, event, provider, completion time, remediation state, and resulting qualification action. Identity resolution, technical investigation, or provider error does not revise an existing scored result. Any different scored outcome must come from a new assessment attempt.

## Uniform assessment standard

Docket offers no assessment accessibility accommodation. Every Judge taking a given event-specific assessment version receives the same timing, delivery configuration, content blueprint, Critical Competency Item requirements, scoring bands, and tier standards. Docket and the Assessment Provider do not provide extended time, additional breaks, alternate formats, assistive-delivery changes, altered questions, or modified scoring for an individual Judge.

Random selection among equivalent forms within the same published assessment version remains allowed for retakes and test security. Those forms must use the same blueprint and standards and do not constitute an individual accommodation.

## Initial external assessment provider

The initial implementation does not administer Judge Qualification Assessments inside Docket. A third-party **Assessment Provider** administers the test and sends the result to Docket. Docket applies its qualification, validity, retake, test-out, access, and audit rules to the received result and remains the system of record for Event Qualifications, Temporary Debate Qualifications, tiers, and tournament assignment eligibility.

Docket creates a single-use **Assessment Attempt ID** before sending the Judge to the provider. The provider delivers a signed, retryable webhook containing that attempt ID, a unique provider result ID, Docket Judge and event identifiers, assessment version, completion time, raw score, Critical Competency Item results, Judging Profile responses, and the Judge's submitted answers. The provider must never send its answer key.

Only an authenticated Judge may initiate their own provider-hosted assessment from Docket. Docket sends the provider the opaque Assessment Attempt ID rather than relying on a name or email address, and the provider returns its stable account identifier with the result. The first verified result creates a one-to-one link between that provider account and the Docket Judge. Names and email addresses may be shown as mismatch warnings but are never authoritative identity keys.

A result without a Docket-initiated attempt, a provider account already linked to another Judge, or an attempt whose returned identity conflicts with its authorization is blocked from qualification processing and placed in **Assessment Identity Review**. An authorized Platform Administrator may resolve the link only through an attributed, evidence-backed, audited review. Any correction creates a new link version and preserves the prior link and decision history; Docket never silently moves assessment attempts, results, qualifications, or assignment evidence between Judges.

Docket verifies the webhook signature and checks every identifier and assessment version against the authorized attempt. A signature failure, mismatch, unknown attempt, reused attempt with a different result, or unsupported assessment version is rejected and cannot create or change a qualification. Repeated delivery of the same provider result ID and identical attempt result is handled idempotently without creating another attempt or tier action.

After verification, Docket stores the immutable provider evidence under the access rules below and calculates the authoritative result and Judge Qualification Tier with Docket's versioned scoring bands and Critical Competency Item policy. A provider-supplied pass status or tier is non-authoritative. Ordinary manual score entry is also non-authoritative and cannot create a qualification.

If webhook delivery is delayed or unavailable, the provider retries automatically. As an alternate transport, an authorized Platform Administrator may upload a provider-signed result file. The upload is attributed and audited and must contain the same result contract; Docket applies the same signature, identity, version, attempt, and duplicate checks as it does to the webhook. This is not manual score entry.

Until a delivered result passes every verification check, the attempt has **Pending Verification** status and creates no new Event Qualification, Temporary Debate Qualification, tier, or debate-assignment eligibility. Tournament staff may reassign another eligible Judge but may not manually declare the pending Judge qualified or use a Tournament Tier Grant to bypass assessment completion. A later verified result takes effect prospectively. Any separate existing valid qualification remains effective while the new attempt is pending.

### Tournament-local tier grants

A Tournament Director or Tabulation Staff member with the Judge and room operations permission bundle may issue a **Tournament Tier Grant** that gives a Judge a higher assignment tier for the current tournament only. The Judge must first complete the applicable assessment attempt and its result must pass identity and delivery verification; the grant may override a nonpassing result but cannot replace, waive, or precede the attempt. The grant does not change the Judge's assessment score, Event Qualification tier, profile, or qualification at any other tournament.

The authorizing Director or staff member may grant any higher tournament tier at their discretion even when the Judge did not test into Qualified status or failed one or more Critical Competency Items. No assessed-tier floor, critical-item prerequisite, evidence standard, or maximum elevation limits the local grant. This explicitly rejects the proposed baseline safeguard; the grant record still makes the discretionary actor and outcome visible.

The higher of the assessment-supported tier and any current Tournament Tier Grant is the Judge's **Effective Tournament Tier**. Each grant records the event, original tier, granted tier, authorizing actor, reason, issue time, and automatic expiration at the end of the tournament. Revocation ends future use while preserving the grant and assignments that relied on it.

This local grant narrows the earlier prohibition on tournament tier changes: tournaments still cannot alter platform scores or Event Qualification tiers, but they may create an explicit, attributed, tournament-scoped assignment tier.

A tournament may configure a **Round Tier Requirement** that requires an existing higher Effective Tournament Tier for a defined round or round category. This filters assignment eligibility but never changes a Judge's assessment score or Event Qualification tier. Docket validates the available Judge pool and explicit local grants before schedule generation; if the requirements make a feasible schedule impossible, Docket reports the shortage and does not silently lower them.

## Event scope and reuse

Each completed Full Judge Qualification Assessment creates an **Event Qualification** tied to one debate or speech event, the assessment version, and its compatible Ruleset versions. A current Event Qualification may be reused across tournaments that use compatible rules. A debate Judge entering a different event must complete that event's full assessment or an authorized Quick Judge Assessment, even if qualified in another event. A speech Judge may enter a different speech event without an assessment, though the event-specific full assessment remains recommended.

A tournament may also require a short **Tournament Judge Orientation** covering its Tournament Overrides and local operating procedures. Orientation completion is a tournament-specific assignment prerequisite, but it does not change the Judge's Event Qualification score or tier.

## Validity and reassessment

An Event Qualification remains current through one defined competitive season. It expires at the season boundary and the Judge must complete the next season's applicable assessment before receiving another assignment.

Docket may supersede the qualification earlier only when a material change to the event's Ruleset or Judge Qualification Assessment makes the prior result incompatible. A new compatible assessment version is then required. Editorial changes that do not alter tested knowledge, scoring, or tier meaning do not invalidate a qualification.

Expired and superseded qualifications remain immutable in history with their assessment version, score, tier, validity dates, and supersession reason. Prior round assignments continue to reference the qualification that supported them.

The Judge-Use Objective in [[scheduling-model]] uses the Effective Tournament Tier when matching Judges to rounds while retaining its assessment and grant sources separately. Availability and conflicts remain hard constraints; a higher tier never overrides them.

Round Competitiveness Priority is derived only from current-tournament data as defined in [[scheduling-model]]. Docket does not treat a School's reputation, a Competitor's fame, an external ranking, or a result from another tournament as evidence that a round deserves a higher-tier Judge.

The assessment result, applicable assessment version, completion time, and resulting tier must be retained so a schedule can explain which qualification evidence supported an assignment. Docket must not derive a tier from competitors' win rates on the Judge's prior Ballots.

Ballot timeliness is tournament operations data, not qualification evidence. A tournament-local Judge Assignment Hold or removal from future assignments after an escalated missing Ballot does not change the Judge's assessment score, Event Qualification, Judge Qualification Tier, Judging Profile, or eligibility for another tournament.

## Access and disclosure

A Judge may view all of their own assessment data, including submitted responses, raw scores, qualification status, tiers, grant history, and Judging Profile. This does not disclose the platform's protected answer key, unpublished question bank, scoring logic, or another Judge's data.

Tournament Directors and Tabulation Staff with the Judge and room operations permission bundle may view only the operational qualification summary needed for the current tournament: assessment-completion status, whether the supporting qualification is full or temporary, Event Qualification tier, Effective Tournament Tier, qualification validity, Tournament Tier Grant history, and the Judge's **Published Judging Profile**. They may not view raw scores or question-by-question responses.

A Judging Profile is private by default. The Judge may publish a version for tournament use; publication is explicit, versioned, and may not expose scored assessment responses. Docket's assignment engine may process the private profile as a secondary fit signal without disclosing it to tournament personnel. Directors and authorized staff see only the published version.

After Pairing Publication, active School Members for either Entry in the assigned round may view that assigned Judge's Published Judging Profile. They receive no assessment status, raw score, detailed response, tier, or grant history. Other Judges, unrelated Schools, authenticated Competitors without a separately granted profile-view permission, and Public Viewers receive no assessment or profile access.

Detailed assessment questions, submitted responses, raw scores, and scoring metadata may be accessed by authorized Platform Administrators only while performing an audited **Assessment Integrity Review** concerning provider delivery, identity, security, or test administration. The review records the reviewing actor, purpose, scope, time, and findings. It is not an appeal and cannot alter the result, score, or tier; any different outcome requires a retake. Ordinary platform support access does not reveal this evidence.

## Deferred assessment-product decisions

- Exact questions, score calculation, ranking method, tier thresholds, calibration, detailed compatibility rules, and integrity-review authority belong to the separate Assessment Provider product and are deferred from the current Docket backend design.
