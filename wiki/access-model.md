# Access Model

## First-slice actors

The initial backend supports authenticated accounts for:

- **Platform Administrator** — operates Docket across tournaments for platform support and governance.
- **Tournament Owner** — the single Tournament Director accountable for ownership, Director appointments, and closure.
- **Tournament Director** — governs rules and delegated operational staff for a tournament.
- **Tabulation Staff** — performs tournament operations under delegated authority.
- **Coach** — represents a School and manages that School's participation according to [[registration-model]].
- **Judge** — receives assignments and submits ballots for assigned rounds.
- **Competitor** — holds an individual authenticated Account and participates in tournament Entries.

The system also represents:

- **Public Viewer** — an unauthenticated reader who can access only information explicitly published by a tournament.

Precise permissions, delegation rules, and authority boundaries remain to be defined. Authorization must be scoped to the relevant platform, tournament, school, or round rather than inferred from possession of a generic role name.

## Google authentication boundary

Google Identity Services using OpenID Connect is the sole authentication provider for the first backend slice. Docket does not store or administer passwords, passkeys, TOTP secrets, SMS factors, recovery codes, or other primary or additional authentication factors. Google owns sign-in, factor enrollment and challenges, account-security policy, and recovery of the Google account.

Any verified Google account may authenticate a Competitor, Coach, Judge, Tournament Owner, Tournament Director, or Tabulation Staff account. Platform Administrators and Legal and Privacy Operations personnel must instead use a Google Workspace account from a Docket-approved organization-managed domain. This is an eligibility prerequisite for those platform permissions, not an authorization grant: an approved domain never creates a permission, School Membership, or Tournament Staff Assignment.

Competitors, Coaches, and Judges may create a **Basic Docket Account** through Google sign-in. That account carries no School, tournament, Judge-assignment, or platform authority merely because it exists. Competitor-School relationships, School Memberships, tournament staff positions, Tournament Director and Owner authority, and platform permissions require separately authorized workflows.

Each authenticated **Docket Account** links to exactly one current **Google Identity Subject**, identified by the verified token issuer and stable Google subject identifier, and one Google Identity Subject may belong to only one Docket Account. Email address, display name, and hosted-domain claims are mutable attributes and cannot serve as the account key, prove a School Membership, or grant a tournament or platform role. Docket requires Google's verified-email claim but never auto-links or merges Docket Accounts merely because an email address matches.

The backend validates every Google authentication response server-side before creating a Docket session. Validation includes the token signature against Google's published keys, accepted issuer, Docket client audience, expiration and issued-at constraints, verified email, and the request's one-time state, nonce, and cross-site request protections. A client-side success message or unverified email value is never sufficient.

Google authentication establishes only who the actor is. One Docket Account may independently hold Coach, Judge, tournament, and platform roles, but Docket separately authorizes each action through its explicit platform, tournament, School, or Judge-assignment context. An authenticated actor selects an **Active Role Context** through Docket's native interface and may switch between every context that Account currently holds without using a separate Account or performing a new sign-in merely to switch.

Each browser tab maintains its own Active Role Context, so switching one tab does not alter another. Docket synchronizes the Account's most recently used context across devices and restores it after sign-in when the Account still holds it, including Platform Administrator and Legal and Privacy Operations contexts. This supersedes the earlier ordinary-only restoration rule. Restoring a privileged context activates its stricter session limits and does not waive any command-specific Google Reauthentication requirement. If the remembered context is no longer authorized, Docket displays the context selector.

An Account holder cannot disable, forget, or reset cross-device context restoration. Docket clears the remembered context only when its underlying authority is revoked, expires, is transferred, or otherwise becomes invalid.

The application header persistently identifies the active role and its School, tournament, Judge, or platform scope. Platform Administrator and Legal and Privacy Operations contexts receive a distinct privileged visual treatment. The interface, projections, navigation, and backend command scope change with the selected context; possession of one role never blends its permissions into another.

If the current context contains unsaved work, Docket blocks the switch until the user saves the draft, discards the changes, or cancels the switch. It never carries an unfinished form, selected private record, or context-scoped client cache into another role context.

A role-context deep link identifies its required context without embedding private payload data. If the Account currently holds that context, Docket asks before switching and then resolves the resource. If it does not, Docket returns a generic access-denied response that does not reveal whether the resource exists. A deep link never enters a privileged context automatically.

Docket provides one Account-level notification inbox, but every notification is labeled and filterable by role and exact School, tournament, Judge, or platform scope. A notification rendered outside its context exposes only the label and safe routing summary; opening it offers the necessary context switch before private detail is loaded.

The Account holder may configure routine email and in-app notification preferences separately for each context. Authentication and security alerts, suspension notices, ownership or authority changes, ballot-result emergencies, Legal Hold obligations, and other required governance notices cannot be disabled.

Every backend request carries an explicit Active Role Context and Docket revalidates the current grant instead of trusting the client selection. A stale or revoked context is rejected without returning protected data and sends the actor to context selection. Ordinary context-switch events are retained as Routine Operational Telemetry for two years; privileged context entry and exit events are retained as restricted security provenance for seven years.

An ordinary **Docket Session** expires after 12 hours of inactivity or seven days after issuance, whichever occurs first. Switching into a Platform Administrator or Legal and Privacy Operations context activates the privileged 30-minute inactivity and 12-hour absolute limits without requiring a separate login solely for the switch. Sensitive commands remain subject to their own fresh Google Reauthentication guard. Expiration requires a new Google authentication; Docket does not silently extend an expired absolute lifetime.

One Docket Account may hold no more than five concurrent ordinary sessions and one concurrent privileged session. Starting a new privileged session terminates the prior privileged session. A session-management view shows each device, approximate location, last activity, session class, and termination control, and allows the Account holder to terminate all sessions.

Whenever a device restores Platform Administrator or Legal and Privacy Operations, Docket sends a security alert and displays a prominent privileged-context banner containing the device, approximate location, time, and an immediate session-termination control.

The following commands require **Google Reauthentication** completed within the preceding 10 minutes: ownership transfer, role or permission change, Google Identity Link Replacement, Legal Hold action, sensitive-data export, Docket Account suspension, and publication or correction of Final Results. Domain-specific approval and separation-of-duty rules still apply after reauthentication; fresh authentication never constitutes approval.

When a person's verified Google email or profile changes but the issuer and subject remain the same, Docket keeps the same Account, refreshes the verified contact attributes after authentication, and retains the prior values only in restricted identity history. The change never grants or revokes authority.

If a Google account becomes inaccessible, deleted, or suspended, Docket denies new authentication without deleting the Docket Account, its grants, or its historical actor attribution. The person must attempt Google recovery first. If recovery is impossible, a controlled, evidence-backed **Google Identity Link Replacement** may replace the current link without rewriting prior actions.

When one person has duplicate Docket Accounts or a disputed identity link, Docket never merges them automatically. The affected Account holder, an authorized School Manager or Tournament Owner handling that person's access failure, or a Platform Administrator documenting a detected identity conflict may request a **Platform Identity Review**; only Platform Administrators conduct and decide it. The review requires proof that Google recovery was attempted, control of the proposed replacement Google account, and at least two independent links to the existing Docket identity, such as prior verified contact information, institutional confirmation, or verified participation records. A matching name or email alone is insufficient. The review previews every affected role and record, selects one surviving Account, deactivates the duplicate, and preserves the original Account as the actor on every historical event.

One Platform Administrator approves a review for an ordinary Account. Two distinct Platform Administrators approve a review for any Account that currently or historically holds Tournament Ownership, platform authority, Legal and Privacy Operations, or another sensitive authority. Docket notifies the old and new verified addresses and every affected School or Tournament Owner of the outcome without disclosing submitted identity evidence.

Identity evidence is encrypted and available only to assigned reviewers. Docket irreversibly deletes its contents 30 days after final resolution unless a Legal Hold applies. A content-free Platform Identity Review Decision Record remains for seven years when current or historical sensitive authority was affected and for two years after an ordinary Account review.

An affected Account holder may request one **Platform Identity Review Reconsideration** within 14 days by presenting new evidence or identifying a specific process error. Administrators who approved the original outcome cannot decide the reconsideration. Any corrected outcome is a new immutable decision linked to the original; no actor edits or erases the original review.

The reconsideration is final for that review. A correction changes current identity links and authority prospectively without rewriting historical attribution. Evidence of a separate later fraud or compromise may open a new security review but cannot create another reconsideration of the same case.

During a Google outage, an already-valid Docket Session continues until its normal inactivity or absolute expiration. Docket blocks new sign-ins, session renewal, identity-link changes, and every command requiring Google Reauthentication. Docket provides no local-password fallback, support bypass, or impersonation route.

## Access Offers

Every **Access Offer** is bound to one intended verified Google email address and one exact School, tournament, or platform authority scope. Redemption requires a Docket Account authenticated with that verified address. Support cannot retarget an issued offer; if the recipient must use another Google identity, an authorized inviter cancels the offer and issues a new one.

Docket sends every Access Offer by email and also places it in the recipient's separate in-app **Access Inbox** when a matching Docket Account already exists. Docket does not require SMS delivery. Delivery failure raises an operational warning and permits notice resend without changing the offer's scope, state, token, or expiration.

School and tournament Access Offers expire seven days after issuance. Platform-permission offers expire after 24 hours. Resending a notice does not extend or replace the original token; an authorized actor must issue a new offer.

Opening an Access Offer grants nothing. Before acceptance, Docket shows the offering actor, governed School or tournament, offered role and permissions, and expiration. The recipient explicitly accepts the exact offer. Platform permissions, Tournament Ownership, and Tournament Director authority additionally require Google Reauthentication before acceptance.

If the recipient already holds the exact offered authority, acceptance makes no change and records a redundant offer outcome. If acceptance would violate an exclusivity invariant, including the one-School-Manager rule, Docket rejects it rather than replacing the current holder or partially granting the offer.

Before acceptance, the original offering actor or any current actor holding the corresponding authority-management permission may revoke the Access Offer. Docket also invalidates it automatically if its offering actor loses the authority required to issue it. After acceptance, the offer is immutable evidence of the grant; any later removal uses the normal role-revocation or transfer workflow.

Issuing a replacement for the same verified email and exact authority scope revokes every earlier Pending Access Offer and leaves only the newest version redeemable. Resending the notice for the same offer does not replace its token or move its expiration.

Every Access Offer is reviewed and issued individually. Docket prohibits batch issuance for all School Membership, tournament staff, Director, Owner, platform, and Legal and Privacy Operations authority. Access Offers are never used to onboard Judges or Competitors or to advertise a tournament. Judges and Competitors create their own Basic Docket Accounts, and tournament discovery uses the [[tournament-directory-model|Active Tournament Directory]].

An Access Offer sent to the wrong verified email must be revoked and reissued; no actor may edit or retarget it. Docket sends the incorrect recipient a revocation notice that reveals no additional private scope information beyond the original notice.

At acceptance, Docket transactionally revalidates the Access Offer state, bound Google identity, offering actor's authority, offered scope, and all exclusivity invariants. Only the first valid terminal action succeeds. Failed delivery is retried three times over 24 hours and then escalated to the offering actor without changing the offer's expiration.

A permanent email bounce marks the Access Offer delivery Undeliverable, stops automatic retries, and alerts the offering actor. An existing matching Account keeps its Access Inbox offer, but correction of the email still requires revocation and individual reissue.

## Docket Account status

A Platform Administrator may impose an immediate **Docket Account Suspension** only for a documented security, fraud, abuse, or legal risk. Suspension terminates all sessions and blocks authentication without deleting roles, records, or historical attribution. A second Platform Administrator must review it within 24 hours. It expires automatically after seven days unless a reasoned extension receives fresh independent approval.

Docket may reinstate the Account early once the documented risk is resolved and a second Platform Administrator approves reinstatement. Each extension is limited to 30 days and requires a fresh independent review. Suspension never converts automatically into a permanent ban; ongoing authority removal uses the applicable role-revocation, ownership-transfer, or legal process.

Detailed suspension evidence is encrypted and deleted 90 days after the suspension's final reinstatement, expiry, or replacement by another formal process unless a Legal Hold applies. Docket retains only a content-free Suspension Decision Record for two years for an ordinary Account or seven years when current or historical sensitive authority was affected.

The suspended Account holder may submit one challenge within seven days using new evidence or identifying a specific process error. Two Platform Administrators who neither imposed nor previously approved the suspension decide it. The suspension remains effective during review, and any outcome appends a new immutable decision.

The challenge decision is final for that suspension. Separate later evidence of a new compromise, fraud event, or legal issue starts a new security process rather than reopening the completed challenge.

Docket immediately notifies the suspended Account's verified address. When operations are affected, it also notifies the relevant School Managers and Tournament Owners of only the suspension status, affected authority, and expected review time; it does not disclose security evidence or allegations.

If a suspension email fails, Docket retries it three times over 24 hours, retains the notice in the Account's restricted inbox, and escalates the delivery failure to Platform Administrators. Notice failure never delays, lifts, or cancels the suspension.

Suspension never transfers authority. Authorized actors reassign nonexclusive duties through ordinary workflows. An unavailable Tournament Owner uses the existing Emergency Authority Grant or Ownership Recovery process, and future Judge assignments use the ordinary schedule-revision controls. Platform support cannot impersonate the suspended actor.

An Account holder may request **Voluntary Account Deactivation** only after every exclusive duty, including Tournament Ownership and School Manager, has been transferred. A Competitor may submit the request while entered in an active tournament, but Docket postpones the seven-day execution window until the Competitor has no future round assignment and every Disqualification or Correction requiring their response is resolved or past its deadline. Docket does not wait for Tournament Closure or unrelated Judge feedback. It allows cancellation during the seven-day period only after Google Reauthentication. If Google access is lost, a Platform Identity Review opened before execution pauses the deactivation until that review resolves.

At execution, deactivation is irreversible: it terminates all sessions, removes the current Google identity link, and revokes remaining nonexclusive future authority. Docket deletes unnecessary profile data within 30 days unless an independent retention rule or Legal Hold applies. The person's name remains only where it forms part of an independently retained historical or published tournament record; other retained internal attribution uses only the minimum restricted identity required by its retention policy. A returning person creates a new Basic Docket Account and receives no former authority automatically.

Logging out terminates only the selected Docket Session and does not sign the user out of Google. **Log Out Everywhere** terminates every Docket Session. Docket sends security alerts for a new privileged session, replacement of an existing privileged session, Log Out Everywhere, administrative session termination, and Account suspension, but not for routine single-session logout.

## Google profile and Account transparency

Docket imports only the Google Identity Subject, verified email, and display name. It does not import the Google profile image or request or ingest Google birth date, gender, contacts, calendar, Drive content, location history, or other unrelated profile or product data.

An Account holder may choose a **Docket Display Name** different from the Google profile name. Self-service changes are limited to once every 30 days and remain subject to impersonation and abuse controls. A Platform Administrator may approve an earlier safety or identity correction through documented review. Docket keeps the verified Google name restricted to identity and audit purposes, exposes only the current Docket Display Name outside immutable history, and retains the name originally used in a historical tournament publication.

The Account holder may generate a self-service **Account Data Export** containing their profile, current and historical roles, Access Offer history, Docket Sessions, notification settings, Judge qualification summaries, and their own submitted content. It excludes another person's private data, security and identity evidence, Legal Hold details, and internal investigative records. Fresh Google Reauthentication is required. Docket generates the archive asynchronously, permits one completed export per 24 hours, sends a readiness notice without attaching data to email, requires the authenticated holder to download it, and deletes the generated archive after seven days.

The Account-facing **Account Security History** shows only recent sign-ins across devices, Google Reauthentication events, current or historical suspension status events, and completed Account exports. Docket retains those items for two years and does not show privileged-context restoration, ordinary session creation or termination, or Google identity-link changes in that view.

Docket does not impose a universal age restriction on Coach or Judge authentication and does not collect birth date for that purpose. Tournament policies and governing Rulesets determine whether a person may serve as a Coach or Judge. For a minor Competitor, an authorized School actor records the minimized one-school-year Minor Participation Authorization before affiliation or participation; Google authentication is not consent, and Core Entry Data excludes guardian contact information and full birth date. Renewal follows School transfer or material policy change, and revocation uses the governed active-Entry review. The School attests that every applicable School, tournament, home-jurisdiction, and host-jurisdiction requirement is satisfied and applies the strictest declared requirement. When authorized School or tournament actors cannot reconcile them, Docket blocks affiliation or participation without interpreting the law.

See [ADR 0029](../docs/adr/0029-use-google-as-the-sole-authentication-provider.md).

### Legal and Privacy Operations

**Legal and Privacy Operations** is a platform-level permission held only by specifically authorized Platform Administrators; generic Platform Administrator status does not confer it. Tournament Owners, Directors, Tabulation Staff, Coaches, Judges, and Accommodation Operations staff cannot create, inspect, expand, extend, review, or release a Legal Hold.

One Legal and Privacy Operations administrator prepares a proposed Legal Hold, and a second distinct administrator must approve it before it becomes active. The proposal identifies the legal basis, external case reference, exact records and scope, start time, and a review date no more than 90 days after activation or the prior review. The preparing actor cannot approve their own proposal. Hold details and approval records are visible only within Legal and Privacy Operations audit views.

Every scope expansion, extension, and release uses the same two-person preparation and approval rule. A hold remains active when its review date passes so overdue review can never trigger accidental deletion, but Docket marks it overdue and escalates it continuously to Legal and Privacy Operations until reviewed. Review does not silently broaden the hold and creates a new attributed version.

Release immediately resumes every deletion that the hold suspended; any deletion already past due is queued without a new tournament-side approval. An affected School may see only that its deletion is legally paused or has resumed. It cannot see the legal basis, case reference, scope, administrators, or review details. Public and tournament views expose neither the hold nor its status.

The Tournament Record Retention Model in [[retention-model]] never grants access merely because a record remains stored. Existing audience and purpose restrictions continue throughout the retention period. Once an expired restricted record or routine telemetry payload is deleted, no former tournament, School, Judge, support, or audit permission can retrieve it; only a content-free deletion event remains where the applicable audit policy permits.

### Ordinary Platform Support Actions

The first slice exposes no generic administrator console, database editor, arbitrary script runner, or user-impersonation session. A Platform Administrator may invoke only these typed ordinary **Support Actions**:

- inspect redacted service-health, delivery, job, and validation diagnostics;
- resend an already-authorized notification or invitation without changing its recipient, authority, or payload;
- replay an idempotent failed background job or an unchanged verified provider delivery through all original validation and authorization checks;
- rebuild a derived cache or search index from authoritative source records;
- clear a processing lock only after Docket proves that the lock expired and no owner still holds it; or
- regenerate a missing derived projection from its immutable authoritative source without approving or publishing a new domain version.

Every state-changing Support Action requires a support-ticket reference, reason, exact target and scope, validation and impact preview, administrator identity, execution time, outcome, and a notification to the **Affected Owner**: the account holder, School Manager, or Tournament Owner responsible for the targeted scope. A failed action retains its failure outcome and cannot be relabeled successful. Replay and regeneration never waive an original guard or convert rejected source data into accepted domain data.

Ordinary Support Actions cannot impersonate an actor; create or edit authoritative tournament or School data; change a result, Ruleset, configuration, permission, role, ownership, qualification, retention time, or Legal Hold; approve or publish on another actor's behalf; reopen a closed workflow; or alter immutable history. They cannot expose complete Ballots, Accommodation Requests, raw assessment evidence, identity evidence, disqualification evidence, private correction evidence, secrets, or other sensitive records. Access to those records requires the applicable purpose-limited workflow and permission, such as Assessment Integrity Review, Accommodation Operations, Legal and Privacy Operations, Ownership Recovery, or an authorized correction case.

A cache, index, or projection rebuild on a Closed tournament is infrastructure recovery only: it reconstructs the same current authorized projection and cannot select a different version or reopen the tournament. Support Action records follow [[retention-model]] according to whether they are routine telemetry or outcome-relevant Competitive Evidence.

## Competitor Account boundary

In full production operation, every Competitor holds one individual authenticated Docket Account. A Competitor is not owned by a School, and the Account persists independently from the School represented in a particular Entry.

Only a School may initiate a **Competitor School Invitation**. An authorized School Roster Manager sends it to the Competitor's exact verified Google email, and the Competitor must explicitly accept it; the Competitor cannot request or create the relationship. Email-domain matching never creates, recommends, or confirms the relationship. Docket delivers the invitation by email and the private Account inbox and expires it after seven days. The Roster Manager may revoke it before acceptance. An identical pending invitation is a recorded no-op, a notice resend preserves the original identity and expiry, and a wrong-recipient invitation must be revoked and reissued rather than edited.

Acceptance creates the Account's one current **Competitor School Affiliation**. A later accepted invitation from a different School replaces the current affiliation while retaining the same Account. The Competitor-controlled profile, settings, and personal tournament history follow the Account. Historical Entries and public results remain attributed to the School represented at that time. The destination School receives only current information explicitly needed for its affiliation and future Entries; former-School private notes, attestations, accommodations, communications, evidence, and internal records do not transfer.

Authorized Coaches may manage the School affiliation and School-scoped Entry information but cannot edit the Competitor's Google identity, Account security, Docket Display Name, personal settings, Account Data Export, or another School's data. The Competitor controls the Account profile and Docket Display Name. A School actor may report an identity or eligibility concern but cannot directly edit the profile. Either the Competitor or a Roster Manager may end the affiliation. Docket immediately blocks new Entries for that School but delays final removal until no active Entry still represents it, preserving historical Entries and attribution. A destination-School invitation changes affiliation immediately for future activity, but every existing Entry stays attributed to the former School through that tournament. The Competitor cannot represent both Schools in one tournament, and Docket notifies the Competitor and authorized Managers of both Schools.

Record-only Competitors may be uploaded solely as non-production Competitor Test Fixtures. Test deployments accept only a versioned runtime-validated JSON fixture containing a synthetic fixture identifier, display name, School reference, and test-required eligibility fields. A fixture cannot contain a real email, Google Identity Subject, contact detail, or production authority; cannot authenticate or participate in a production tournament; and must be rejected by every production deployment.

Competitor identity recovery uses Google recovery first and then the existing Platform Identity Review. A Coach or School Manager may submit supporting evidence but cannot control the Account, select the replacement Google identity, or access the Account through the review.

Authentication alone does not make a Competitor available to a School roster. The Competitor must first accept that School's invitation and hold the resulting current Competitor School Affiliation. Only a Coach whose active School Membership includes the applicable roster-scheduling authority may place that affiliated Competitor into the School's Tournament Roster or prepare an Entry; School affiliation alone grants neither action. This School-side authority does not grant Tournament Schedule, Pairing, or publication authority.

An Entry Manager places affiliated Competitors on the School's Tournament Roster and prepares, signs off, submits, updates, and withdraws an Entry. The Coach's sign-off is final for the School; Docket does not require a separate Competitor Entry Confirmation. A Competitor may file a private Entry Error Report but cannot independently submit, withdraw, accept, or reject the Entry. The responsible Coach responds within 24 hours or before the applicable registration or pairing deadline, whichever comes first; an unresolved report escalates to the School Manager and then tournament registration staff without mutating the Entry. Competitors receive direct in-app and email notices only for their own Pairing Publications, Disqualifications, and Corrections. Entry acceptance and rejection notices go to the responsible Coach. This direct-notice restriction does not remove the separately accepted authenticated Judge Pool read view.

An overlapping Entry is prohibited unless the Tournament Director published and locked a Cross-Entry Compatible label before registration opened. The invitation also publishes a Cross-Entry Request deadline equal to the earlier of the Entry edit deadline or the first Pairing Publication for either affected event, plus a Director-configured Cross-Entry Hold Limit defaulting to fifteen minutes. The responsible Coach submits one request bound to the Competitor, School, complete event set, and current published Schedule. Registration-authorized staff may prepare it, but the Tournament Director need only verify the cross-entry and acknowledge Docket's schedule-delay warning; approval cannot promise a hold beyond the published limit. Schedule revisions revalidate the approval, and adding an event requires a new request. A Director may revoke approval before either affected round starts when it becomes infeasible, immediately notifying the Coach and Competitor and blocking affected future Pairings until one Entry is removed or a feasible revision is published.

The cross-entry indicator is not public and is not shown to opponents or unrelated Coaches or Competitors. The assigned Judge for each affected round can see that cross-entry is approved and the applicable hold time. The requesting Coach and Competitor retain their own request status, and authorized tournament staff retain the operational record.

At the affected round's current published start, Docket automatically activates and displays the Cross-Entry Hold to the assigned Judge and authorized staff. Only a pre-start published Schedule revision may move that start; Judges and Coaches cannot extend the countdown. Timely arrival clears the hold and records actual start. Expiry immediately enters the ordinary Competitor No-Show workflow with no additional grace period.

An external Practice Invitation is usable only by a Docket Account authenticated through the exact Google email recipient and grants access to one Practice Workspace session. Any Account holder, including a minor, may accept without School approval; this supersedes the earlier own-School Coach approval rule. The invitation expires after seven days or session end. Resend preserves its recipient, token, and expiry; a wrong recipient requires revocation and a new invitation. The creating Entry Manager is the Practice Session Owner and may transfer that role only to another Entry Manager at the same School; this authority never extends to official tournament operations.

Practice Workspaces are private and undiscoverable outside the owner, accepted participants, and explicitly authorized observers. Their least-privilege roles are Organizer, Competitor, Judge, and Observer, with compatible multi-role assignment allowed; Judges cannot edit Competitor material and Observers cannot submit Ballots or feedback. Before start, permission reductions require notice and authority increases require participant acceptance. The Practice Role Lock fixes accepted participants' roles at session start and prohibits all later role changes. The owner may remove an accepted participant immediately with reason and notice, leaving only a seven-day-or-earlier-deletion export of that participant's own material. Irreversible closure locks new practice activity without extending deletion beyond thirty days after creation. A Judge's ordinary identity and public profile remain public, but participation in a particular private session and all practice feedback remain nonpublic.

Practice Safety Reports go to the owner and platform safety staff, plus an available authorized own-School Coach when the subject is a minor. The owner may remove someone immediately but cannot suppress or decide a report. Platform safety staff make the formal decision and may restrict the reported Account from the affected session and new Practice Invitations while investigating. Broader suspension uses the existing suspension workflow. One reconsideration is available within fourteen days to a different reviewer while protections remain active; the second decision is final except for objective system or identity error. Platform safety staff receive complete evidence; parties see only their own submissions and minimized decisions, owners receive operational instructions, and a minor's Coach receives need-to-know safeguards. Authenticated reporter identity is withheld from the reported Account unless safety, fairness, or law requires it. Proven malicious reporting uses a separate investigation and existing Account governance without competitive effects. Minimized reports, decisions, and necessary evidence remain restricted for two years after resolution, accept appended objective corrections, are excluded from Account exports unless legally required, and may be preserved only by an exact existing Legal Hold.

After session start, a Practice Standby may fill only the identical role accepted and locked before start; no new invitation, role conversion, or permission expansion is allowed. Google Practice Meeting access belongs to the Practice Session Owner, accepted Competitors and Judges, and Observers explicitly granted attendance before the Practice Role Lock. The owner authorizes creation through their connected Google Account. Invitations target exact authorized Account emails, and a forwarded link grants no Docket authority. Observers are ordinary attendees only. Docket exposes no public link and imports no attendance, join-time, recording, transcript, or chat data. Session deletion remains available during an unresolved safety review and cannot withdraw or change that separately preserved report.

Missing pre-creation Google authorization sets Meeting Setup Failed and blocks online-session start. Authorization lost after creation sets Google Connection Lost: current authorized participants keep the link, but managed changes stop until reconnection or validated replacement. Ownership transfer cancels and replaces the meeting through the new same-School owner's Google Account. Owner Account unavailability before start requires accepted same-School transfer or cancellation; during a session, participants may finish but no role, invitation, meeting, or schedule change is allowed, followed by transfer or closure. A suspected pre-start leak rotates the meeting; after start, Google admission controls apply without automatic Docket penalties. Google outages permit retry, a validated Google Meet replacement, rescheduling, or cancellation only.

One accepted adult Organizer may be designated before the Practice Role Lock as backup Google co-host. If that person becomes unavailable afterward, the owner may designate another already accepted adult Organizer as emergency backup co-host; this changes only Google meeting-management authority, not the locked Docket role, and requires attribution and participant notice. Accepted Organizers, Competitors, and Judges may present when enabled; an Observer may present only through an explicit pre-lock grant and never receives co-host, recording, transcript, or meeting-management authority. Google calendar events remain private and privacy-minimized, and Docket never exposes participant emails. Docket Practice Invitation acceptance—not Google RSVP or calendar deletion—controls participation. Routine twenty-four-hour and one-hour reminders are mutable, while cancellation, removal, link replacement, and safety notices are mandatory.

The platform-wide Event Ballot Rubric governs every official and Practice Ballot for its event. Rules-governance Platform Administrators collaboratively edit a document-like draft made of ordered open-ended question fields, but only a validated, reviewed, reasoned, audited published version governs a round. Practices pin it at the Practice Role Lock; official tournaments pin it with their pre-pairing Event Ruleset. The owner cannot customize it. Only the owner may edit the separate Practice Feedback Rubric, no other participant may suggest changes, and titled versions support same-School reuse or sanitized cross-School sharing before lock. Submitted Practice Ballots go only to their Judge, assigned Competitors, and owner or authorized Organizer. Docket blocks controlled Judge departure until Ballot submission and treats browser abandonment or connection loss as an outstanding draft pending a server receipt. Panel Judges cannot see one another's Ballots before all submit, after which the session applies the event panel rule and exposes its official Practice Panel Result without granting tournament authority. Only the owner may authorize reasoned pre-closure Ballot reopening or make a versioned Practice Placement Correction limited to competitor placements; the owner cannot change Judge-authored fields, Feedback, attribution, or supply a missing Ballot. Practice Feedback follows the tournament-style lifecycle. Result audiences and exports remain permission-filtered and no practice result enters profile or permanent history.

Only the Competitor's Coach may submit a Judge Conflict for that Competitor or Entry. The Competitor cannot submit one directly. Under an enabled Judge Striking Policy, each individual Competitor or all Competitors in a team Entry initially choose their Entry's Judge Strikes. Each Entry designates one Primary Responsible Coach and may predesignate a Backup Responsible Coach before opening; both require an active School Membership tied to the affected Competitors, and the backup acts only when the primary is unavailable. The acting Coach resolves teammate disagreement and may approve, correct, or voluntarily release strikes. One native Judge Strike Portal window governs the tournament unless a pre-registration policy defines separate event sessions. It opens 36 hours before Tournament Start Time and closes at 3:00 p.m. in the tournament's official named timezone on the calendar day before the tournament, controls across daylight-saving changes, and also displays the user's local equivalent. Docket transactionally records approval and returns an in-app receipt; there is no email delivery to the Tournament Director or external host. Every pre-close change requires fresh approval. Ordinary workflow does not add a Judge after closing and has no supplemental strike path. A late exception requires platform-health evidence, applies only to affected Entries for the verified outage duration capped at 30 minutes, needs Director approval, and ends once an opponent or pairing is known. A missing approval is nonoperative after reminders six and one hours before closing. Strike records use the same restricted seven-year Competitive Evidence period as other Competitor tournament records. A Judge Strike is not a verified conflict.

An unexpected withdrawal or No-Show may add an Emergency Replacement Judge after closing only through reasoned Tournament Director or Judge-and-room-authorized Tabulation Staff approval. Docket revalidates verified conflicts, does not reopen strikes, prefers eligible Judges who were previously available, and requires assignment-specific Tabulation Staff sign-off. Admission immediately sends in-app and email notice to the assigned Judge, affected Competitors and Coaches, Tournament Directors, and relevant Tabulation Staff using a generic operational-replacement reason that withholds private withdrawal or No-Show evidence. The Judge must acknowledge within ten minutes after notice and always before the round starts; if fewer than ten minutes remain, the remaining pre-start time is the deadline. Until acknowledgment the assignment is unconfirmed and staff may select another candidate. One timestamped delivery cycle dispatches both the in-app and email notices and fails only when neither channel confirms delivery; confirmed delivery without acknowledgment is not a delivery failure. Staff may use the Judge's restricted operational contact method and record the attempt, but cannot acknowledge for the Judge. After three failed delivery cycles Docket ends the assignment as Delivery Failed and staff must find another Judge. Delivery Failed creates neither a No-Show nor a Judge Reliability Record. A late acknowledgment cannot revive the canceled assignment; Docket tells the Judge it was replaced, and selecting that Judge later requires a new governed assignment. The tentative Judge's public display name, Published Judging Profile, and Unconfirmed status are visible only to affected Competitors and Coaches and authorized staff; no public Pairing Publication changes before acknowledgment and final sign-off. There is no fixed numerical use cap, but every additional emergency assignment requires a new reason and sign-off. The second use in one event or third use tournament-wide, whichever occurs first, and every later use raises a Director staffing warning. The workflow cannot bypass the ordinary Judge Pool or Judge Strike Policy. Before the round starts, an affected Coach may report a verified conflict, qualification error, or assignment-data error: a verified conflict invalidates the assignment, while a qualification or data allegation receives urgent validation and blocks start only when it could make the assignment invalid. The Coach receives no new discretionary strike or veto. An acknowledged Emergency Replacement Judge who fails to appear enters the ordinary Judge No-Show grace, contact, classification, and reliability workflow; replacing that Judge requires a new emergency reason, validation, approval, and sign-off and never occurs automatically.

A Competitor Account suspension blocks Account access but does not withdraw an Entry or create a competitive outcome. The responsible Coach continues managing the Entry, and only the normal eligibility, Disqualification Ruling, schedule, or withdrawal workflow may change participation.

Before a minor Competitor may accept a School affiliation or participate in a tournament, an authorized School actor must record a Minor Participation Authorization containing only actor, time, School, Competitor, applicable policy or legal basis, and expiration. It lasts one school year and requires renewal after School transfer or material policy change. Google authentication alone is not consent, guardian documents are not collected by default, and Core Entry Data contains neither guardian contact details nor a full birth date. Revocation blocks future Entries immediately without erasing history; an active Entry enters a noticed Director-reviewed eligibility or withdrawal workflow and is never silently withdrawn or disqualified.

See [ADR 0030](../docs/adr/0030-use-individual-competitor-accounts.md), which supersedes ADRs 0002 and 0003.

## Coach authorization

A Coach acts for a School only through an explicit **School Membership**.

After discovering a tournament in the [[tournament-directory-model|Active Tournament Directory]], a Coach uses Register or Manage Entries from its Tournament Invitation Page. The action requires the applicable School Membership and enters the tournament's ordinary Registration Policy workflow; the tournament does not invite the Coach to create an Account.

When the Coach lacks an existing School Membership, Docket sends the request to that School's current School Manager for explicit sign-off. The request expires after seven days or at the applicable tournament registration deadline, whichever occurs first, and Docket sends a reminder before expiration. Only that Manager's approval may create the requested non-Manager Membership and role assignments. A denial records a reason and permits one new request after a material correction. A Platform Administrator may intervene only when no functioning current School Manager exists and never overrides an active Manager's decision. When no verified School or current Manager exists, the Coach enters the existing Provisional School and first-membership Platform Administrator verification workflow. Tournament registration never bypasses either path.

- A Coach may hold School Memberships for multiple Schools.
- A designated Coach with membership-management authority may invite another Coach to the same School.
- The first School Membership requires Platform Administrator approval supported by institutional evidence.
- A matching institutional email domain may support verification but never grants access by itself.
- Revoking a School Membership prevents future actions immediately while retaining the actor attribution on every historical action.
- School authority is never inferred from a Coach's account-wide identity or participation in a tournament.

### School Membership roles

Each School Membership may hold one or more roles:

- **School Manager** — invites and revokes School Memberships and assigns non-Manager School roles.
- **Roster Manager** — issues Competitor School Invitations and manages the School's accepted roster affiliations.
- **Entry Manager** — places affiliated Competitors on Tournament Rosters and gives the School's final sign-off to register, update, and withdraw Entries.
- **Judge Manager** — manages the School's supplied Judges, availability, and declared conflicts.

One Coach may hold all four roles, so the exclusive School Manager may also act as the Entry Manager who oversees Tournament Roster scheduling. Roster Manager, Entry Manager, and Judge Manager may each be held by multiple Coaches. School Manager is exclusive: every School has exactly one, never zero or more than one. Only the School Manager assigns the non-Manager roles; the Manager role itself requires a controlled transfer or recovery process.

Entry authority operates on the Entry Registration State defined in [[registration-model]]. Registration state is separate from Competitive Eligibility: an Entry Manager does not gain disqualification authority merely because they can create, submit, update, or withdraw their School's Entry.

An Entry Manager may place affiliated Competitors on the Tournament Roster, create and edit Drafts, provide the School's final Entry sign-off, submit complete Entries, and withdraw their School's Draft, Submitted, Waitlisted, or Accepted Entries. Registration-authorized Tabulation Staff and Tournament Directors may return Submitted Entries to Draft for correction and decide Submitted or Waitlisted admission as Waitlisted, Accepted, or Rejected. Tournament-side transitions require an attributed reason and do not authorize edits to the Competitor's Account or identity. Withdrawn and Rejected restoration is outside ordinary registration authority.

Roster and Entry Managers may supply the minimized Core Entry Data defined in [[registration-model]] for their own School. The responsible Coach contact must be an authenticated Coach with an active Membership for that School. Grade or eligibility category, the School eligibility attestation, and the responsible Coach contact are visible only to authorized members of that School and to Registration-authorized tournament personnel with an operational need. Public Viewers receive only an explicitly published Accepted Entry's display name, School, and event; they never receive those restricted fields or internal identifiers and metadata.

Only a Tournament Director may configure, accept, and publish a Registration Data Schema before registration opens. After opening, the Director may only remove a field, stop its collection, or make it optional; no actor may add a field, create a new obligation, tighten validation, broaden access or retention, make an additional field public, or invalidate a previously complete Entry. Entry Managers may answer the accepted Schema for their own School. A field's declared audience is an additional restriction and never expands an actor's underlying School or tournament authority. Medical and accommodation details cannot be collected through this authority and require the separately governed Accommodation Request workflow.

An Entry Manager or Responsible Coach Contact may create and view a complete Accommodation Request only for an Entry belonging to their School. A Tournament Director may view complete requests and may explicitly delegate the Accommodation Operations permission to selected Tabulation Staff. No generic registration, pairing, room, Judge-assignment, publication, or support permission implies that access. Accommodation Operations actors may review the request, communicate with the School, and produce need-to-know implementation instructions within the separately defined request lifecycle.

An operational actor receives only an Accommodation Implementation Instruction relevant to that actor's current assignment. Tabulation Staff, room staff, and Judges do not receive diagnoses, supporting evidence, the School's narrative, or unrelated instructions through this projection. Public Viewers, unrelated Schools, unrelated Judges, and unauthorized staff cannot see either the request or the fact that it exists. Every full-request view, mutation, export, and disclosure is audited; instruction delivery is also attributed.

The Entry Manager or Responsible Coach Contact may edit Drafts, submit requests, supply a requested clarification as a new version, and withdraw any non-Closed request for their own Entry. Accommodation Operations staff may request specified clarification and may approve a Submitted request only when they can grant it exactly as submitted. Only the Tournament Director may issue a Partially Approved or Denied decision; each requires a specific operational reason and an available alternative when one exists. Any revision that reduces a prior approval has the same Director requirement. No actor may silently edit a submitted request or overwrite a decision version.

Only a Tournament Director configures the Accommodation Request-By Time within the Tournament Registration Policy. It defaults to the Entry submission deadline and can only be extended after registration opens. Own-School actors retain ordinary request authority after that time without a Late Registration Exception. Accommodation Operations staff and the Director retain their ordinary review authority; lateness grants no additional denial power. Any approved impact on a published, unstarted schedule or pairing remains subject to the existing Director approval, withdrawal, and republication authority. Operational recipients learn only the changed assignment or implementation instruction, not that a Late Accommodation Request caused it.

Withdrawal revokes active instructions and closes the request after required notification completes. Tournament Closure closes every remaining request and begins the 30-day sensitive-content deletion period. After Tournament Closure, an Entry Manager or Responsible Coach Contact for the Entry's School may request earlier deletion; the request is executed automatically when the Accommodation Request is Closed and no separately authorized Legal Hold applies. Tournament Owners, Directors, Tabulation Staff, and Accommodation Operations staff have no ordinary authority to extend retention or veto deletion. After deletion, previously authorized actors can access only the content-free Accommodation Audit Stub within their existing restricted audit scope.

The Accommodation Audit Stub is never available to Public Viewers, ordinary tournament screens, or School exports, including exports for the Entry's own School. Authorized restricted audit access ends when Docket deletes the stub one year after Tournament Closure unless a Legal Hold applies. Stub deletion does not change any actor's access to independently retained public tournament history.

A genuine student transfer keeps the same Competitor Account and replaces its one current Competitor School Affiliation through a destination-School invitation accepted by the Competitor. The Competitor's profile settings and personal tournament history follow the Account. Historical Entries and public results keep the School represented at the time, and the destination School receives only current affiliation and future-Entry information. Former-School private notes, attestations, accommodations, communications, evidence, and internal records never transfer. Neither School gains authority over the Account or the other's private data. The replacement is immediate for future activity, but an existing Entry stays attributed to its former School through that tournament and prevents dual-School representation there.

An Entry School Attribution Correction remains available for a proven historical error. A Platform Administrator may validate the cross-School identity evidence but cannot alter tournament results; the affected Tournament Director must approve the correction under ordinary or Post-Closure rules, including downstream impact handling and republication.

Only a Tournament Director configures and publishes the Tournament Registration Policy. After a deadline but before first-round Pairing Publication, only a Director may approve an Entry-scoped Late Registration Exception with a reason and impact preview. Registration staff may prepare a request but cannot approve it. Entry Managers retain withdrawal authority at all times; after the School edit deadline, Docket records a Late Withdrawal and applies notices and downstream effects rather than denying the withdrawal.

Before registration opens, a Director may replace and republish the Policy. After opening, only a Director may approve a Registration Policy Revision, and only to extend a deadline or relax a requirement after reviewing the impact preview. No actor may shorten a deadline, impose a stricter requirement, or make a timezone correction produce an earlier instant. Every revision preserves the prior version and requires a public change notice plus affected-School notifications. First-round Pairing Publication ends this authority.

The published Policy's Admission Policy defines capacity, optional per-School caps, and objective named priority groups. Docket calculates the Admission Recommendation and waitlist order. Registration-authorized staff or a Director may confirm the exact calculated transitions but cannot manually reorder the waitlist, skip a higher-ranked eligible Entry, alter a seed outcome, or invent an unpublished priority. The same authority confirms waitlist promotion and the standardized capacity rejections at waitlist closing.

Before first-round Pairing Publication, Registration-authorized staff or a Tournament Director may approve an evidence-backed Entry Restoration to Submitted for the same Entry identity. After publication but before any round starts, only a Director may restore a mistakenly withdrawn, formerly Accepted Entry after reviewing the full impact and completing every required Pairing Withdrawal and republication. No actor may restore an Entry to active registration after any round starts or bypass fresh admission review by restoring Accepted directly.

A voluntary **School Manager Transfer** selects an existing School Member, requires the recipient's acceptance, and replaces the Manager atomically with a permanent audit record. If the Manager loses access, Docket attempts normal account recovery first. Otherwise, an existing School Member may request **School Manager Recovery**, which requires institutional evidence, notification to the current Manager, approval by two distinct Platform Administrators, recipient acceptance, and a permanent audit record.

While recovery is pending, Platform Administrators may perform specific, attributed Support Actions but cannot create a temporary or second School Manager.

See [ADR 0008](../docs/adr/0008-single-school-manager-with-composable-roles.md).

## Platform governance authority

Only a Platform Administrator may complete School Verification or a School Merge. Coaches and Tournament Directors may submit requests and evidence, but they cannot approve these platform-wide identity changes. Each administrative action remains attributed to the Platform Administrator who performed it.

Platform Administrators cannot impersonate another user in the first slice. Support uses read-only diagnostics and explicitly allowed **Support Actions** instead. Every state-changing Support Action executes under the Platform Administrator's own identity and requires a reason and audit record. Docket must not create a session, token, or request context that identifies the administrator as another actor.

Only an authenticated Judge may initiate that Judge's provider-hosted assessment. A conflicting or provider-originated assessment identity may be resolved only by a Platform Administrator through an attributed, evidence-backed Assessment Identity Review. The review may change the current provider-account link through a new version but never silently moves or rewrites historical attempts, results, qualifications, or assignments. A matching name or email address is insufficient evidence by itself.

See [ADR 0005](../docs/adr/0005-prohibit-administrator-impersonation.md).

## Tournament operations delegation

Tabulation Staff act through a revocable **Tournament Staff Assignment** containing one or more explicit **Tournament Permission Bundles** for a single tournament. The initial bundles cover:

- Registration operations
- Pairing operations
- Judge and room operations
- Ballot and correction operations
- Publication operations

Tournament Director appointment, Ruleset Migration, Emergency Amendments, tournament ownership, and tournament closure remain Director-only governance actions. Tabulation Staff cannot grant themselves permissions or delegate authority they do not control. Revocation prevents future actions immediately while retaining historical attribution.

Administrative Outcomes such as byes and forfeits require a Tournament Director or Tabulation Staff member with the Ballot and correction permission bundle. They are attributed staff operations and never appear as Judge-submitted Ballots.

Only a Tournament Director may issue the evidence-backed Administrative Ruling that resolves a missing Competitive Result when a single Judge has not submitted or a panel has not reached an Irreversible Majority. Judge-and-room operations staff may investigate and contact the Judge after escalation but cannot infer, enter, or approve a winner. A late or missing Ballot never authorizes an automatic forfeit or another Entry-level penalty.

The same Ballot and correction authority is required to initiate Ballot Reopening. Reopening requires a reason, impact preview, and confirmation; any later Downstream Conflicts require separate explicit resolution rather than inheriting permission from the reopening action.

An identical Ballot retry returns the assigned Judge's original receipt and grants no new access or authority. If a Judge submits different competitive data after the result locks, Docket rejects the Conflicting Ballot Attempt, retains it in restricted audit history, and notifies that Judge and the Directors or Ballot-and-correction staff authorized to investigate. Only the existing Ballot Reopening workflow may authorize a corrected version; the attempted payload never becomes current by itself.

The original Judge supplies a Result Correction whenever available. If unavailable, a Director or Ballot-and-correction staff member may enter a Staff Result Correction only with documented Judge confirmation or an Administrative Ruling. The correction remains attributed to the staff actor and is never presented as a Judge submission.

Only a Tournament Director may create a Completed Round Decision Correction, and only for a verified input error or disqualification. The Director must review the impact, supply evidence and Ruleset authority, and explicitly confirm a new Director-attributed decision version. Tabulation Staff may prepare supporting information but cannot approve or enter the completed-round change. Pairing data remains immutable.

Only a Tournament Director may issue a Disqualification Ruling and select its Ruleset-supported round-only, prospective-removal, or full-tournament scope. The Director must confirm an exact impact preview. Retroactive forfeits require separate Completed Round Decision Corrections for every affected round; neither staff nor the Director may perform an unversioned bulk rewrite.

Only a Tournament Director may upload the tournament event's `.docket-standings.json` Standings Rules Configuration. Each upload creates an attributed version with its source filename, content fingerprint, event, and time; uploading a replacement never overwrites an earlier version. Docket, not the uploader, performs schema and semantic validation and generates the calculation preview.

Only a Tournament Director may accept one exact validated configuration after reviewing its calculation preview. Docket publishes its human-readable summary without internal metadata on the Tournament Invitation Page before first-round Pairing Publication; that first publication locks the internally referenced version. No ordinary staff permission allows a post-lock replacement.

The Director-selected Standings Publication Policy is part of that exact accepted configuration. The invitation-page summary discloses whether standings release after every preliminary round, at named checkpoints, or only after all preliminaries. First-round Pairing Publication locks the schedule, and no Director or staff permission allows an ordinary outcome-aware timing change.

Docket calculates and validates an eligible standings snapshot automatically but never publishes it automatically. A Tournament Director or Pairing-authorized Tabulation Staff member may approve one exact validated fingerprint. The Director or Publication-authorized staff may publish only that approved version. One actor may perform both when holding both authorities, while validation, approval, and publication remain separately attributed. Direct rank or metric editing is prohibited.

Public Viewers may read a published Standings Snapshot Summary containing Entry display identity, School, rank, win-loss record, every ranking-affecting aggregate metric, checkpoint, and any public Correction Notice. Active School Members may additionally view the per-round calculation inputs, drops, exclusions, Administrative Outcome treatment, and intermediate metrics for only their own Entries. Source versions, fingerprints, publishing attribution, exact timestamps, Judge-linked scores, detailed Ballots, feedback, private eligibility and correction evidence, and Judge assessment data remain restricted by their source permissions. The backend enforces separate audience projections before returning data.

Only a Tournament Director may approve an Emergency Standings Rules Correction, and only for evidence that Docket differs from the standings policy already published to participants. Tabulation Staff may investigate the defect and prepare evidence and impact analysis but cannot approve the replacement. The Director must review and explicitly confirm the exact validated replacement, calculation preview, full impact preview, notices, and reason. Strategic policy changes and outcome-driven adjustments do not qualify. See [[standings-model]].

Only a Tournament Director may upload and accept the event's `.docket-advancement.json` Advancement Plan. Docket performs runtime validation and generates the Advancement Plan Preview; the Director must review that preview and explicitly accept one exact fingerprint. Docket publishes the human-readable Plan summary before the first round, and first-round Pairing Publication locks it with its referenced standings fingerprint. No ordinary staff permission authorizes upload, acceptance, or post-lock replacement. See [[advancement-model]].

Only a Tournament Director may upload and accept the event's `.docket-awards.json` Award Plan after Docket runtime validation and Award Plan Preview review. First-round Pairing Publication atomically locks it with compatible standings and advancement sources. No ordinary staff permission authorizes upload, acceptance, or post-lock replacement. Every configured award and recipient is public with Final Results, while internal Plan metadata remains restricted. See [[award-model]].

Only a Tournament Director may approve an Emergency Award Plan Correction, and only when evidence shows that the locked Plan differs from award rules published before the tournament. Tabulation Staff may prepare the evidence, replacement, and impact analysis but cannot approve them. The Director must confirm the exact validated replacement, Plan preview, full award impact preview, reason, and notices. Outcome-driven or strategically discretionary changes do not qualify.

Only a Tournament Director may approve an Emergency Advancement Plan Correction, and only for verified divergence from the advancement policy already published to participants. Tabulation Staff may prepare evidence and impact analysis. The Director must confirm the exact validated replacement, Plan preview, full impact preview, reason, conflicts, and notices. Before elimination starts the correction may replace and recalculate the Field; after elimination starts it cannot authorize replacements, reseeding, or bracket rewrites.

Pairing-authorized Tabulation Staff may prepare and review a calculated Advancement Field and submit a recommendation but cannot directly edit its qualifiers or seeds. Only a Tournament Director may approve one exact validated Field fingerprint after all required preliminary results, eligibility decisions, standings inputs, timely No-Show Disputes, and blocking conflicts are resolved. The Director or Publication-authorized staff may publish only that approved version. A source change or recalculation invalidates approval and requires the full approval and publication workflow again. Docket then auto-populates each elimination schedule block from the native Tournament Schedule, current Advancement Field, bracket, and eligible Judge pool as a nonoperative Pending Out-Round Schedule Review. Pairing-authorized Tabulation Staff may confirm, deny, or edit the proposed Competitors and Judges, subject to validation. Before Tournament Schedule Publication, the Director selects one supported tournament-scoped Out-Round Confirmation Permission rather than naming a specific verifier. The selection locks when the first tournament round starts. A later reasoned Director correction is allowed only for a staffing-access change, creates a new version prospectively, and cannot alter completed verifications.

The standard gate uses one Pairing-authorized Tabulation Staff verifier and a second distinct active staff member holding the configured confirmation permission. If an intended confirmer loses permission before acting, Docket discards that unfinished confirmation work; it preserves the first verification only while its exact source version remains unchanged and requires another active permission holder to confirm. If no eligible second confirmer is reasonably available, a Tournament Director may activate a Single-Person Out-Round Verification Exception for one exact round and schedule version. The verifier must hold Pairing authority; a Director may act as verifier only when they also hold it, and the unavailable confirmation permission is not required. Docket prominently warns that verification separation is absent and retains the activation reason, verifier-availability check, round, source versions, warning acknowledgment, actor, and time as restricted audit data. The exception is not participant-facing unless it later causes an ordinary public correction. The checklist covers the prior result or Administrative Outcome, Ballot or panel majority, disputes, corrections, disqualifications, Entry identity and School, seed, bracket position, sides, opponent, Judge qualification and conflicts, room, time, Advancement Field, and bracket lineage.

Relevant source changes make the verification stale and require a new round-and-version-specific exception when a second confirmer remains unavailable. Before start, the gate performs a brief no-change confirmation. A non-overridden gate may repeat the Single-Person Out-Round Verification Exception only through a fresh warning and confirmation when no second verifier is still available. An earlier override remains visibly marked as overridden and must receive a new confirmation from two distinct authorized actors; the single-person exception is unavailable for that step, and a new hard-constraint failure blocks the round. A failed pre-publication gate may use an Out-Round Gate Override only when a Tournament Director invokes it and a second distinct actor holding the Out-Round Confirmation Permission confirms it. Docket shows every failed check and a competitive-impact warning and records the impact preview, specific reason, affected round and Entries, source versions, both actors, confirmation, and internal time. An override may address only operational uncertainty or incomplete verification; it cannot bypass a verified conflict, Competitor eligibility, Judge qualification, disqualification, Ruleset requirement, bracket identity, or another hard integrity constraint. Override data remains restricted to authorized staff and audit history; participants receive the ordinary pairing and any later applicable Correction Notice. A restricted active-risk warning remains until the affected round is Completed and its downstream effects are reviewed; the complete record remains Seven-Year Competitive Evidence. If review proves no competitive effect, staff close the warning as `Reviewed—No Competitive Effect`, preserve the audit record, and publish no Correction Notice unless public information changed. An error found in the pre-start confirmation cannot take that shortcut: the pairing returns to the full first verification, the source error is resolved, and the regenerated pairing repeats approval and republication. If a round starts without the gate, Docket preserves it as operated, creates a high-priority Downstream Conflict for Director resolution, and corrects only unstarted downstream artifacts.

Public Viewers may read the published Advancement Field Summary: advancing Entry display identities and Schools, seeds, byes, applied cut-tie outcome, and any public Correction Notice. Active School Members may additionally view the calculation and scoped eligibility explanation for only their own excluded Entries. Governing versions, fingerprints, publishing attribution, exact timestamps, private disqualification or eligibility evidence, detailed Ballots, Ballot Feedback, unpublished drafts, and Judge assessment data remain unavailable publicly and to unrelated Schools. The backend enforces separate public, School-scoped, and staff projections before data reaches the UI.

Only a Tournament Director may approve one exact validated Final Results Draft after every placement- and award-affecting decision, correction, disqualification, conflict, and calculation is resolved. The Director or Publication-authorized staff may publish only that approved fingerprint. No actor may edit a calculated placement or award directly; source correction and draft regeneration are required. Feedback and a panel Ballot that cannot alter the locked Panel Decision do not independently grant authority to delay, alter, or fabricate a result. See [[final-results-model]].

That Final Results Approval and Publication authority includes the exact automatically calculated Award Results Draft referenced by the Final Results Draft. Docket grants no independent award-approval or award-publication permission. An award-source change requires regeneration and invalidates the earlier Final Results Approval.

Public Viewers may read the event champion, finalist, other configured public placements, complete elimination bracket and decisions, final standings, every configured award and recipient, represented Schools, and applicable human-readable Correction Notices in the Final Results Summary. They cannot access detailed Ballots, feedback, Judge-linked scores, private eligibility or disqualification evidence, Judge assessment data, governing configuration fingerprints, versions, publishing attribution, exact timestamps, correction evidence, internal notes, or audit details through any public Docket UI. Authorized staff retain those records through internal review, history, and audit access.

Tabulation Staff may investigate a Downstream Conflict and prepare an impact analysis and resolution plan. Only a Tournament Director may approve the resolution; permission to reopen or correct a Ballot does not grant that approval authority. See [[ballot-model]] for the state-dependent resolution rules.

Tabulation Staff with the Pairing operations permission bundle may compare pre-tournament Schedule Candidates and prepare a selection recommendation. Only a Tournament Director may approve Schedule Selection and establish the Tournament Schedule. The approval records the Director and reason; staff preparation never becomes approval implicitly. See [[scheduling-model]].

Docket creates every Tournament Schedule natively; it does not depend on an externally authored operational schedule. Pairing-authorized Tabulation Staff may prepare an edit and its impact preview, but a Tournament Director approves the exact version and the Director or Publication-authorized staff publishes it. During competition, Docket recommends against edits unless a Judge or Competitor No-Show, verified conflict, disqualification, room change, or comparable tournament-critical operation makes a Critical Tournament Schedule Edit necessary. `Other Tournament-Critical Operation` requires a specific explanation and impact preview. A noncritical edit remains technically available to otherwise authorized actors, but Docket displays a stronger warning and requires explicit confirmation of its necessity. Every change remains versioned, reasoned, validated, approved, published, and noticed.

Tournament Directors and Pairing-authorized Tabulation Staff may determine and edit the tournament's draft Pairing Plan round by round. Docket recommends a plan for the Director's adopted Ruleset, but administrators may select another supported combination. The Tournament Director approves the exact Pairing Plan version included in Schedule Selection. After approval, the Director or Publication-authorized staff may publish the aligned Tournament Schedule and plan version on the Tournament Invitation Page. See [[pairing-model]].

Schedule Selection locks the selected pairing method and all method-defining options for every round in that tournament. Before any round starts, only a Tournament Director may perform and approve a Pre-Tournament Emergency Pairing Correction that creates and republishes a new locked version with a reason, validation, impact preview, and explicit confirmation. Once any round starts, no actor, including the Tournament Owner acting in another capacity or a Platform Administrator, may alter the locked policy through ordinary operations, Support Actions, Emergency Amendment, Pairing Withdrawal, or schedule correction.

After Tournament Schedule Publication, Pairing-authorized staff may prepare a Pairing Plan Revision and impact preview, but a Tournament Director must approve and republish the exact plan version. If the affected round already has a Pairing Publication but has not started, only a Tournament Director may approve Pairing Withdrawal before a corrected Pairing Publication. No actor may use a plan revision to rewrite a started or completed round.

Pairing-authorized Tabulation Staff may prepare a Round Pairing Correction only for an evidenced Qualifying Pairing Error affecting an unstarted round and only under the current locked policy. Qualifying errors are limited to Entry status or duplication, corrected inputs, failure to apply locked policy, prohibited assignments, Judge eligibility, room or schedule conflicts, and system calculations. Strategic or matchup preferences do not qualify. The correction has no effect until a Tournament Director signs off. A published round must be withdrawn and republished; a started or completed round cannot be corrected through this workflow.

For an ordinary round, a Tournament Director or Pairing-authorized Tabulation Staff member may approve one exact Docket-validated pairing version. The Director or Publication-authorized staff may then publish only that version. The same actor may approve and publish when holding both authorities, but Docket records the validation, approval, and publication separately. Editing an approved version revokes its ready-to-publish status and requires new validation and approval.

For a Schedule Disruption, Pairing-authorized Tabulation Staff may compare Schedule Revision Proposals and prepare a recommendation. Only a Tournament Director may approve one exact proposal version. After that approval, either the Director or Publication-authorized Tabulation Staff may publish the approved version; publication cannot alter it and no revision is applied automatically.

A Tournament Director or Tabulation Staff member with the Judge and room operations permission bundle may issue or revoke a Tournament Tier Grant for the current tournament at their discretion. The grant may bypass a nonpassing assessment tier or failed Critical Competency Item and may confer any higher local tier. For debate, it cannot bypass completion of either the Full Judge Qualification Assessment or an authorized Quick Judge Assessment; for speech, assessment completion is optional. The action records the actor, discretionary reason, source result when present, granted tier, event, time, and expiration. It never changes the Judge's platform assessment score or Event Qualification tier.

The same Director or Judge-and-room-authorized staff may authorize a Quick Judge Assessment when tournament staffing requires moving a Judge into a debate event without a current Event Qualification. Its Temporary Debate Qualification is valid only on the authorized tournament days.

A Judge scheduled for another debate tournament must complete the event's Full Judge Qualification Assessment at least two days before that tournament or complete a newly authorized Quick Judge Assessment for it. Staff cannot reuse a prior tournament's Temporary Debate Qualification.

For Judge qualification operations, Directors and Judge-and-room-authorized Tabulation Staff may view assessment-completion status, qualification source and validity, assessed and Effective Tournament Tiers, Tournament Tier Grant history, and the Judge's Published Judging Profile. They cannot view raw scores or question-by-question responses. A Platform Administrator may access detailed assessment evidence only within an attributed, purpose-limited, audited Assessment Integrity Review; the review cannot change a result, and ordinary Support Actions do not confer that access.

Only a Tournament Director may configure the tournament's Urgent Change Threshold. An Urgent Schedule Revision requires the approving Director's additional short-lead-time confirmation and a documented Judge Continuity Plan before publication; operational urgency does not transfer approval authority to staff.

See [ADR 0006](../docs/adr/0006-delegate-tabulation-permissions.md).

## Tournament ownership

Every tournament has exactly one **Tournament Owner** and may have multiple additional Tournament Directors.

- The Owner is always also a Tournament Director.
- Only the Owner may appoint or remove other Directors, transfer ownership, or close the tournament.
- Additional Directors may govern Rulesets, Emergency Amendments, and Tournament Staff Assignments.
- Ownership transfer requires explicit acceptance by the recipient and a permanent audit record.
- A tournament may never have zero Owners or multiple simultaneous Owners.

Only the Owner may perform Tournament Closure. Final Results Publication by an otherwise authorized Director or staff member marks Competitive Completion but does not grant closure authority or close the tournament automatically. Closure requires an explicit Owner action after the Feedback Deadline and a Closure Readiness Review. Unpublished Final Results, an open round, an unexpired Feedback Deadline, a timely filed unresolved No-Show Dispute, or a result-affecting unresolved correction or conflict cannot be waived. The Owner may resolve outcome-irrelevant warnings only by recording completion or an acknowledgment and reason. Authorized correction workflows remain available after closure. See [[tournament-lifecycle-model]].

After closure, public tournament pages remain readable and authenticated actors retain only their existing permission-scoped historical views, audit access, and exports. Ordinary registration, staffing, configuration, schedule, pairing, round, Ballot, feedback, standings, advancement, award, and result mutations are rejected. Only an actor already authorized for the relevant correction, Feedback Reopening, Ownership Recovery, or legally required privacy workflow may invoke that explicit Post-Closure Exception. The exception grants no unrelated authority and never revives an expired or revoked staff permission.

A Tournament Director may initiate and approve an objective correction to a rejected No-Show Dispute through a scoped Post-Closure Correction Case. A Platform Administrator may validate Docket system evidence but cannot decide the competitive correction or change its result. Qualifying evidence is limited to objective delivery logs, check-in records, immutable timestamps, published time or room errors, or a proven data-entry defect; a new explanation or merits argument cannot reopen the dispute.

An authorized correction opens a Post-Closure Correction Case but leaves the tournament Closed. The actor may perform only commands authorized by both the original correction permission and the case scope. Public Viewers continue seeing the last published version until the corrected version completes its existing approval and republication workflow. Case resolution never grants closure authority or requires the Owner to close the tournament again.

If the Owner loses account access, Docket attempts normal account recovery first. If the Owner cannot regain or exercise authority, an existing Director may request **Ownership Recovery**. Recovery requires verified evidence, notification to the current Owner, approval by two distinct Platform Administrators, recipient acceptance, and a permanent audit record.

During an active tournament, a Platform Administrator may issue a narrowly scoped, automatically expiring **Emergency Authority Grant** to an existing Director instead of rushing a permanent transfer. The grant does not change ownership, and all actions remain attributed to the Director who performs them.

See [ADR 0007](../docs/adr/0007-single-owner-multiple-directors.md).

## Judge access lifecycle

An authenticated Judge may use Judge This Tournament from a Tournament Invitation Page to submit availability and event preferences to that tournament's Judge pool. This is not an Access Offer, acceptance, assignment, or qualification grant.

The Tournament Director or Judge-and-room-authorized Tabulation Staff accepts Judge Pool Participation only after Docket validates the Judge's qualification status, event preferences, availability, and conflicts. Interest submission alone does not make the Judge usable for assignment.

Docket sends the Judge both an in-app and email acceptance or decline notice. A decline carries an operational reason category without changing the Judge's qualification score or tier. Docket has no automatic Judge Pool capacity limit or waitlist in the first release.

Coaches, the Tournament Director, authenticated Competitors, and accepted Judges may view the accepted Judge Pool. Judge-and-room-authorized Tabulation Staff retain operational access. The participant view always shows the current pool with a subject-to-change notice, adding or removing a Judge immediately when acceptance changes. It shows only each accepted Judge's Docket Display Name, Independent or School-Supplied affiliation, and accepted event categories. A pending or declined Judge sees only their own participation status. The pool excludes qualification scores and tiers, assessment status and data, availability, conflicts, contact information, pending or declined requests, private Judging Profiles, withdrawal records, and change actors, reasons, and timestamps. Authorized staff retain the restricted change history. See [[tournament-directory-model]].

Accepted Judge Pool Participation is classified as **Independent** or **School-Supplied**. A School Judge Manager may nominate a School-Supplied Judge, but the Judge must confirm the relationship. A School-Supplied Judge counts toward the School obligation only after confirmation and tournament acceptance. The Judge or School Judge Manager may request a source correction before the Judge Withdrawal Lock Time; after it, only the Tournament Director or Judge-and-room-authorized Tabulation Staff may approve the correction with a recorded reason. The source supports obligation and scheduling decisions but never changes qualification.

When withdrawal or source correction removes a School-Supplied Judge from that School's count, Docket immediately marks the judging obligation deficient and notifies the School Manager, Judge Manager, Entry Manager, and authorized tournament staff. The School may supply a replacement until the published replacement deadline. After it, Docket applies only a remedy already disclosed in the locked Registration Policy and never invents an ad hoc penalty.

Before the **Judge Withdrawal Lock Time**, an accepted Judge may withdraw from the pool and Docket removes an unassigned Judge immediately. The lock is 96 hours before the explicit Tournament Start Time published in the tournament's official timezone. That time must exist before Judge participation opens; afterward the Tournament Director may move it and the lock later but never earlier. From the lock onward, ordinary withdrawal is prohibited, but the Judge may request a **Special Circumstance Withdrawal** with a category and short explanation. Docket never requires medical records, marks the Judge operationally unavailable immediately, and presents authorized staff with the staffing and schedule impact. The Tournament Director or Judge-and-room-authorized Tabulation Staff records an Excused or Unexcused outcome and reason. Docket sends privacy-minimized notices, including to the School Judge Manager for a School-Supplied Judge. If the withdrawal affects a published future assignment, Docket uses the existing schedule-revision and notification workflow.

An Unexcused outcome creates a restricted **Judge Reliability Record** retained for two years. The Judge and future Tournament Directors or Judge-and-room-authorized staff may see the tournament, date, and outcome when reviewing Judge Pool Participation, but not the private explanation. The record never automatically changes qualification, tier, or acceptance and never creates a platform blacklist. An Excused outcome creates no negative cross-tournament disclosure.

The No-Show grace period defaults to ten minutes after the current published round start. A Tournament Director may publish a different per-event period before Schedule Selection but cannot shorten it afterward. Docket requires an in-app notice, email, and documented operational attempt through the responsible Coach for a Competitor or the Judge's restricted tournament contact method for a Judge; it never requires or exposes a minor's phone number. Judge-and-room-authorized Tabulation Staff classify the No-Show. A Competitor may file a private No-Show Dispute directly in Docket within 60 minutes or before the event's next round starts, whichever occurs first. Docket associates and notifies the responsible Coach, who may add a statement but cannot block, withdraw, or suppress it. Only the filing Competitor may withdraw the dispute, and only before the Director decides; the interface requires explicit confirmation, the confirmed withdrawal is final for that classification, and the audited classification remains operative. Check-in and arrival records, message timestamps, staff-contact records, Docket delivery logs, or proof of an incorrect published time or room may establish process or factual error; Docket does not require medical evidence, and an absence explanation affects consequence rather than classification accuracy. The classification and replacement remain operative during review. The Director decides within 30 minutes of filing or before the next event round starts, whichever is first; overdue review escalates without automatic approval or denial. A rejection is final for the tournament, subject only to an objective system or data-entry correction through the governed correction workflow rather than a rehearing on the merits. Docket privately sends the Competitor and responsible Coach an in-app and email notice of the decision, general reason category, Ruleset consequence, deciding authority, and decision time without exposing staff notes or another participant's restricted information. Delivery is tracked but informational: no acknowledgment is required, delivery failure neither reopens the dispute nor delays operations, and the notice remains available in the Competitor's Account.

Docket may then show Tabulation Staff a complete Replacement Candidate Pool with separate rankings but cannot insert a Judge or Competitor into the round. Competitor candidates must be authenticated, fully authorized and eligible for the same School and event, free of assignment conflict, and permitted by the Ruleset. If none exists, the opposing Entry receives a Bye Round. Before replacement sign-off, Tabulation Staff may withdraw the classification when the missing person arrives and restore the original assignment. After replacement or bye approval, reversal requires a Director-approved correction; a Started round can never restore the original person. Tabulation Staff sign-off invokes the applicable governed pairing or scheduling command, which versions and validates the change, preserves the original, sends affected-party notices, and publishes a Correction Notice when public information changed. A successful dispute appends a reasoned reversal, removes the current restricted No-Show flag, corrects every not-yet-started artifact, and retains the original classification only in restricted audit history. Started and Completed rounds remain as operated under the locked Ruleset's correction treatment. When the correction changes public information, the notice identifies the prior and corrected pairing, Bye Round, result, or standings effect using the generic explanation “No-Show classification corrected” without absence details, submitted evidence, staff reasoning, or restricted actor metadata. A Competitor No-Show otherwise remains restricted competitive evidence for that tournament, visible only to the Competitor, their authorized Coach, and authorized tournament staff; it creates no cross-tournament score or public history. An Unexcused Judge No-Show creates the same restricted two-year Judge Reliability Record without automatically changing qualification.

Before Pairing Publication, a Judge may manage only their availability, event preferences, and declared conflicts. After publication, the Judge may access only the assigned round's participants, room, schedule, applicable rules, and Ballot.

When Schedule Revision Publication changes a Judge's assignment, the Judge must provide Assignment Acknowledgment. Until then, the assignment is unconfirmed and escalated to tournament operations. The acknowledgment confirms receipt and availability but grants no approval authority.

A debate Judge must hold either a current Event Qualification or a tournament-day Temporary Debate Qualification for the assigned event. A speech Judge may be assigned without either, though assessment completion is recommended. Every Judge must complete any required Tournament Judge Orientation. See [[judge-qualification-model]].

The Judge may draft and submit the Competitive Result during the assignment. After Ballot Submission, the Competitive Result is read-only and a receipt remains available; changing it requires an authorized Ballot Reopening. Ballot Feedback may be added or revised afterward without staff reopening until the tournament's Feedback Deadline so the tournament can keep moving while useful feedback is completed later. The pre-tournament configuration defaults to 72 hours after the final scheduled round. After locking, only a Tournament Director may extend the deadline; no actor may shorten it. After the deadline, further Judge editing requires an audited Feedback Reopening. Judges cannot access other Judges' Ballots or unpublished standings.

On a panel, each Judge accesses and submits only their own Ballot. Docket may lock the Panel Decision when an Irreversible Majority is reached, but remaining Judges must still submit and are tracked as outstanding. Preliminary Ballots collect Ruleset-valid Speaker Points; Elimination Round Ballots never display or accept a Speaker Point field.

Docket reminds a Judge at the scheduled round end, marks and notifies the Ballot as Late at its Ballot Due Time, and escalates it to Judge-and-room operations staff and Tournament Directors ten minutes later. A late Ballot whose panel already has an Irreversible Majority remains due without blocking that Panel Decision; otherwise, result-dependent standings, advancement, and pairing remain blocked pending the Judge's result or a Director's Administrative Ruling.

Escalation places a tournament-local Judge Assignment Hold on the Judge, preventing new assignments at that tournament. The missing submission releases the hold automatically, or a Tournament Director may clear it with a recorded reason. A Director may remove the Judge from future assignments, but any change to a published assignment must follow the applicable pairing or schedule-revision approval and notice workflow. The hold grants staff no authority to change the Judge's qualification data or affect another tournament.

Feedback Drafts are visible only to their Judge. Feedback Publication makes that version visible to active School Members for both Entries. Later edits remain private until republished, and Ballot Feedback is not available to Public Viewers.

A Judge may view all of their own assessment data while Docket retains it and controls explicit, versioned publication of their Judging Profile. After Pairing Publication, active School Members for both assigned Entries may view only the assigned Judge's Published Judging Profile. They cannot view assessment status, responses, scores, tiers, or grants. Unrelated Schools, other Judges, and Public Viewers receive no qualification or profile access. See [[judge-qualification-model]].

Retention does not expand those audiences. Full answers, detailed scores, Critical Competency detail, provider payloads, and integrity evidence become inaccessible to every actor after their two-year deletion. The Judge may still view the minimal Qualification History Summary for seven years after expiration or supersession; tournament personnel receive historical qualification or profile data only through an already-authorized tournament evidence or audit projection. Deleting or replacing the current Judging Profile does not remove an exact tournament-scoped version still retained as restricted Competitive Evidence for a tournament that actually disclosed it.

See [[ballot-model]] for the result and feedback state model.
